# Marine engine service cost in Australia

A service quote is only meaningful once you know what is in it. Two quotes for
"annual service" can cover quite different work, and the cheaper one is often
cheaper because it leaves out the items that actually protect the engine.

## What should be in a basic service

At minimum:

- Engine oil and filter
- Fuel filters, primary and secondary
- Air filter or breather element
- Belt inspection and tension
- Coolant level and condition check
- Visual inspection of hoses, clamps and mounts
- Battery and terminal check
- Raw water strainer clean
- Run-up and basic checks

## What is usually extra, and often matters more

These are the items that separate a real marine service from an oil change:

**Impeller replacement.** A wear item with a defined interval. Ask whether it is
included or quoted separately.

**Anode inspection and replacement — including the heat exchanger pencil
anode.** Frequently skipped, and the most consequential omission on this list.

**Heat exchanger removal and cleaning.** Periodic, not annual, but it is the
item behind a great many overheats.

**Mixing elbow inspection.** Corrodes; a known failure point.

**Gearbox oil change.** Separate schedule, separate oil.

**Coolant change.** The inhibitors deplete.

**Valve clearance adjustment.** On engines that require it, at interval.

**Injector testing.** Long interval, and the reason for a lot of smoke and
starting complaints.

## What drives the price

**Cylinder count and engine size.** Oil volume, filter count and labour all
scale.

**Single or twin.** Two engines is roughly twice the consumables and close to
twice the labour.

**Access.** An engine you can walk around is quicker than one reached through a
hatch on your knees.

**Mobile or workshop.** Mobile service saves you moving the boat; call-out and
travel are usually part of the rate.

**Location.** Rates and travel differ between capital city marinas, regional
ports and remote areas.

**Parts choice.** Genuine, aftermarket or a mix. All legitimate; you should
know which.

**Condition on arrival.** Seized drain plugs, corroded fasteners and a bilge
full of oil add time.

## How to get a comparable quote

Ask for the service to be quoted as a **line-item list** rather than a single
figure, with parts and labour separated, and with the periodic items — impeller,
anodes, heat exchanger, gearbox oil, coolant — listed as either included or
excluded.

Then ask what they recommend based on your hours and last service, and get that
priced too. A quote that is cheap because it omits the impeller is not cheap.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often does a marine diesel need servicing?**
At the manufacturer's hour interval or annually, whichever comes first. See our
service schedule guide.

**Why is a marine service more expensive than a car service?**
Access, marine-specification parts, the raw water and exhaust systems that a car
does not have, and often a mobile call-out.

**Is the impeller included in a standard service?**
Often not. Ask explicitly — it is a wear item and the most common cause of
overheating.

**Can I service my own marine engine?**
Oil, filters, impeller and strainer are within reach of a competent owner. Valve
clearances, injector work, alignment and cooling system overhaul are not
beginner jobs.

**What is the most commonly skipped service item?**
The pencil anode inside the heat exchanger. Many engines have never had one
replaced.
