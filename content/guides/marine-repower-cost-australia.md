# What does a marine repower cost in Australia

The engine is often less than half of it. That single fact explains most of the
shock owners get when the first quote arrives.

## The five parts of a repower quote

**1. The engine.** A price you can look up, and the only part most owners
research.

**2. Removal of the old engine.** Disconnection, extraction, and disposal or
trade of the core. Where the boat needs a hatch cut, a bulkhead opened, deck
fittings removed or a crane and haul-out, this line grows quickly.

**3. Installation.** New engine beds or modification of existing ones,
alignment, mounts, coupling, and fitting.

**4. Ancillaries and integration.** This is the line people forget:

- Wiring loom and instrument panel — modern engines rarely use old gauges
- Control cables or electronic controls
- Exhaust system, often different in size and outlet position
- Fuel lines, filters and sometimes tank modifications
- Cooling plumbing, seacock and strainer
- Gearbox — new, or the old one adapted and checked
- Propeller and possibly shaft, to match new power and gearbox ratio
- Engine bay soundproofing and ventilation

**5. Commissioning.** Sea trial, propeller matching, adjustment, and handover.
This is not optional; a repower that has not been sea-trialled under load is not
finished.

## What makes one boat more expensive than another

**Access.** The single biggest variable. A flybridge with a removable hatch
above the engine is a different job from a sailing yacht where the engine comes
out through the saloon.

**Whether the new engine is a drop-in.** Some manufacturers offer repower
packages designed to fit an older model's footprint. Where one exists for your
boat, it removes a great deal of bed and alignment work.

**Physical size difference.** A larger or taller engine can require bed
modification, exhaust rerouting, and sometimes changes to the engine box.

**Gearbox compatibility.** A different reduction ratio changes the propeller
requirement. Propeller work is a real line item.

**Twin installations.** Two engines is more than twice the work if the space
between them is tight.

**Location.** Capital city marinas, regional ports and remote locations differ
in labour rate, haul-out cost and freight.

**Electronic versus mechanical.** Electronic engines bring diagnostics and
efficiency and bring a harness, a panel and sometimes electronic controls.

## Questions that make quotes comparable

- Is the propeller included? What about the shaft?
- Is the exhaust system included, end to end?
- New instrument panel and loom, or reuse?
- New gearbox, or existing gearbox reused and on what basis?
- Haul-out, crane and yard fees — included or extra?
- Who pays if the beds need more work than assumed?
- What is the sea trial and propeller matching allowance?
- What warranty applies to installation labour, separate from the engine?
- Delivery lead time for the engine

## Is it worth it

A repower usually does not return its full cost at resale, but it is the more
visible and verifiable improvement to a buyer than an undocumented rebuild, and
it resets the maintenance clock on much more than the engine. Keep every invoice
and the commissioning report with the boat's papers.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is repower so much more than the engine price?**
Because removal, beds, exhaust, wiring, controls, propeller and commissioning
are separate work, and on many boats they exceed the engine itself.

**Can I keep my old gearbox?**
Sometimes, if it is sound and compatible. It should be inspected while
accessible rather than assumed.

**Do I need a new propeller?**
If power or gearbox ratio changes, almost certainly. Propeller matching is part
of commissioning.

**Does a repower add resale value?**
It typically does not return its full cost, but it is documented and verifiable
in a way a rebuild claim is not.

**How long does a repower take?**
Engine lead time usually dominates. Ask whether the engine is in the country
before signing.
