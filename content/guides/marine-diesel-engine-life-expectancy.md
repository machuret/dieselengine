# How long should a marine diesel last

The honest answer is that hours are a poor predictor on their own, and the boats
that prove it are everywhere in Australian marinas: commercial engines with
enormous hours still working, and lightly used pleasure engines rebuilt at a
fraction of that.

What determines engine life is load, maintenance, cooling, fuel and how the
engine spent the time it was not running.

## Why hours mislead

An hour meter counts running time. It does not know:

- whether those hours were at a sensible load or at idle
- whether the raw water side was ever serviced
- whether the oil was changed by the calendar as well as by hours
- whether the engine sat for years between them
- whether it ran hot, once, badly

Two engines with identical hour readings can be in completely different
condition. This is why a pre-purchase inspection looks at oil analysis,
compression, cooling system condition and service records rather than the meter.

## What actually shortens marine engine life

**Chronic light loading.** Extended idling and long periods at low load glaze
bores, cause carbon build-up and let unburnt fuel wash the cylinder walls. It is
the classic pleasure-boat pattern.

**Raw water side neglect.** Heat exchangers silt up, coolers corrode, impellers
shed vanes. The engine does not fail immediately; it runs a little hotter for a
long time, and that shows up as head gasket and injector problems years later.

**One overheat.** A single serious overheat can distort a head or damage a
turbo. Engines rarely recover their previous condition afterwards.

**Fuel contamination.** Water and microbial growth in the tank cause injector and
pump wear that is expensive and often blamed on age.

**Salt water in the wrong place.** Anywhere seawater reaches the fresh water
circuit or the oil, damage accelerates fast.

**Long layup without preparation.** See our guide on low hours.

## What extends it

- Running at a genuine working load regularly
- Servicing the raw water circuit as seriously as the oil
- Changing oil on the calendar, not only the meter
- Keeping the fuel clean and the tank full over layup
- Fixing small leaks and small overheats immediately
- Keeping records, which also protects resale value

## When to stop rebuilding

There comes a point where a rebuild stops making sense — usually when the block
or head is at the end of its overbore range, when parts availability has become
a problem, or when the ancillaries and installation are as tired as the engine.

That is the repower conversation, and it is as much about the age of the mounts,
wiring, controls, exhaust and instruments as about the engine itself.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many hours is high for a marine diesel?**
It depends on the engine type and the life it has had. A lightly built
high-speed pleasure engine and a slow-turning commercial engine are not
comparable on hours at all.

**Are low hours good when buying?**
Not by themselves. Low hours accumulated over a long time, with long idle
periods, can mean more internal deterioration than steady working use.

**What should I check instead of hours?**
Service records, oil analysis, compression or leak-down, cooling system
condition, exhaust colour under load, and evidence of past overheats or water
ingress.

**Does running an engine hard shorten its life?**
Running it at its designed load does not. Running it beyond its rating, or hard
when cold, does.

**Is it worth rebuilding an old marine diesel?**
Sometimes — parts availability, block condition, and the state of the rest of the
installation decide it. Compare against a repower before committing.
