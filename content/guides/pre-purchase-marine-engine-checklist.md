# Pre-purchase marine engine inspection checklist

A general boat survey covers the hull, the systems and the safety gear. It does
not usually include a detailed engine assessment. On a boat where the engine is a
large share of the value, that is the gap where the money is lost.

## Before you look at the engine

**Service records.** Continuous, dated, with invoices, is the strongest signal
available. Gaps are questions.

**Hours, and hours per year.** Both matter. Very low annual hours over many
years is a specific risk — see our guide on low hours.

**How it has been used.** Coastal cruising, marina-hopping, charter, commercial,
or sitting. Each leaves a different signature.

**Where it has been kept.** Warm tropical water, permanent shore power, or a
trailer are all different environments for corrosion.

## Cold start

Insist on starting the engine cold. A seller who has warmed it before you arrive
has removed the most informative single event of the inspection.

Watch for:

- Time and effort to start
- Smoke at start-up, its colour and how long it persists
- Anything untoward in the sound as it settles
- Whether water flows from the exhaust promptly

## Static inspection

**Oil.** Level, colour, and whether it smells of fuel. Milky means water.

**Coolant.** Level, colour, and whether there is oil film on it. Check the
header tank cap and its seat.

**Gearbox oil.** Level, condition, milky or metallic.

**Raw water side.** Strainer condition, impeller pump for signs of past leaks,
heat exchanger end caps, the state of the mixing elbow. Ask when the heat
exchanger was last cleaned and when the pencil anode was last replaced. "I don't
know" is a common and expensive answer.

**Exhaust.** Corrosion, soot streaks indicating a leak, and the condition of
hoses and clamps.

**Belts, hoses and clamps.** Age, cracking, and whether clamps are marine grade.

**Mounts.** Sagging, cracked rubber, corroded studs.

**Wiring and the loom.** Corroded connectors, added wiring, tape repairs.

**Signs of past overheating.** Discoloured paint, scale trails, replaced hoses in
one area, a new heat exchanger on an otherwise original engine.

**Salt trails and rust streaks.** They point at leaks, past and present.

## Under load

A sea trial is essential, and it must include time at cruise and a run to full
throttle.

- Does it reach the rated maximum rpm? An engine that will not is over-propped,
  fouled, or down on power.
- Temperature stability at sustained cruise
- Exhaust colour under load
- Vibration through the range
- Gear engagement, ahead and astern
- Any smell of exhaust in the accommodation

## Tests worth paying for

**Oil analysis.** Cheap relative to what it reveals — wear metals, coolant,
fuel dilution, water.

**Compression or leak-down test.** The most direct measure of internal
condition. Leak-down additionally tells you where the loss is.

**Injector testing** on an engine with starting or smoke symptoms.

**Coolant pressure test** where a head gasket is suspected.

**A marine engine surveyor**, separate from the hull surveyor, for a
high-value engine. On a repower-scale purchase this is inexpensive insurance.

## Using what you find

Findings are either deal-breakers or negotiation. A deferred heat exchanger
service is a number. A block with unknown history and no records is a risk that
should be priced or walked away from.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a standard boat survey include the engine?**
Usually only superficially. Engine detail is typically a separate engagement.

**Is a compression test necessary?**
On any significant purchase, yes. It is the most direct evidence of internal
condition available without opening the engine.

**What is oil analysis and is it worth it?**
A lab test of a used oil sample for wear metals, coolant, fuel and water. It is
inexpensive and frequently decisive.

**Should I trust a seller's service records?**
Records with matching invoices from identifiable workshops are strong evidence.
A handwritten list with no invoices is not.

**What is the biggest red flag?**
An engine that has been warmed up before you arrive, combined with no records.
