# Marine diesel engine glossary

The terms a marine mechanic will use on the phone, in plain language.

## Cooling

**Raw water** — the seawater or river water drawn in from outside the boat to
carry heat away. It never touches the inside of the engine on an
indirectly-cooled installation.

**Coolant / fresh water circuit** — the closed loop of treated water and
antifreeze that circulates through the engine itself.

**Heat exchanger** — the component where the two circuits meet. Coolant passes
over a bundle of tubes carrying raw water.

**Aftercooler / intercooler** — cools the air after the turbocharger, before it
enters the cylinders. Cooler air is denser air.

**Keel cooler** — an external cooling arrangement where coolant is circulated
through pipes or a plate in contact with the water outside the hull, with no raw
water pumped aboard.

**Impeller** — the flexible rubber vaned wheel inside the raw water pump. A
wearing item, and the most common single cause of overheating.

**Anti-siphon / vented loop** — a fitting that prevents seawater siphoning back
into the engine through the exhaust when the engine is stopped.

## Exhaust

**Wet exhaust** — the standard arrangement where raw water is injected into the
exhaust to cool and silence it.

**Mixing elbow** — where that water joins the exhaust gas. A known corrosion
point that needs periodic inspection.

**Dry exhaust** — an insulated exhaust with no water injection, common on
commercial vessels.

**Waterlock / muffler** — a chamber that holds water and stops it running back
towards the engine.

## Drive

**Shaft drive** — the propeller shaft passes through the hull at an angle, in a
stern tube, sealed by a stern gland.

**Stern gland / shaft seal** — the seal where the shaft passes through the hull.
Traditional packing glands drip slightly by design; dripless seals do not.

**Cutless bearing** — the water-lubricated bearing supporting the shaft near the
propeller.

**Saildrive** — a sealed vertical leg through the hull with the propeller on the
bottom, sealed by a rubber diaphragm.

**Sterndrive / outdrive** — an inboard engine driving a steerable leg through
the transom.

**IPS / pod drive** — forward-facing pod propulsion mounted through the hull,
steering by rotating the pods.

**Reduction ratio** — how much the gearbox slows the propeller relative to the
engine, e.g. 2:1.

**Thrust bearing** — the bearing that takes the propeller's push and transfers
it into the boat, usually inside the gearbox.

## Fuel and combustion

**Injector** — sprays atomised fuel into the cylinder. Wears, and can be tested
and reconditioned.

**Injection pump** — meters and delivers fuel at high pressure to the injectors.

**Common rail** — a modern system holding fuel at very high pressure in a shared
rail, with electronically controlled injectors.

**Lift pump** — the low-pressure pump that brings fuel from the tank.

**Water separator** — the primary filter that removes water before it reaches
the engine.

**Bleeding** — purging air from the fuel system after a filter change or a run
dry.

**Blow-by** — combustion gas escaping past the piston rings into the crankcase.
Excessive blow-by indicates bore or ring wear.

## Condition and testing

**Compression test** — measures cylinder pressure; a broad indicator of engine
condition.

**Leak-down test** — pressurises a cylinder and measures where the air escapes,
which localises the problem to rings, valves or head gasket.

**Oil analysis** — laboratory testing of a used oil sample for wear metals,
fuel, coolant and water.

**Bore glazing** — a polished cylinder wall from prolonged light loading, which
stops the rings sealing and increases oil consumption.

**Survey** — a formal inspection of a vessel, often required for insurance,
sale, or commercial operation.

## Corrosion

**Sacrificial anode** — a metal deliberately chosen to corrode instead of the
components around it.

**Galvanic corrosion** — corrosion driven by two dissimilar metals connected in
seawater.

**Stray current corrosion** — corrosion driven by an electrical fault, much
faster and more destructive than galvanic corrosion.

**Pencil anode** — the small anode threaded into a heat exchanger or cooler.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between raw water and coolant?**
Raw water is the seawater drawn in from outside to remove heat. Coolant is the
treated closed-circuit fluid inside the engine. They meet only in the heat
exchanger.

**What is a mixing elbow?**
The fitting where raw water is injected into the exhaust to cool it. It corrodes
over time and is a routine inspection item.

**What does 2:1 reduction mean?**
The propeller turns at half engine speed.

**What is blow-by?**
Combustion gas leaking past the piston rings into the crankcase — a sign of bore
or ring wear when excessive.

**What is bore glazing?**
Cylinder walls polished smooth by long periods of light load, so the rings no
longer seal properly.
