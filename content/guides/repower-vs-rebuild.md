# Repower or rebuild your marine diesel

This decision is made badly more often than almost any other in boat ownership,
usually because it gets framed as a price comparison between two quotes when it
is really a comparison between two different outcomes.

## What each one actually gives you

**A rebuild** returns the existing engine to serviceable condition. Everything
around it — mounts, wiring loom, control cables, instruments, exhaust, cooling
hoses, fuel lines, the gearbox — stays as old as it was.

**A repower** replaces the engine, and in practice usually replaces a good deal
of what surrounds it, because a new engine rarely bolts straight onto old
foundations with old wiring and old controls.

That difference is why repower quotes look so much larger. They frequently
include work a rebuild quote silently defers.

## Questions that decide it

**Is the block serviceable?**
If it is at the end of its overbore range, cracked, or has been rebuilt before,
a rebuild may be a one-time-only proposition or not possible at all. This is the
first thing a workshop should establish.

**Are parts available?**
Some engines are exceptionally well supported in Australia decades on. Others
are not, and a rebuild that stalls waiting for parts is expensive in ways the
quote never showed.

**How old is everything else?**
An engine with a tired loom, corroded mixing elbow, perished mounts and
thirty-year-old instruments is not really an engine decision. Add the deferred
items to the rebuild quote before comparing.

**What is the gearbox condition?**
A rebuild usually leaves the gearbox alone. If it is close to needing work, that
belongs in the comparison.

**How long will you keep the boat?**
A rebuild suits a boat you intend to sell within a few years. A repower suits a
boat you intend to keep.

**Can the new engine physically fit?**
Modern engines are often physically different — different mounting points,
different height, different exhaust arrangement, sometimes different gearbox
alignment. Access through the hatch is a real constraint on some boats.

**Do you need the electronics?**
Modern electronically controlled engines bring diagnostics, better fuel
consumption and cleaner running, but also require a technician with the right
diagnostic tools. On a remote-cruising boat this cuts both ways.

## Where the money hides

Both routes have costs that do not appear until the engine is out or opened up:

- Machining discovered to be necessary once the block is stripped
- Engine beds needing modification or reglassing for a new engine
- New propeller and shaft to match different power or gearbox ratio
- Exhaust system replacement to suit a different outlet
- New instrument panel and loom
- Crane, haul-out or hull opening to get the engine in and out

Ask for the quote to state explicitly what is excluded and what is
contingency.

## A reasonable process

1. Get a condition assessment before getting quotes — compression or leak-down,
   oil analysis, and an inspection of the surrounding systems.
2. Ask the rebuild workshop what they will do if the block turns out to be
   unserviceable, and what that costs.
3. Get the repower quote itemised into engine, installation, and ancillaries.
4. Add the deferred items to the rebuild side so you are comparing two boats,
   not two engines.
5. Consider resale. A documented repower is visible to a buyer; a rebuild is a
   claim in a listing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a rebuild always cheaper than a repower?**
On the engine alone, usually. Once the surrounding systems and the work a
repower includes are counted, the gap often narrows considerably.

**How do I know if my block can be rebuilt?**
A workshop needs to measure it. Bore condition, previous overbores, and cracks
are the deciding factors, and they are not visible from outside.

**Does a repower add value to the boat?**
It is generally the more visible and more verifiable improvement to a buyer,
particularly with documentation. Neither typically returns its full cost.

**Can I fit a different brand of engine?**
Often yes, but it depends on the beds, the space, the gearbox and the exhaust
arrangement. This is an installation question, not a catalogue question.
