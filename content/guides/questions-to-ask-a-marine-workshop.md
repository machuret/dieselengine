# Questions to ask a marine workshop before you book

Ask these before the job starts. Every one of them is harder to ask afterwards.

## About the diagnosis

**What exactly did you test, and what were the readings?**
A diagnosis backed by numbers — compression, pressures, temperatures, oil
analysis — is different from an opinion.

**What else could produce this symptom, and how did you rule those out?**
The answer tells you whether they diagnosed or guessed.

**If this fix does not solve it, what is the next step and who pays for the
first attempt?**
Agree this in advance, not after.

## About the quote

**Is this a fixed price or an estimate?**

**What is explicitly excluded?**
Parts at trade or retail? Consumables? Travel? Crane or haul-out? Disposal?

**What happens if you find more once it is open?**
Ask for a dollar threshold above which they must call you before continuing.

**What is the labour rate, and is there a minimum call-out?**

**Do you charge for diagnostic time separately, and is it credited against the
repair if I proceed?**

## About parts

**Genuine, aftermarket or reconditioned?**
All three are legitimate. You should know which you are buying.

**Are the parts in stock, and if not, what is the lead time?**
Parts availability drives most marine job delays.

**Do the parts carry their own warranty, and for how long?**

**Can I have the old parts back?**
A reasonable request, and a useful one.

## About the work

**Who will actually do it — and will it be the same person throughout?**

**When will you start, and when will it be finished?**
Get both. "We'll get to it" is how a boat spends six weeks on the hardstand.

**Will the boat be usable in between?**

**Will you road-test or sea-trial it afterwards?**
For anything affecting cooling, fuel delivery or the drivetrain, the job is not
finished until it has run under load.

## About accountability

**What warranty applies to your labour?**

**Is that warranty transferable if I sell the boat?**

**Will I get a written report of what was found and done?**
This is what protects your resale value and your consumer law position.

**Are you insured, and are you accredited to work in my marina?**

## After the job

Ask for:

- An itemised invoice showing parts and labour separately
- The written report of findings
- Any test results, before and after
- The old parts, if you asked for them
- A note of what they recommend watching or doing next

File all of it. On resale, a documented service history is worth real money, and
in a dispute it is your evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it rude to ask for a fixed price?**
No. It is reasonable for defined work like a service. For diagnostic and
internal work an estimate with an agreed call-back threshold is the fair
structure.

**Should I supply my own parts?**
You can, and it sometimes saves money, but most workshops will not warrant
labour on parts they did not supply.

**Can I ask for the old parts back?**
Yes, and it is a normal request. Ask before the job, not after.

**How long should a quote be valid?**
Ask. Parts prices move, and a quote should state its validity period.

**What if the final bill is much higher than the quote?**
Raise it immediately and in writing. An agreed call-back threshold, set before
the work started, is your best protection — see our guide to repair rights.
