# Petrol vs diesel boat engines

For a boat, this is not the same decision as it is for a car. The considerations
that dominate on the road — purchase price, fuel price at the pump, emissions
rules — are outranked on the water by safety, range, and how long the engine
will last in a marine installation.

## Safety, first

Petrol vapour is heavier than air and it collects in the bilge. A boat is a
sealed container with a low point, which is exactly the wrong shape for storing
a fuel that produces explosive vapour at ordinary temperatures.

This is why petrol installations require blowers, ignition-protected electrical
components and strict ventilation procedures, and why the pre-start routine on a
petrol inboard is not optional.

Diesel does not produce an ignitable vapour at normal ambient temperatures. For
inboard installations this single fact drives much of the market.

## Range and consumption

Diesels are more efficient and are usually operated at lower revs for a given
job. On a displacement hull the difference in range from the same tankage is
substantial, and range is the thing that determines where you can go.

For a boat used for long coastal passages in Australia — where the distance
between fuel stops can be considerable — this is often the deciding factor on
its own.

## Engine life

Marine diesels are built to run at sustained high load for long periods. Marine
petrol engines are generally lighter-duty designs asked to do the same work.

The practical result is that diesel installations tend to have longer service
lives under the loads a boat imposes, which is why commercial vessels are
almost uniformly diesel.

## Where petrol still wins

**Purchase price.** Petrol engines are cheaper to buy and cheaper to install.

**Weight.** A lighter engine matters on a small planing hull.

**Power to weight at high speed.** For fast, light boats, petrol is competitive.

**Small outboards.** Below a certain size, petrol outboards dominate for good
reasons — cost, weight, simplicity, availability of service.

**Servicing familiarity.** Petrol engines are closer to automotive practice,
which can help in some areas.

## The Australian angle

Diesel dominates inboard installations here, which has a knock-on effect: the
service network, the parts availability and the mechanic expertise are oriented
that way, particularly outside the capitals. A diesel inboard is easier to get
serviced in a regional port than a petrol inboard.

## Rough guide

| Boat type | Usual choice |
|---|---|
| Small runabout, trailered | Petrol outboard |
| Trailerable sports boat | Petrol, sometimes diesel sterndrive |
| Cruising yacht | Diesel inboard |
| Coastal cruiser / flybridge | Diesel inboard |
| Commercial and charter | Diesel |
| Long-range motor yacht | Diesel |

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are most boat inboards diesel?**
Safety of the fuel in an enclosed hull, better range from the same tankage, and
longer service life under sustained load.

**Is diesel cheaper to run in a boat?**
Usually, because of efficiency and range rather than pump price. The purchase
premium takes time to recover on a lightly used boat.

**Are petrol inboards dangerous?**
They are safe when the ventilation, blower and ignition-protection requirements
are properly followed. The risk comes from shortcuts and from poor maintenance
of the fuel system.

**Can I convert a petrol boat to diesel?**
It is a repower with weight, beds, tankage and often exhaust changes. It is
occasionally done and it is rarely cheap.

**Do diesels last longer than petrol marine engines?**
Under the sustained loads boats impose, generally yes — they are built for that
duty cycle.
