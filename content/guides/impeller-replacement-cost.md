# Impeller replacement cost

This is one of the cheapest jobs on a marine engine and one of the most
expensive to skip. The impeller is the flexible rubber wheel inside the raw
water pump, and when it fails the engine loses its cooling water.

{{PRICE_TABLE:impeller-replacement}}

## What you are paying for

**The impeller itself** — a small rubber part, priced by engine and pump model.

**A gasket or O-ring** for the pump cover, which should be replaced at the same
time.

**Labour**, which is almost entirely a function of access. On some engines the
pump is at the front, at arm's height, and the job takes minutes. On others it
is behind the alternator, under the exhaust, reachable only by feel, and it
takes an hour.

**Any consequential work**, which is where the real variation lives.

## Why the cheap job becomes an expensive one

When an impeller fails it usually sheds vanes, and those vanes go downstream —
into the heat exchanger tubes, sometimes into the exhaust elbow. Finding them
matters:

- **Every vane must be accounted for.** Count the vanes on the old impeller
  against what the new one has. Missing pieces are somewhere in the cooling
  circuit.
- **Retrieving them** may mean removing the heat exchanger end cap, which is a
  separate job.
- **A blocked heat exchanger** after a shredded impeller causes the next
  overheat, and the owner blames the pump.

So "impeller replacement" after a failure is a different job from a scheduled
impeller change, and it should be quoted that way.

## Cost drivers

- **Pump access** — the dominant factor in labour
- **Whether the cover screws come out**, or shear and need extraction
- **Pump condition** — a scored wear plate or worn cam means the new impeller
  will not last, and the pump may need rebuilding or replacing
- **Shaft seal condition** — a weeping pump shaft usually means the pump needs
  more than an impeller
- **Whether vanes must be retrieved** from downstream
- **Mobile call-out** versus a job done alongside a service
- **Location**

## Doing it as part of a service

If the impeller is due, having it done during the annual service costs you the
part and a small amount of labour, because the mechanic is already there with
the engine bay open. Having it done urgently at a berth after an overheat costs
a call-out plus whatever the failure caused.

Scheduled is always the cheaper version of this job.

## Carrying a spare

A spare impeller and cover gasket aboard is standard practice, and on many
engines the job is within reach of an owner with a screwdriver and patience.
Knowing where your pump is, and having the spare, is the difference between an
inconvenience and a tow.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should an impeller be replaced?**
At the manufacturer's interval — commonly annually or at a set hour figure —
and immediately on any sign of reduced water flow.

**Can I replace a marine impeller myself?**
On many engines, yes: remove the pump cover, extract the old impeller, fit the
new one lubricated, replace the gasket. Access is the deciding factor.

**What happens if a vane breaks off?**
It travels downstream and can block the heat exchanger. Every vane must be
accounted for, and missing pieces retrieved.

**Why does my new impeller keep failing?**
Usually a worn wear plate or cam inside the pump, running dry on a blocked
intake, or a partially closed seacock. Replacing the impeller alone does not fix
those.

**Is it worth replacing the whole pump?**
If the wear plate is scored, the cam is worn or the shaft seal weeps, yes — a
new impeller in a worn pump has a short life.
