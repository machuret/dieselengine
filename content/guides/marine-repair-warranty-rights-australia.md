# Your repair rights under Australian Consumer Law

Boat owners often assume that once the invoice is paid, a bad repair is bad
luck. It generally is not. Australian Consumer Law provides guarantees that
apply to services as well as to goods, and they cannot be signed away by a
disclaimer on a job card.

This is general information to help you have a better conversation, not legal
advice. For a dispute that matters, the ACCC and your state consumer affairs or
fair trading body are the authorities, and they publish current guidance.

## The consumer guarantees, in plain terms

When you buy services in Australia, they come with guarantees that the work will
be:

- **provided with due care and skill**
- **fit for the purpose you made known**, and
- **delivered within a reasonable time** where no time was agreed

For goods — a new engine, a reconditioned unit, parts — the guarantees include
acceptable quality, matching the description, and being fit for purpose.

These apply automatically. A sign saying "no refunds" or a term in the job card
disclaiming liability does not remove them.

## Major and minor failures

The remedy depends on how serious the problem is.

For a **minor failure**, the provider gets the opportunity to fix it — repair,
or re-do the work — within a reasonable time and at their cost.

For a **major failure**, you generally get to choose: cancel and get a refund
for the unused portion, or keep it and get compensation for the drop in value. A
failure is major when the problem is substantial, when the service is
substantially unfit for its purpose, or when you would not have bought it had
you known.

You may also be able to recover consequential losses that were reasonably
foreseeable — for a boat, that can include the cost of putting right damage
caused by the bad work.

## What decides these disputes

Almost always, evidence.

**Get the quote in writing** with scope and exclusions stated.

**Agree a call-back threshold** — a dollar figure above which they must contact
you before doing more work. This is your single strongest protection against a
bill that triples.

**Keep the invoice itemised** into parts and labour.

**Ask for a written report** of what was found and done.

**Ask for the old parts back** if you have any doubt.

**Photograph** the state of things before and after.

An owner with a written quote, an itemised invoice and photographs is in a
completely different position from one with a verbal price and a card payment.

## If something goes wrong

1. **Raise it promptly and in writing.** Email creates a record; a phone call
   does not.
2. **Describe the problem factually** and say what you want — repair, re-do,
   refund of the unused portion.
3. **Give them a reasonable opportunity to fix a minor failure.** Refusing that
   weakens your position.
4. **Get an independent assessment** if they dispute the fault. A second
   workshop's written opinion is powerful evidence.
5. **Escalate to your state consumer body** if it does not resolve. Most have a
   complaints process before any tribunal step.
6. **Small claims tribunal** in your state handles disputes up to a monetary
   limit, without needing a lawyer.

## Warranty versus consumer guarantees

A manufacturer's or workshop's warranty is an **extra** promise. It sits on top
of the consumer guarantees; it does not replace them. So a warranty that has
expired does not necessarily end your rights, particularly where a component
failed much sooner than a reasonable person would expect.

For marine engines this matters, because "reasonable" is judged partly on price
and expected life — and a marine engine is an expensive, long-lived item.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Australian Consumer Law cover boat repairs?**
The consumer guarantees apply to services generally, including repair services,
subject to the thresholds and exclusions in the law. Check your circumstances
with the ACCC or your state fair trading body.

**The workshop's job card says they are not liable. Is that valid?**
A business cannot contract out of the consumer guarantees. Terms attempting to
do so are generally not effective.

**My engine failed just after the warranty expired. Do I have any rights?**
Possibly. The consumer guarantees consider what is reasonable given the price
and nature of the goods, independently of a warranty period.

**What if the final bill is far above the quote?**
Raise it immediately in writing. An agreed call-back threshold set before the
work is your best protection; without one, whether the extra was reasonable and
authorised becomes the question.

**Who do I complain to?**
Start with the business, in writing. Then your state or territory consumer
affairs or fair trading body. Small claims tribunals handle disputes below a
set amount.
