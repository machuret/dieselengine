# Marine engine emissions rules in Australia

Emissions is the area where boat owners most often act on half-remembered
information, usually imported from car regulation or from another country. The
picture in Australia is layered, and the layer that applies to you depends on
what your vessel is and where it operates.

Treat this as orientation. The current detail lives with AMSA, the Commonwealth
department administering marine pollution legislation, and your state
environmental and maritime agencies.

## The layers

**International rules, applied domestically.** Australia is party to MARPOL, the
international convention on pollution from ships. Annex VI covers air pollution
from ships, including nitrogen oxide limits on marine diesel engines above a
power threshold and limits on the sulphur content of marine fuel. These are
implemented through Commonwealth legislation and Marine Orders. They are aimed
primarily at commercial shipping, and the engine power threshold puts most
recreational engines outside them.

**Domestic commercial vessel rules.** Vessels in the national system have
obligations relating to pollution prevention, and surveyors look at oily water,
fuel containment and discharge arrangements.

**State and territory rules.** This is the layer most likely to affect a
recreational owner. States regulate discharge into their waters, and some waters
— marine parks, enclosed waterways, drinking water catchments — carry additional
restrictions. Excessive smoke and oil discharge can attract penalties.

**Engine manufacturer certification.** Engines sold new are usually certified to
an emissions standard for a market — commonly US EPA tiers or EU stages. This is
a product characteristic and it matters when importing an engine or repowering,
because it can affect what is available and what a manufacturer will support.

## What this means in practice

**For a recreational owner with an existing engine.** There is generally no
periodic emissions test to pass, as there is for a car in some jurisdictions.
What you do have is a duty not to pollute: no oil discharge, no visible
excessive smoke, and proper handling of waste oil and bilge water.

**Persistent black or blue smoke is a maintenance problem before it is a
compliance problem.** It means fuel or oil is being wasted, and the engine is
telling you something. See our smoke diagnosis guides.

**For anyone repowering.** Ask what standard the new engine is certified to and
whether that matters for your intended operation, particularly if the vessel is
or may become commercial.

**For anyone importing an engine.** Certification, parts support and service
support in Australia are all worth checking before the purchase, not after.

**For commercial operators.** Your obligations are more specific and they are
part of survey. Speak to your surveyor.

## Waste oil and bilge water

The rules that catch people are these, and they apply everywhere:

- Do not discharge oil or oily water overboard
- Use the waste oil facilities at your marina or a licensed collector
- Keep the bilge free of oil so that an automatic bilge pump does not do it for
  you
- Deal with a fuel or oil leak promptly rather than letting the bilge absorb it

An oily sheen from a bilge pump discharge is the most common way a recreational
owner encounters this regulation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do recreational boats need an emissions test in Australia?**
There is generally no periodic emissions test equivalent to a car inspection.
Discharge and pollution rules still apply.

**Is there a rule about black smoke from a boat engine?**
Excessive smoke can attract attention under state pollution rules, and it always
indicates a fault worth fixing — overloading, injector problems, restricted air,
or a turbo issue.

**What is MARPOL Annex VI?**
The international convention annex covering air pollution from ships, including
engine nitrogen oxide limits above a power threshold and fuel sulphur limits.
It is aimed at commercial shipping.

**Can I import a marine engine into Australia?**
Generally yes, but check certification, parts availability and local service
support first — and whether the vessel is or will be in commercial survey.

**Where do I dispose of used engine oil?**
At a marina waste oil facility or a licensed waste oil collector. Never
overboard, and never into a stormwater drain.
