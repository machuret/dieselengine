# How a marine gearbox works

A marine gearbox does three things: it selects ahead, astern or neutral; it
reduces engine speed to a propeller speed that actually works; and it takes the
propeller's thrust and passes it into the boat.

It is not a car gearbox. There is one forward ratio, not five, and the reverse
ratio is usually the same as forward — a boat is expected to manoeuvre astern
with real authority.

## Reduction is the point

A diesel makes its power at two to three thousand rpm. A propeller large enough
to move a displacement hull efficiently cannot turn anywhere near that fast
without cavitating.

So the gearbox reduces. A 2:1 box turns the propeller at half engine speed; a
3:1 box at a third. Larger reductions swing larger, slower propellers, which is
generally more efficient for heavier and slower boats.

This is why the gearbox ratio, the propeller and the hull have to be considered
together. Change one and the others may no longer suit.

## How the clutches engage

Most modern marine gearboxes are hydraulic. An oil pump driven off the input
shaft supplies pressure; moving the control lever directs that pressure to one
of two clutch packs — ahead or astern. Release the pressure and you are in
neutral.

Consequences worth knowing:

- **Pressure comes from engine rotation.** The box will not engage properly at
  very low idle if the pump cannot make pressure.
- **Oil level and oil condition are the clutch's operating fluid**, not just
  lubrication. Low oil means slipping clutches.
- **Engagement should be positive and reasonably quick.** A box that takes
  several seconds to bite, or bites and then slips under load, is telling you
  something.

Smaller sailboat gearboxes are often mechanical cone-clutch designs instead,
engaged directly by the lever. They are simple, and they are unforgiving of
being slammed between gears at speed.

## The thrust bearing

The propeller pushes the boat by pushing on the shaft, and something has to
absorb that push. In most installations that something is a thrust bearing
inside the gearbox.

This is why the gearbox mounts and the flexible coupling matter, and why a
gearbox that has been run with a badly misaligned shaft often fails at the
bearing rather than the clutches.

## Reading the oil

Gearbox oil tells you most of what you need to know:

| What you see | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Clean, correct level | Normal |
| Milky | Water ingress — often the oil cooler |
| Dark and burnt-smelling | Clutch slip |
| Metallic particles | Internal wear |
| Level rising | Water or engine oil entering |

Milky gearbox oil after a cooler failure is common: the gearbox oil cooler often
sits in the raw water circuit, and a corroded cooler will let seawater in.

## Alignment is the owner's real job

Nothing kills a marine gearbox faster than a shaft that is not aligned to it.
The symptoms — vibration, a stern gland that will not stop dripping, coupling
bolts that keep loosening, mounts that sag — usually appear before the gearbox
itself complains.

Alignment should be checked after a repower, after a grounding, after new engine
mounts, and periodically on a boat that flexes.

## Frequently asked questions

**What oil goes in a marine gearbox?**
It depends entirely on the make and model — some take automatic transmission
fluid, others engine oil of a specified grade. Using the wrong one causes clutch
problems. Check the plate on the box or the manual.

**Why is my gearbox slow to engage?**
Low oil, worn clutch packs, a control cable not reaching full travel, or an idle
speed too low to build oil pressure. Cable adjustment is the cheapest thing to
rule out.

**What does the reduction ratio mean?**
It is how much slower the propeller turns than the engine. 2:1 means half engine
speed.

**Can I change the gearbox ratio?**
Sometimes, and it is a common part of solving a propeller mismatch — but it
changes the propeller requirement too. It is a calculation job, not a guess.

**Why is my gearbox oil milky?**
Water is getting in, most often through a failed gearbox oil cooler in the raw
water circuit. Stop running it and have the cooler pressure tested.
