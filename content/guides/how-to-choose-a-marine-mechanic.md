# How to choose a marine diesel mechanic

Marine engine work is expensive and hard to verify after the fact. Choosing well
matters more than negotiating hard.

## Marine, not automotive

A diesel mechanic who works on trucks is not automatically able to work on your
boat. The engine block may be a relative of something automotive, but the raw
water circuit, the wet exhaust, the gearbox, the shaft, the galvanic environment
and the installation constraints are not.

Most expensive mistakes on marine engines come from someone treating the cooling
system and the exhaust as if they were automotive.

## What to look for

**Experience with your engine family.** Ask directly which brands and families
they work on regularly. Someone who sees your engine weekly will diagnose faster
and stock the right parts.

**The right diagnostic capability.** Modern electronically controlled engines
require the manufacturer's or an approved diagnostic tool. Ask whether they have
it for your engine before booking.

**Mobile or workshop.** Mobile mechanics come to your berth, which suits
servicing and most diagnostics. Major work needs a workshop. Ask which they are
set up for.

**Insurance and licensing.** Public liability cover, and whether they are
authorised to work in your marina — many marinas require contractors to be
registered with them.

**Written quoting.** A quote should say what is included, what is excluded, what
is an estimate versus a fixed price, and what happens if something is found once
the job is open.

**Willingness to explain.** A mechanic who will tell you what they found and why
they recommend the work is worth more than one who is slightly cheaper.

## Warning signs

- A diagnosis over the phone with no inspection, followed by a large quote
- Replacing parts sequentially without testing, and billing for each
- Reluctance to give anything in writing
- No mention of the raw water side when quoting an overheating fix
- Cash-only with no invoice — which also removes your consumer law protections
- Pressure to decide immediately

## Before they arrive

You will get a better outcome and a smaller bill if you have:

- Engine make, model and serial number
- Approximate hours
- What the symptom is, when it started, and what conditions bring it on
- What has been done recently
- Any service records you hold
- Clear access to the engine bay

"It overheats above 2000 rpm after twenty minutes, and it started after I ran
over a rope" is worth an hour of diagnostic time.

## Getting a second opinion

For anything approaching the cost of a rebuild or repower, a second opinion is
normal and reasonable. A good workshop will not object. If a major job is
recommended, ask what evidence supports it — compression figures, oil analysis,
photographs, a leak-down result — and take that evidence with you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do marine mechanics need a licence in Australia?**
Requirements vary by state and by the type of work. Electrical and gas work is
separately licensed. Ask about qualifications, insurance and marina
accreditation, and get everything invoiced.

**Should I use a dealer or an independent?**
Dealers have factory diagnostics, training and warranty authority, which matters
on newer electronically controlled engines. Independents are often better value
on older mechanical engines. Match to your engine.

**Is a mobile marine mechanic as good as a workshop?**
For servicing and most diagnostics, yes. Major internal work needs a workshop.

**How do I know a quote is fair?**
Compare like for like on scope, not just total. Get a second quote for anything
substantial, and ask what evidence supports the diagnosis.

**What if the work is not done properly?**
You have rights under Australian Consumer Law for services as well as goods.
Keep the invoice and the quote — see our guide to repair rights.
