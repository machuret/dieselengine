# Marine diesel in Coffs Harbour

Halfway between Sydney and Brisbane, on a coast where boats are offshore within
minutes of leaving the breakwater.

## What breaks here

**Fuel, under motion.** The harbour is small and the coast outside it is open
and swell-prone. The fleet fishes well out. A tank that is fine alongside finds
its water and sludge in a seaway, and the filters block at the worst moment.

**Cooling, moderately.** Sub-tropical water without the intensity of the Far
North. Heat exchangers here foul at a pace between Sydney's and Cairns', and
anode consumption is brisk but not extreme.

**Current loading.** The East Australian Current runs strongly down this
section of coast. Boats work with it or against it, and it affects passage
planning and fuel burn more than owners further south expect.

**Deferred work on transient boats.** Coffs is a well-used stop for boats moving
up and down the coast, and the trade sees a lot of maintenance that has been
put off for a passage.

## The trade

The Coffs Harbour International Marina and the harbour precinct hold the local
capability, serving a commercial fishing fleet, charter and dive operations, and
a recreational fleet that fishes offshore.

It is a genuinely regional market: competent local service, limited dealer
breadth.

Two things follow from being a regional harbour rather than a capital.

**Mobile attendance is the norm.** The marina and harbour berths are reachable
by road, and most servicing and diagnostics happen where the boat lies rather
than by moving it.

**The people who support your engine after the job matter more than the quote.**
In a market this size, the question is not who is cheapest today but who will
still be servicing your engine family in five years. Ask what they hold, what
they have worked on, and who covers them when they are away.

## Booking

**Hardstand and slipway capacity is finite** in a regional harbour, and whale
and game seasons bring peak activity. Book well ahead, or plan to take the boat
to a capital for major work.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my engine falter offshore but not in the harbour?**
Motion stirs settled water and sludge into the fuel pickup. It is tank
contamination rather than an engine fault.

**Can most work be done at the berth?**
Servicing and diagnostics, generally yes — mobile attendance is the norm here.
Anything below the waterline needs the slipway, which is the booked resource.

**What limits work here?**
Hardstand and slipway capacity, which is finite. Book well ahead.

**Does the East Australian Current matter?**
It runs strongly here and affects passage planning and fuel burn noticeably.

**What power margin do I need?**
Enough for an exposed coast with real swell — boats are offshore within minutes
of leaving the harbour.
