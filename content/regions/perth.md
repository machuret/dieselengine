# Marine diesel in Perth

Perth is the most isolated major boating market in Australia, and the isolation
shapes every decision — not just what you buy, but how you maintain and what you
carry aboard.

## Ask about state stock, not national availability

The question that matters here is not whether a part exists. It is whether it is
in Western Australia.

Anything that is not comes across the Nullarbor by road or by air, and both cost
days and dollars. A brand with a well-stocked WA distributor is worth more to a
Perth owner than a brand with better specifications and a Sydney warehouse.

The practical habits that follow:

- **Confirm WA parts holding** for your engine family, not just the brand
- **Carry more spares aboard** than an east coast owner would — impeller and
  gasket, filters, belts, hoses
- **Do not defer a part you have in hand** waiting for a convenient service date

## The sea breeze is a design condition

The afternoon south-westerly builds a short, steep chop through summer with
reliable regularity. Boats leaving Fremantle or Hillarys are in open Indian
Ocean immediately and they come home in it.

That produces the familiar pattern: an engine that runs fine leaving in the
morning and struggles on the way back, as motion stirs the fuel tank and the
filters block. Primary water-separating filtration with a vacuum gauge earns its
keep here.

It also argues against under-powering. An engine chosen at the bottom of the
design range is the one working hardest on the run home.

## The Swan River and the anode question

Boats berthed up the Swan sit in brackish water; boats at Fremantle and Hillarys
sit in full salinity. A boat that moves between them is in a changing
electrolyte, and that makes anode alloy a real decision rather than a default.

Magnesium is fresh water only. Zinc performs poorly in fresh. Aluminium works
across a wider range. See [why anodes matter](/guides/why-anodes-matter/).

## Rottnest and the offshore islands

Boats here routinely run further from help than their length suggests. Redundancy
and a real spares inventory matter more than they would on a sheltered east coast
harbour.

## The trade

**Fremantle** is the industrial and commercial end, with real engineering
capability attached to the fishing and offshore industries. **Hillarys** and
**Mandurah** serve a very large recreational and trailer-boat fleet.
**Applecross** and the Swan River carry their own service trade.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are parts harder to get in Perth?**
Anything not held in state comes across the Nullarbor. Ask about WA stock
specifically rather than national availability.

**Which engine brand is best in Perth?**
Weight local parts holding and local diagnostic capability heavily. It matters
more here than small differences in specification.

**Why does my engine falter on the way home?**
The afternoon sea breeze stirs the fuel tank. Settled water and sludge reach the
pickup and the filters block. It is a tank problem, not an engine fault.

**Does the Swan River affect anode choice?**
Yes. A boat moving between brackish river water and the ocean sits in a changing
electrolyte, and the alloy should be chosen deliberately.

**What should I carry for a Rottnest run?**
Spare impeller and cover gasket, fuel filters, belts, and the tools to fit them.
Distance from help is the Perth-specific factor.
