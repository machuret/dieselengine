# Marine diesel in Broome

The largest tides in Australia, a cruising ground with almost no infrastructure,
and a supply chain that runs two thousand kilometres to Perth.

## What breaks here

**Sediment, driven by the tide.** Broome's tidal range is among the largest in
the world, and on the springs the water carries an extraordinary amount of
suspended material. Strainers fill and heat exchangers foul at a rate that has
to be experienced to be believed. Service intervals that suit a southern boat
are meaningless here.

**Heat, with the tropical consequences.** Ventilation, cooling margin and
corrosion intervals all follow the pattern of the far north.

**Fuel, through the wet.** Tanks breathing humid monsoonal air, and long periods
between uses.

**Damage from drying moorings and careening.** Beach careening is part of local
practice, and it puts loads through a hull and drivetrain that a permanently
floating boat never sees. Alignment is worth checking after.

## Distance is the real failure mode

A boat cruising the Kimberley can be days from a workshop and out of mobile
coverage. There are no marinas along most of it and no fuel stops for long
stretches.

That changes what maintenance means. It is not about extending engine life — it
is about **not stopping somewhere you cannot be helped**:

- Redundancy where it can be afforded
- A genuine spares inventory built with the supplier before departure, not an
  afterthought
- An engine a competent person can work on without a factory laptop
- Conservative intervals on everything in the raw water circuit

This is one of the few places in Australia where mechanical simplicity is still
a serious argument against electronic efficiency for a cruising boat.

## Tides govern the yard

Slipway and hardstand access is tide-dependent, and a job programme is built
around access windows. Ask for the haul-out and relaunch dates first; everything
else fits between them.

## The trade

Capability sits around the port and the boat harbour, serving the Kimberley
charter fleet, the pearling industry, commercial fishing and remote support
vessels.

It is a small market with genuinely capable people, because it has to be. What
it does not have is dealer breadth or deep multi-brand parts holding. Parts come
from Perth by road freight or by air, and that is the defining commercial fact
of owning an engine here.

## Booking

The dry season, around the wet and cyclone risk. Slots fill early.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do my strainers fill so fast in Broome?**
The tidal range keeps an extraordinary amount of sediment suspended, especially
on spring tides. Southern service intervals do not apply.

**How long do parts take to reach Broome?**
Road freight from Perth takes days; air freight costs accordingly. Ask for
honest lead times and plan around them.

**Mechanical or electronic for Kimberley cruising?**
Mechanical simplicity carries real weight when a technician is a long flight
away. Weigh it against efficiency and local diagnostic capability.

**How do the tides affect getting work done?**
Slipway and hardstand access is tide-dependent. The haul-out and relaunch dates
frame the whole job.

**What spares should I carry for the Kimberley?**
Build the list with the supplier before departure. There is nowhere to buy them
along the coast.
