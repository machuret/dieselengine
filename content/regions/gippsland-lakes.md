# Marine diesel in the Gippsland Lakes

Australia's largest inland navigable waterway, and the only significant boating
region in the country where the water is often not salt. That one fact changes
a maintenance decision most owners never think about.

## The anode problem nobody warns you about

Salinity across the lakes varies enormously — fresher at the western end and up
the rivers, brackish through the middle, close to sea water near the Entrance.
It also shifts with rainfall and with the state of the Entrance itself.

That matters because **anode alloy is chosen for an electrolyte**:

- **Magnesium** is fresh water only and wastes very rapidly in salt
- **Zinc** performs poorly in fresh water, becoming passivated and protecting
  nothing
- **Aluminium** works across a wider range and is usually the answer for a boat
  that moves between them

A boat kept at Paynesville that runs down to the Entrance regularly is moving
through a changing electrolyte all season. Have the alloy chosen deliberately
rather than fitted by default.

## What else breaks here

**Weed and silt in the raw water.** The lakes carry weed, and the rivers feeding
them carry silt. Strainers clog, and a strainer you can clear quickly and safely
is worth having.

**Shallow water.** Much of the system is skinny, which puts intakes closer to
the bottom and more suspended material through them.

**Bore glazing from easy running.** Boats inside the lakes do a great deal of
low-load cruising in flat water, which is precisely the pattern that glazes
bores and builds carbon. It is worth running the engine up under real load
deliberately.

**Layup.** Gippsland winters are cold and the fleet sits.

## The trade

**Paynesville** is the recreational centre, with marina, slipway and a service
trade built around a large cruising and sailing fleet that lives on the water
year-round.

**Lakes Entrance** is the commercial fishing port and the only way in and out of
the system from Bass Strait, with the engineering a working fleet requires.

**Metung, Bairnsdale and Sale** add capacity around the system.

Local capability for servicing, repowers and slipping is genuinely good for a
regional area, because the fleet is large. Melbourne is three to four hours away
for anything unusual.

## Two different boats

A boat that crosses the Entrance and a boat that never leaves the lakes should
not be maintained the same way. The Entrance is a working bar, and it demands
power margin, reliable fuel delivery under motion, and cooling that copes with
sustained load at low boat speed. A lakes-only boat has a far gentler life.

## Frequently asked questions

**What anodes should I use in the Gippsland Lakes?**
Usually aluminium, for a boat moving between fresh, brackish and salt.
Magnesium is fresh water only and zinc performs poorly in fresh. Have it chosen
for your berth and your actual use.

**Why do my strainers keep clogging with weed?**
The lakes carry weed and the rivers carry silt. A strainer you can clear quickly
is a practical necessity here.

**Is low-load lake cruising bad for the engine?**
Prolonged light loading glazes bores and builds carbon. Run the engine up under
real load regularly.

**Does crossing the Entrance change what I need?**
Substantially. Power margin, fuel filtration and cooling under sustained load
all matter more for a boat that works the bar.

**Can major work be done locally?**
Yes. Paynesville and Lakes Entrance both have real capability, with Melbourne
three to four hours away for the unusual.
