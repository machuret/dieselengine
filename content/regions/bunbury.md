# Marine diesel in Bunbury and Busselton

South West WA: a sheltered bay, an unsheltered coast outside it, and Perth close
enough to supply the region but not close enough to service it.

## What breaks here

**Sand through the raw water intake.** Geographe Bay is shallow and sandy in its
inshore reaches, and a large part of the fleet is trailered and launched off
beaches and ramps. Sand abrades impellers and settles in heat exchangers. For
the trailer fleet, **flushing after each use does more good than any other
single habit** — and it is the one most often skipped.

**Fuel under motion, outside the bay.** Geographe Bay is comparatively benign;
the coast around the capes is not. The afternoon south-westerly builds a
reliable chop through summer, and boats come home in it. A tank that behaves in
the bay finds its sludge off the capes.

**Layup over a wet, cold winter.** The South West season is real and the fleet
quietens. Time-based deterioration is the local risk.

**Moderate corrosion.** Cool to temperate water. Anodes here last longer than in
Queensland, which is one thing an owner gets for free.

## The WA parts question, at one remove

Everything true of Perth's supply chain is true here with two hours added.

A part not held in Western Australia comes across the Nullarbor. A part not held
in Bunbury comes from Perth. Neither is a barrier and both are lead time, so the
question to ask a workshop is the same one Perth owners ask: **do you hold it,
or does it come from somewhere else, and how long?**

Confirm before authorising work rather than after.

## The trade

**Bunbury** has a working port, the Koombana Bay marina precinct and a
commercial fishing presence, plus industrial engineering attached to the port
and the region's resource industries. That gives it more heavy capability than
its recreational fleet would sustain.

**Busselton and the Geographe Bay coast** carry the recreational and charter
fleet, with Augusta and the capes fleet further south.

Perth is about two hours from Bunbury for dealer breadth and for anything
unusual.

## Booking

The wet winter months, when the recreational fleet is quiet and yards have
capacity.

## Frequently asked questions

**What matters most for a trailered boat here?**
Flushing after each use. Shallow sandy launching puts sand through the raw water
circuit, and flushing is the habit that prevents most of the damage.

**Why does my engine struggle coming home?**
The afternoon sea breeze builds a chop that stirs the fuel tank. Settled water
and sludge reach the pickup and the filters block.

**How long do parts take?**
What is not in Bunbury comes from Perth; what is not in WA comes across the
Nullarbor. Ask before authorising work.

**Is Geographe Bay hard on engines?**
The bay itself is comparatively benign. The shallow sandy inshore reaches and
the coast outside the capes are the harder parts.

**When should I book work?**
Winter, when the fleet is quiet and yards have room.
