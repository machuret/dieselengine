# Marine diesel in Brisbane

Brisbane's marine diesel trade is shaped by two bodies of water that behave
completely differently, and which one your boat lives in changes what goes wrong
with it.

## Two waters, two sets of problems

**Moreton Bay** is shallow, sandy and, across much of it, turbid. Boats at Manly,
Scarborough and Raby Bay draw sand into their raw water circuits. Strainers fill
faster than owners expect and heat exchangers silt from the inside.

**The Brisbane River** is a different environment again. It runs muddy, and after
heavy rain it runs very muddy, carrying debris with it. A boat berthed at
Rivergate or Newstead deals with a markedly dirtier raw water supply than one in
the bay, and the wet season makes it worse.

Both are warm. Warm water is the hardest condition a heat exchanger meets,
because there is less temperature difference available to move the heat, and it
consumes anodes faster than cooler southern water does.

## What that produces in local workshops

The complaints that walk through the door here cluster predictably:

**Overheating that is not the impeller.** The impeller gets replaced, the
problem returns, and the actual cause is a heat exchanger silted with bay sand
or river mud. Read
[how marine diesel cooling works](/guides/how-marine-diesel-cooling-works/) if
you have never had yours cleaned.

**Anodes gone, and nobody looked inside.** The pencil anode in the heat
exchanger is the most neglected component on a Queensland marine engine.

**Exhaust elbow corrosion.** Warm salt water and a wet exhaust. It is a
scheduled inspection item here, not a long-interval afterthought.

**Diesel bug.** Warm, humid, and boats that sit between uses. The tank breathes
moist air every night and condenses water on the walls. See
[marine diesel fuel quality and storage](/guides/marine-fuel-quality-australia/).

**Flood-affected boats and engines**, which is a Brisbane-specific question
worth asking about any second-hand purchase along the river.

## The trade here

Brisbane is the repower capital of the east coast. It has less new-boat
glamour than the Gold Coast an hour south and considerably more of the workshops
that take tired engines out and put fresh ones in. The river also carries
commercial workboats, so the engineering firms supporting them handle heavier
installations than a purely recreational precinct would.

The practical consequence for an owner: **you can get a genuine second quote
without anyone travelling far**, and a Coomera comparison is an hour away.

Precincts to know: **Rivergate** and **Newstead** on the river, **Manly** on the
bay, **Scarborough** at Redcliffe, **Raby Bay** at Cleveland.

## Timing

Queensland boating does not have Melbourne's hard off-season, but workshop
diaries still tighten ahead of summer and school holidays. The wet season, from
about December, disrupts river work and is when flooding risk is considered.

Booking a repower for spring in late winter is normal practice.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my engine overheat in Moreton Bay but not offshore?**
Shallow, sandy, turbid water loads the raw water circuit. Silted heat exchangers
and clogged strainers are far more common here than engine faults.

**How often should a Brisbane boat's heat exchanger be cleaned?**
More often than a generic manual interval suggests, and more often again for a
boat berthed up the river. Warm silty water is the hardest case.

**Is Brisbane a good place to repower?**
It has unusual depth in repower and refit work, plus commercial capability on
the river, and the Gold Coast is close enough for a comparison quote.

**Does flood history matter when buying a boat here?**
Yes, and it is a legitimate question about any second-hand boat or engine
sourced along the Brisbane River.

**Why do my anodes disappear so fast?**
Warm water accelerates everything galvanic. Check the pencil anode inside the
heat exchanger too — on many engines it has never been replaced.
