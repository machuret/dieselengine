# Marine diesel in Hervey Bay and Bundaberg

Two ports sharing a coast, a commercial whale watching fleet, and a steady
stream of cruising boats stopping to get work done.

## What breaks here

**Sand, from the Great Sandy Strait.** The strait is shallow, sandy and tidal,
and boats work in skinny water. Raw water intakes draw sand, which abrades
impellers and settles in heat exchangers. It is one of the more demanding
stretches of the Queensland coast for raw water circuits.

**Mud, from the Burnett.** The river runs muddy and after rain it runs very
muddy. A boat berthed up the river deals with a markedly dirtier supply than one
in the bay.

**Seasonal neglect on transient boats.** Cruising boats arriving here have often
deferred maintenance for a passage. What they need is rarely what they came in
for.

**Sub-tropical corrosion** — brisk, without the intensity of the Far North.

## The transient-boat pattern

Bundaberg Port Marina is the first Australian landfall for many international
cruising yachts and a regular stop for coastal cruisers. That gives the local
trade an unusual caseload: **boats whose maintenance was deferred for a
passage, arriving with a departure date.**

For a local owner that matters in one practical way — the workshops here are
used to working to a deadline and to diagnosing an unfamiliar engine quickly,
which is not true of every regional trade.

For a visiting owner it means getting a completion date in writing is a normal
request rather than an imposition.

## The trade

**Urangan Boat Harbour** at Hervey Bay serves the whale watching fleet, charter
operators and a substantial recreational fishing fleet.

**Bundaberg Port Marina** and the Burnett River carry the cruising-boat service
trade and the local fleet.

Dealer breadth is the constraint rather than capability. For an unusual engine
family, confirm parts availability before committing to a timeline.

## Booking

**Whale season**, roughly winter into spring, is when the commercial fleet is
flat out and workshop capacity is tightest. Plan major work outside it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the Great Sandy Strait hard on engines?**
It is shallow, sandy and tidal, so raw water intakes draw sand that abrades
impellers and settles in heat exchangers.

**Can work be done at the berth here?**
Servicing and diagnostics, generally yes. Anything below the waterline needs the
slipway, and whale season is when that capacity is tightest.

**When is workshop capacity tightest?**
Whale season, when the commercial fleet is at full operation.

**Does river berthing change servicing?**
The Burnett runs muddy, especially after rain, so strainers and heat exchangers
need more attention than for a bay-berthed boat.

**What limits work here?**
Dealer breadth rather than capability. Confirm parts for an unusual engine
before booking to a date.
