# Marine diesel in Gladstone

An industrial export port first and a boating town second, and that ordering
explains why the engineering here is better than the recreational fleet would
justify.

## What breaks here

**Harbour water fouls everything.** Gladstone harbour carries silt and
industrial activity, and it runs hard on the tide. Strainers clog and heat
exchangers foul faster than in clean reef water, and the difference is
substantial rather than marginal.

**Stray current.** This is the local condition worth knowing. An industrial
harbour with heavy shore power infrastructure is exactly the environment where
anodes disappear for reasons other than ordinary galvanic action — and stray
current is fast and destructive enough to consume a propeller in a season.

**If your wastage rate changed, investigate rather than fitting more anodes.**
See [why anodes matter](/guides/why-anodes-matter/).

**Load against current.** The harbour runs hard on the tide and boats work
against real streams.

**Tropical corrosion**, with the usual consequences for wiring, terminals and
the exhaust system.

## The trade

The harbour supports tugs, barges, pilot vessels and crew transfer craft, and
behind them sits heavy diesel engineering — the kind that maintains large
engines to schedule and to a documented standard.

The recreational fleet, which runs out to the southern Great Barrier Reef and
the Capricorn Bunker group, is served partly by that capability and partly by a
smaller dedicated trade around the marina and Auckland Point.

**One thing to watch**: if you are having recreational work done by people whose
normal customers are commercial, establish that the job is quoted as a
recreational installation rather than to commercial scale. The engineering
discipline is a genuine benefit; the scope should still match your boat.

For dealer breadth, Bundaberg, Rockhampton and Brisbane are the fallback.

## The calendar

Cyclone season from about November to April shapes the yard diary.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do my anodes disappear so fast in Gladstone harbour?**
Heavy shore power infrastructure raises the likelihood of stray current, which
is far faster and more destructive than galvanic action. A change in wastage
rate should be investigated.

**Does harbour water affect servicing intervals?**
Yes. Silt and industrial activity foul strainers and heat exchangers
considerably faster than clean reef water does.

**Is the engineering here good?**
Unusually so for the size of the recreational fleet, because the port's tug,
barge and pilot fleet sustains it.

**Should a workboat here consider keel cooling?**
For a vessel operating constantly in silty harbour water, it is worth genuine
consideration.

**Anything to watch when getting recreational work done?**
Confirm the job is quoted as a recreational installation rather than to
commercial scale.
