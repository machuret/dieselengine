# Marine diesel in Port Macquarie

A river fleet behind a bar that can be dangerous. That one fact raises the stakes
on every maintenance decision here.

## What breaks here

**Fuel delivery, exactly when it matters.** A bar crossing stirs a tank
comprehensively. If there is water or sludge on the bottom — and on a river-
berthed boat there usually is — that is the moment it reaches the filters.

This is the local failure mode, and it is not an engine fault. It is a tank
problem that only presents under motion, which is why a flat-water test proves
nothing. See
[marine diesel fuel quality and storage](/guides/marine-fuel-quality-australia/).

**Cooling under sustained load at low boat speed.** Working a bar means high
load without the water and airflow of cruising. A cooling system with no margin
shows it there.

**River silt.** The Hastings carries silt, and after rain a great deal of it.
Boats berthed up the river draw a dirtier raw water supply than a bay fleet
does, and strainer access matters.

**Brackish-to-salt movement.** A boat that runs between the upper river and the
sea sits in a changing electrolyte, which makes anode alloy a decision rather
than a default.

## The bar sets the standard

Everything the local trade recommends flows from the entrance:

- Do not under-power. Margin is what holds position when you need it.
- Fit a primary water-separating filter with a vacuum gauge, so restriction is
  visible before it becomes a stall.
- Service the raw water side on schedule, not when symptoms appear.
- Have a real plan for getting home on one engine, or on none.

A mechanic here who asks how often you cross the bar is asking the right
question.

## The trade

The fleet lives on the Hastings River, with marina and mooring capacity at Port
Macquarie and a commercial fishing presence. Local service is competent and
proportionate; dealer breadth is thin.

Newcastle is about two and a half hours south for heavy engineering and
machining, Coffs an hour and a half north, and Sydney within a comfortable day.
That puts real capability within reach without putting it on the doorstep.

## Booking

Temperate water and a real season. Off-season yard availability is better and
worth using.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my engine only falter on the bar?**
Motion stirs settled water and sludge off the tank bottom into the pickup. It is
tank contamination, and flat-water testing will not reproduce it.

**What should I fit for bar crossings?**
Power margin, and a primary water-separating filter with a vacuum gauge so you
can see restriction building.

**Does river berthing change servicing?**
The Hastings carries silt, especially after rain. Strainers need clearing more
often than for a bay-berthed boat.

**What anode alloy for a river boat here?**
It depends where the boat sits and how much it moves between brackish and salt.
Have it chosen deliberately.

**Where do I go for heavy engineering?**
Newcastle, about two and a half hours south, for machining and rebuild capacity.
