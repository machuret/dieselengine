# Marine diesel in the Whitsundays

Almost every engine in this water is working. That single fact sets the local
maintenance culture, and a private owner here benefits from standards built for
boats that cannot afford to break.

## Turnaround discipline

A bareboat comes back in the afternoon and goes out again the next morning. That
leaves a few hours, and it means the local trade is organised around work that
can be done between charters:

- **Fault-finding fast**, because a boat alongside is losing money
- **Parts on the shelf** for the families the fleet runs
- **Scheduled servicing planned around the booking calendar**, not around
  whenever the owner remembers
- **Preventive replacement** of items that would otherwise fail mid-charter

A private owner can buy into that discipline. Booking your servicing into the
fleet's quiet weeks gets you attention from people who fix things for a living
rather than as a favour.

## What breaks here, and why

**Raw water intakes full of weed, sand and coral debris.** Boats anchor in
sheltered bays over sand and grass, and the intake is close to the bottom. On a
charter turnaround the strainer is checked as routine; on a private boat it is
often the thing nobody looked at.

**Heat exchangers in warm water.** Less temperature difference to work with
means a partial blockage shows up as an alarm rather than as a slightly higher
gauge reading.

**Gearboxes and control cables**, worn by constant low-speed manoeuvring.
Charter crews and guests change gear far more often than a cruising owner does,
and clutch packs and cable adjustment take that wear.

**Saildrive legs and diaphragms** across the sailing cat fleet, which is
substantial here. Warm water consumes leg anodes quickly.

**Generators**, which on a boat with air conditioning and guests work harder
than either main engine and are neglected in proportion.

## The season and the cyclone calendar

Peak charter runs through the dry; the wet and cyclone risk from about November
to April change where boats are kept and what insurers require.

**Major work happens in the shoulder, and slipway capacity is the constraint
rather than parts supply.** Airlie Beach is well served by road from Mackay and
Townsville, so engines and components arrive routinely. Getting the boat out of
the water on the date you want is the harder problem.

If your vessel carries paying passengers, the engine and its installation are
part of survey — [what survey looks at](/guides/commercial-vessel-survey-requirements/)
covers that separately.

## Precincts

**Abell Point** at Airlie Beach is the main marina and service centre.
**Shute Harbour** provides Whitsunday access and supports commercial vessels.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do my strainers fill so quickly here?**
Boats anchor over sand and grass in sheltered bays, and intakes sit close to the
bottom. Weed and sand in the raw water circuit is normal, not a fault.

**Is it worth servicing at the same yards the charter fleet uses?**
Generally yes. They are organised around fast, reliable work and they stock parts
for the families the fleet runs.

**Why are gearboxes a local issue?**
Constant low-speed manoeuvring on differential thrust wears clutch packs and
control cables far faster than cruising use does.

**What is the hardest thing to book?**
Slipway capacity. Parts arrive by road routinely; getting the boat out of the
water on a chosen date is the constraint.

**When should I plan major work?**
The shoulder seasons, around the cyclone calendar and outside peak charter
periods.
