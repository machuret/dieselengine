# Marine diesel in Sydney

Sydney has the largest recreational fleet in Australia and the least room to
work on it. That single tension explains most of what is different about owning
a marine diesel here.

## The mooring problem

A very large share of Sydney boats live on swing moorings rather than in marina
berths. For servicing that is fine — a mobile mechanic works from a tender and
it is routine.

For anything larger it changes the job completely. The boat has to be delivered
to a facility, and hardstand and travel lift capacity across the basin is
scarce, contested and expensive. On the Gold Coast the lift is a given; in
Sydney it is frequently the thing that determines when your work happens and
what it costs.

**Build the delivery and the yard time into the quote from the start.** They are
real costs that simply do not exist for an owner berthed alongside a crane.

## Where the fleet is, and how it differs

**Pittwater** — the largest cruising and sailing concentration, and therefore the
centre of the saildrive trade. Diaphragms, leg anodes and sailboat auxiliaries.

**Middle Harbour and the lower harbour** — mixed, marina berths and swing
moorings, limited on-site work.

**Rozelle Bay** — commercial and workboat capability, and the hardstand that
goes with it.

**Botany and the Georges River** — trailer boats, sportfishers and a different
service trade again.

These are different markets with different problems, not one city.

## What the water does

Sydney water is temperate and comparatively clean, so the cooling side is far
less punishing than Queensland's. The harder local condition is **exposure**:
boats cross the Heads into open ocean regularly, and a southerly change turns a
flat harbour into something else within the hour.

That produces a specific and very common failure: **an engine that runs
perfectly on the harbour and falters offshore.** Motion stirs the tank, settled
water and sludge reach the pickup, and the filters block. It is a fuel tank
problem presenting as an engine fault, and it is the single most useful thing
for a Sydney owner to understand — see
[marine diesel fuel quality and storage](/guides/marine-fuel-quality-australia/).

## Winter is the owner's advantage

Sydney boating has a real seasonal shape. Yard availability, workshop capacity
and pricing are all better in the cooler months. A job booked for July is a
different negotiation from one wanted in November, and the difference is
measured in weeks of waiting as well as dollars.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my engine lose power only offshore?**
Motion stirs settled water and sludge off the tank bottom into the fuel pickup.
It is tank contamination, not an engine fault, and flat-water testing will not
reproduce it.

**Why is a Sydney repower more expensive than expected?**
Hardstand and travel lift capacity is scarce and expensive, and a boat on a
swing mooring must be delivered to a facility. Neither cost appears in the
engine price.

**Can a mechanic come to my mooring?**
For servicing and most diagnostics, yes — mobile work from a tender is routine
here. Major work needs a yard.

**When should I book yard work?**
The cooler months. Availability and pricing both improve when the fleet is not
using its boats.

**Is Sydney water hard on cooling systems?**
Less so than Queensland's. Temperate and comparatively clean water is the one
thing that is easier here.
