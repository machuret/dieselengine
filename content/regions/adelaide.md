# Marine diesel in Adelaide

Adelaide's marine year has a sharper shape than most Australian boating regions,
and the local pattern of engine problems follows the calendar more than it
follows the water.

## A short season and a long sit

Gulf St Vincent gets genuinely cold and the recreational fleet largely stops.
Boats sit from about May, and sitting is what damages them:

- Oil degrades by acid formation and moisture absorption regardless of hours
- Fuel tanks breathe humid air and condense water on the walls
- Impellers take a set and split on the first turn of the season
- Coolant inhibitors deplete on the calendar
- Anodes keep dissolving on the mooring with the engine switched off

The result is a fleet where the hour meters flatter and the internals do not,
and a service trade whose busiest weeks are the ones where everybody discovers
it at once.

**The single most useful habit here is servicing by the calendar rather than the
meter**, and having it done before the season rather than during it. See
[why low hours is not good news](/guides/low-hours-is-not-good-news/).

## Shallow water leaves marks

Much of the inner gulf is skinny, and the local workshops see the consequences
regularly: propellers and shafts touched on sandbars, cutless bearings worn after
a grounding, and alignment disturbed in ways that show up months later as
vibration or a stern gland that will not settle.

If your boat has touched, get the alignment checked rather than waiting to see
whether it matters. It usually does, and the gearbox pays for it eventually —
see [how a marine gearbox works](/guides/how-a-marine-gearbox-works/).

## Cold water changes the failure mode

Cold, comparatively clean water is easy on cooling systems. Heat exchangers here
silt slowly compared with Queensland's, and overheating is a much smaller share
of the local complaint list.

What it is hard on is starting. Battery capacity, glow arrangements and oil grade
all matter more, and the first start after a winter is where a season of neglect
announces itself.

## Two trades in one city

The recreational fleet is served by a trade proportionate to it. Behind that sits
the heavy marine engineering supporting South Australia's commercial fishing and
aquaculture industry, which gives the city more capability than its recreational
fleet alone would sustain.

For an owner that means rebuild and machining work can often be handled locally
even where dealer choice is thinner than in Melbourne or Sydney.

**North Haven** is the largest SA marina; **Outer Harbor** carries commercial and
hardstand capability; **Wirrina** serves the Fleurieu and gulf access.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to service if the boat barely moved?**
Yes. Oil, fuel, coolant and impellers all degrade on the calendar. In a region
with a short season this is the dominant failure mode.

**When should I book a service?**
Before the season, not during it. The whole fleet discovers its problems in the
same few weeks and the trade is fully committed.

**My boat touched a sandbar. What should I check?**
Propeller, shaft, cutless bearing and alignment. Alignment damage often shows up
months later as vibration or a stern gland that will not settle.

**Is overheating common in Adelaide?**
Less than in warmer states. Cold, comparatively clean water is easy on heat
exchangers, and starting problems are the more common cold-season complaint.

**Can major engineering be done locally?**
Yes. The commercial fishing and aquaculture industry sustains heavy marine
engineering capability beyond what the recreational fleet would support.
