# Marine diesel in Melbourne

Melbourne is the one major Australian market with a genuine off-season, and for
a boat owner that is worth money every single year.

## The layup problem is the local problem

Port Phillip boating drops away sharply from about May, and a great many boats
sit until spring. Sitting is what damages lightly used engines:

- **Oil degrades by acid formation and moisture absorption** whether the engine
  runs or not
- **Fuel tanks breathe humid air** every night and condense water on the walls
- **Impellers take a set** and crack when first turned
- **Coolant inhibitors deplete** on the calendar
- **Anodes dissolve on the mooring** with the engine off

The result is a fleet where hour meters flatter and internals do not. An
eighteen-year-old boat with 400 hours needs more scepticism than one with 2,000
— see [why low hours is not good news](/guides/low-hours-is-not-good-news/).

**The single most valuable habit for a Melbourne owner is servicing by the
calendar, not the meter.**

## Cold water changes the failure mode

Port Phillip is far colder than the Queensland coast. That is genuinely easier on
cooling systems — heat exchangers here silt slowly compared with Moreton Bay —
and harder on everything to do with starting.

Battery capacity, glow systems and oil grade all matter more here. So does the
first start of the season, which is where a winter's neglect announces itself.

## The Heads

Boats going outside face one of the more demanding entrances in the country.
Port Phillip Heads is unforgiving of an engine chosen at the bottom of its design
range, and it is a place where fuel delivery under motion is a safety matter
rather than a convenience.

## The trade

Capability is spread around the bay rather than concentrated: **Williamstown**
and the Yarra for commercial and larger recreational work, **Sandringham** and
**St Kilda** for the metropolitan fleet, **Hastings** on Western Port, and
**Mordialloc** on the creek. Geelong is across the bay.

Melbourne also has substantial general diesel engineering — machining, injection
service, turbocharger work — which supports the marine trade in a way that is not
true everywhere in Australia.

## Use the winter

Yards and workshops have capacity when the fleet is laid up. A repower done over
winter costs you no season, and quotes are more competitive when the diary is
not full.

An owner who discovers in November that the engine is finished has thrown that
advantage away. **If your engine is marginal, plan in autumn.**

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to service if I barely used the boat?**
Yes. Oil, fuel, coolant and impellers all degrade on the calendar regardless of
the hour meter. This is the main Melbourne failure mode.

**When is the best time to have work done?**
Winter. The fleet is laid up, yards have capacity, and you lose no season.

**Is cold water easier on the engine?**
On cooling, yes — noticeably. On starting, no. Battery capacity, glow
arrangements and oil grade all matter more.

**Is a low-hour Melbourne boat a good buy?**
Not automatically. Low hours here often means long periods sitting, which
carries its own deterioration. Look at records and oil analysis.

**What should I specify for going outside the Heads?**
Power margin and serious primary fuel filtration. The Heads punish an engine
chosen at the bottom of its range.
