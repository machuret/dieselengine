# Marine Diesel Engine Problems and Symptoms

Start with what your engine is actually doing. Each symptom below links to a
page with the likely causes ranked by how often they turn out to be the answer,
how a marine mechanic confirms each one, and what the repair costs.

## Shut down now if you see any of these

At sea the calculus is different from a road vehicle: you cannot pull over, and
a small problem becomes a rescue. These four mean shut down and assess.

- **[Overheat alarm sounding](/marine-engine-problems/overheat-alarm-sounding/)** —
  a marine diesel with no cooling water will destroy itself in minutes, and a
  melted exhaust hose is a fire and flooding risk.
- **[No water coming out of the exhaust](/marine-engine-problems/no-water-from-exhaust/)** —
  the earliest and clearest warning you get. Shut down before the temperature
  gauge moves.
- **[Low oil pressure alarm](/marine-engine-problems/marine-low-oil-pressure/)** —
  every second running is bearing damage.
- **[Water in the cylinders](/marine-engine-problems/water-in-cylinders/)** —
  do not crank the engine. Cranking a hydrolocked engine bends rods.

If you are underway and unsure, the safe order is: reduce load, check for
cooling water at the exhaust, shut down, then diagnose.

## A note on diagnosis

Marine faults chain together in a way that misleads. A partly blocked raw water
strainer reduces flow, which raises exhaust temperature, which cooks the exhaust
hose and accelerates elbow corrosion, which eventually blocks and backs water
into a cylinder. Four symptoms, one root cause, and only one of them cheap to
fix if caught early.

Read the symptom page before you authorise a part.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I keep motoring with an alarm sounding?**
Not for an overheat or oil pressure alarm. Both cause damage measured in
minutes. Reduce load, shut down, and diagnose.

**Why do so many marine problems come back to the raw water system?**
Because seawater is corrosive, carries debris, and passes through rubber, brass
and cast iron on its way through your engine. It is the system doing the hardest
job with the least attention.

**My engine ran fine last season — why is it failing now?**
Idle time is what damages marine engines. Fuel degrades, seals dry, impellers
take a set, batteries sulphate. The failure appears on the first hard run.
→ [Why low engine hours can be bad news](/guides/low-hours-is-not-good-news/)

**Is it worth fixing an old marine diesel?**
Frequently yes — marine diesels are built to be rebuilt, and a repower is a
major expense involving beds, controls, shaft and instruments.
→ [Repower or rebuild your marine diesel](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/)
