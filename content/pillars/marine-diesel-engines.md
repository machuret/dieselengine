# Marine Diesel Engines in Australia

A marine diesel shares a combustion principle with a truck engine and almost
nothing else that matters to you as an owner. It is cooled by the sea it floats
in, it corrodes from the inside as well as the outside, it spends most of its
life switched off, and when it stops working you cannot pull over.

This site explains what is actually happening inside your engine, what work
should cost in Australia, and how to find someone competent. We don't repair
engines and we don't sell parts.

## What makes a marine diesel different

### It is cooled by seawater

Almost every marine diesel in Australia is raw-water cooled. Seawater is drawn
in through a skin fitting, past a seacock and strainer, through a rubber-bladed
impeller pump, and through a heat exchanger where it cools the engine's own
closed coolant circuit before being injected into the exhaust and pushed
overboard.

That single design choice creates most of the failure modes on this site: the
impeller, the strainer, the heat exchanger, the exhaust elbow, and every
corroding component in between.

→ [How marine diesel cooling works](/guides/how-marine-diesel-cooling-works/) ·
[Raw water vs keel cooling](/guides/raw-water-vs-keel-cooling/)

### The exhaust carries water

Water is injected into the exhaust to cool it. It works well, and it means that
a corroded or carbon-blocked exhaust elbow can back seawater down into the
cylinders while the engine is stopped. That is how a routine maintenance item
becomes a hydrolocked engine with bent rods.

→ [Exhaust elbow and riser replacement](/services/exhaust-elbow-replacement/) ·
[Marine engine hydrolock](/marine-engine-problems/marine-engine-hydrolock/)

### It corrodes electrochemically

Dissimilar metals in seawater form a battery. Sacrificial anodes are deliberately
made of a metal that corrodes first, protecting everything else. When anodes are
neglected, the corrosion doesn't stop — it moves to the heat exchanger, the
aftercooler, and the engine itself.

→ [Why sacrificial anodes matter](/guides/why-anodes-matter/) ·
[Anode inspection and replacement](/services/anode-replacement/)

### It runs at sustained high load

A car engine spends its life at a fraction of its output. A boat pushing a hull
through water is working hard the entire time it is running. There is no
cruising, no coasting, no overrun. Cooling capacity, fuel delivery and oil
condition all matter more than they would ashore.

### It sits still for months

This is the one owners underestimate most. Fuel degrades in the tank and grows
microbial contamination at the water interface. Seals dry. Impellers take a set.
Batteries sulphate. Then the boat is asked to run hard on the first trip of the
season — which is why most marine engine failures happen in the first hour of
use, not the hundredth.

## The systems, and what goes wrong

**Raw water circuit** — seacock, strainer, impeller pump, heat exchanger,
aftercooler, exhaust elbow. The most common source of marine engine failure in

## Engine types you will meet

- **Shaft drive inboards** — the traditional arrangement; engine, gearbox,
  shaft, propeller.
- **Sterndrives** — engine inboard, drive leg through the transom. Bellows,
  gimbal bearings and universal joints are the wear items.
- **Saildrives** — a vertical leg through the hull on a sailing yacht, with its
  own seal and anode regime.
- **Pod drives** — Volvo Penta IPS and similar.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a marine diesel last?**
A lightly loaded yacht auxiliary can run for decades; a hard-worked charter
engine measures its life in thousands of hours. Hours matter less than how the
engine was cooled, how clean its fuel was, and whether it was used regularly.
→ [How long should a marine diesel last](/guides/marine-diesel-engine-life-expectancy/)

**Is a low-hours engine a good buy?**
Not automatically. Very low hours on an older boat usually means long idle
periods, which cause their own damage — degraded fuel, dried seals, internal
corrosion. An engine used regularly is often in better condition.

**Why is marine work more expensive than automotive?**
Access in a confined engine bay, marine-specification parts, work performed
afloat, and sometimes a haul-out. The labour content of the same nominal job is
simply higher.

**Can my car mechanic service my boat engine?**
No, and you shouldn't want them to. Raw-water cooling, heat exchangers, anodes,
wet exhaust and gearboxes have no automotive equivalent.

**What is the single most important maintenance item?**
The raw water circuit. Impeller on schedule, strainer clear, anodes checked, and
the exhaust elbow inspected before it blocks.
