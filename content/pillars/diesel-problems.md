# Diesel Engine Problems and Symptoms

Start with what your engine is actually doing, not with what you think the part
is called. Below, symptoms are grouped by how they present. Each links to a page
with the likely causes ranked by how often they turn out to be the answer, how a
workshop confirms each one, and what the repair costs.

## Stop driving now if you have any of these

Three faults will destroy an engine in minutes rather than months.

- **[Diesel engine runaway](/diesel-problems/diesel-runaway/)** — the engine
  revs uncontrollably and does not respond to the key, because it has found a
  fuel source other than the injectors. It will not stop on its own.
- **[Low oil pressure warning](/diesel-problems/diesel-low-oil-pressure/)** —
  every second of running on low pressure is bearing damage.
- **[Hydraulic lock](/diesel-problems/diesel-hydraulic-lock/)** — liquid in a
  cylinder. Cranking it bends rods.

Add to that: any overheat where the gauge is in the red, and any sudden loud
mechanical knock. Stop, don't restart, and get it towed.

## Smoke

The colour tells you which system is failing.

- **[White smoke](/diesel-problems/diesel-blowing-white-smoke/)** — unburnt
  fuel or coolant. Cold-start white that clears is usually normal; white that
  persists is not.
- **[Blue smoke](/diesel-problems/diesel-blowing-blue-smoke/)** — burning
  engine oil. Turbo seals, valve stem seals, or worn rings.
- **[Black smoke](/diesel-problems/diesel-blowing-black-smoke/)** — too much
  fuel for the available air. Injectors, air restriction, or boost loss.

## Problems specific to your engine

Some faults are characteristic of particular engines rather than diesels in
general — a known timing chain design, a known injector supplier, a known EGR
cooler failure. If you know your engine, start there instead.

→ [Diesel engine brands in Australia](/engine-brands/)

## A note on diagnosis

Modern diesel faults interact. A blocked DPF causes a boost fault which causes
limp mode which throws a code pointing at the turbo. Replacing the turbo fixes
nothing. This is why a proper diagnosis on a modern diesel takes hours and costs
money, and why the cheapest quote is frequently the most expensive outcome.

Read the symptom page before you authorise a part.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I keep driving with the check engine light on?**
It depends entirely on which fault. Each symptom page answers this near the top,
because it's the question people actually have. If the light is flashing, or
there is a temperature or oil pressure warning alongside it, stop.

**Will a scan tool tell me what's wrong?**
It tells you what the ECU noticed, which is not the same thing. A code is where
diagnosis starts, not where it ends.

**Why do so many modern diesel problems come back to the DPF?**
Because the DPF is the component most dependent on how the vehicle is driven,
and Australian city duty cycles are close to worst-case for it.
→ [How a DPF works](/guides/how-a-dpf-works/)

**Is it worth fixing an old diesel?**
Usually yes on the engine itself, if the rest of the vehicle is sound — diesel
bottom ends are built to be rebuilt.
→ [Rebuild or replace a diesel engine](/guides/rebuild-vs-replace-diesel-engine/)
