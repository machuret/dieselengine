# Buy a marine diesel engine in Broome

Broome has the largest tides in Australia, a cruising ground with almost no
infrastructure, and a supply chain that runs to Perth. Buying an engine here is
an exercise in choosing something you can keep running a long way from help.

## What the local market looks like

The capability sits around Broome's port and the boat harbour, serving the
Kimberley charter fleet, the pearling industry, commercial fishing and the
vessels that support remote operations.

It is a small market with genuinely capable people, because it has to be. What
it does not have is dealer breadth, deep parts holding across many brands, or
the ability to conjure a component overnight.

Perth is around two thousand kilometres away by road. Parts come by road freight
or by air. That is the defining commercial fact of this market.

## Choose what you can support

For a Kimberley-based boat, the priority order is different from anywhere else
in the country:

1. **Parts availability and commonality.** An engine family with a broad parts
   footprint is worth more than one with better numbers.
2. **Who can work on it here**, and whether they need a factory tool.
3. **Mechanical simplicity, where the cost is acceptable.** This is one of the
   few places where the argument against an electronic engine still holds real
   weight for a cruising boat.
4. **Redundancy.** Twin installations, or a serious auxiliary plan.
5. **Spares carried aboard**, treated as part of the purchase rather than an
   afterthought.

## The tides

Broome's tidal range is among the largest in the world — many metres, with
enormous streams to match. Practically:

- **Tidal streams are a real load** and a real passage-planning constraint
- **Slipway and hardstand access is tide-dependent**, and the yard programme
  reflects it
- **Water carries a great deal of suspended sediment**, especially on the
  spring tides, which fouls strainers and heat exchangers
- **Drying moorings and beach careening** are part of local practice, which
  puts a different set of stresses on a hull and drivetrain

## The Kimberley is remote in a way that is easy to underestimate

A boat cruising the Kimberley coast can be days from a workshop and out of
mobile coverage. There are no marinas along most of it and no fuel stops for
long stretches. Engine failure is a serious event.

That argues for conservative choices throughout: adequate power, robust
cooling, generous filtration, redundancy where it can be afforded, and a spares
inventory built with the supplier before departure.

## Heat and the wet

Tropical ambient temperatures and a monsoonal wet season with cyclone risk.
Ventilation, aftercooler capacity and corrosion intervals all follow the
tropical pattern, and the yard calendar follows the season.

## What to check before you commit

- Parts holding in Broome and in Perth for the engine family
- Whether anyone locally can diagnose it without a factory laptop
- Freight lead time from Perth, stated honestly
- Tide-dependent yard access in the programme
- A spares inventory agreed as part of the purchase

## Frequently asked questions

**Where do I buy a marine diesel engine in Broome?**
The port and boat harbour precinct holds the local capability, oriented towards
the charter, pearling and commercial fleets, with Perth as the supply chain.

**How long do parts take to reach Broome?**
Road freight from Perth takes days and air freight costs accordingly. Ask for
honest lead times and plan around them.

**Mechanical or electronic for Kimberley cruising?**
Mechanical simplicity carries real weight here. Weigh it against efficiency and
against who can plug into the engine locally.

**How do Broome's tides affect a repower?**
Slipway and hardstand access is tide-dependent and the yard programme is built
around it. Sediment on spring tides also fouls raw water circuits.

**What spares should I carry for the Kimberley?**
Build the list with the supplier as part of the purchase — impellers, filters,
belts, hoses, and whatever the specific engine is known to need. There is
nowhere to buy them along the coast.
