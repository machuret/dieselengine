# Buy a marine diesel engine in Melbourne

Melbourne is the one major Australian market with a genuine off-season, and
that single fact is worth money to anyone buying an engine here.

## What the local market looks like

The trade is spread around Port Phillip rather than concentrated: Williamstown
and the Yarra for the commercial and larger recreational end, Sandringham and
St Kilda for the metropolitan fleet, Hastings and Western Port on the other
side of the peninsula, and Geelong across the bay.

Dealer coverage is good for the major brands, and there is substantial general
diesel engineering capability in Melbourne that supports the marine trade —
machining, injection service, turbocharger work — which is not true everywhere
in the country.

## Use the winter

Boating on Port Phillip drops off sharply over winter, and a great many boats
are laid up from about May. For an owner buying an engine that is an advantage:

- Yards and workshops have capacity when the fleet is not using its boats
- A repower done over winter costs you no season
- Quotes are more competitive when the diary is not full

An owner who discovers in November that the engine is finished has lost that
advantage. If your engine is marginal, the time to plan is autumn.

## What local conditions do to the choice

**Cold water and a long layup.** Port Phillip is a great deal colder than the
Queensland coast, which is easier on cooling systems and harder on everything
associated with sitting still. Long layup is where lightly used engines
deteriorate: moisture in the oil, condensation in the fuel tank, impellers
taking a set. Read [why low hours is not good news](/guides/low-hours-is-not-good-news/)
before you buy a used engine with a flattering hour figure.

**Anode alloy.** Cold, full-salinity water with boats often on shore power
through winter is a specific galvanic environment. Get the alloy right and
check the wastage rate — see [why anodes matter](/guides/why-anodes-matter/).

**Port Phillip Heads.** Boats that go outside deal with one of the more
demanding entrances in the country. As with Sydney, that argues against
choosing an engine at the bottom of the design power range and for serious fuel
filtration.

**Western Port and the Yarra** are muddier than the open bay, which is a raw
water consideration for boats kept there.

## What to check before you commit

- Whether the yard can hold the boat over winter and at what rate
- Layup preparation as part of the handover, if the new engine will sit
- Whether the quote covers the exhaust and instruments
- Cold-start behaviour and glow or heater arrangements on the engine you choose
- Whether the gearbox is in scope

## Frequently asked questions

**Where do I buy a marine diesel engine in Melbourne?**
Dealer and workshop capability is spread around Port Phillip — Williamstown and
the Yarra, Sandringham, St Kilda, Hastings and Western Port, and Geelong.

**When is the best time to repower in Melbourne?**
Over winter. The fleet is laid up, yards have capacity, and you lose no
boating season.

**Does cold water change the engine I should buy?**
It is easier on cooling than the tropics. The bigger Melbourne factor is long
layup, which is hard on oil, fuel and impellers regardless of engine choice.

**Is a low-hour Melbourne boat a good buy?**
Not automatically. Low hours here often means long periods sitting, which
carries its own deterioration. Look at records and oil analysis, not the meter.

**What should I specify for going outside the Heads?**
Adequate power margin and good primary fuel filtration. The Heads are
unforgiving of an engine chosen at the bottom of the range.
