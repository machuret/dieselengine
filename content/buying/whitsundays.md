# Buy a marine diesel engine in the Whitsundays

The Whitsundays market is unlike anywhere else in Australia because most of the
engines being bought here go into commercial vessels, not private boats.

## What the local market looks like

Airlie Beach and Abell Point are the centre, with Shute Harbour and the
Coral Sea marina area adding capacity. The fleet is dominated by bareboat
charter operators, day-trip and dive boats, and the support craft around them.

The service trade reflects that. The workshops here are used to:

- **Survey requirements** and the documentation that goes with them
- **Fleet purchasing** rather than one-off owner decisions
- **Very high annual hours** on engines that never sit idle
- **Turnaround pressure** — a charter boat out of service is losing money daily

For a private owner buying here, that is mostly good news: the capability is
real. What you will not find is the breadth of dealer choice of a capital city.

## Duty rating is the whole conversation

If the vessel is or will be a domestic commercial vessel, the engine's duty
rating is not a detail:

- **Continuous or heavy-duty ratings** exist for exactly this use
- **A recreational-rated engine** in a charter boat is outside its
  specification, which affects warranty and can affect survey
- **Survey looks at the installation**, not just the engine — fuel shut-offs,
  guarding, exhaust insulation, bilge arrangements

Read [engine requirements for commercial vessel survey](/guides/commercial-vessel-survey-requirements/)
before committing, and speak to your surveyor before the purchase rather than
after.

## What local conditions do to the choice

**Hours accumulate at a commercial rate.** A bareboat in the fleet can put on
more hours in a year than a Sydney cruiser does in a decade. Buying decisions
should be made on cost per hour over the vessel's life, not on purchase price.

**Tropical heat.** Engine room temperature is a genuine constraint, and
ventilation is part of the installation rather than an afterthought.

**Coral, sand and weed.** The raw water side works hard. Strainer access and
heat exchanger accessibility are operational matters when the boat has to turn
around the same day.

**Cyclone season.** From about November to April the operational pattern and
the yard calendar both change. Major work is planned around it.

## Getting it there

Airlie Beach is well served by road from Mackay and Townsville, and engines
arrive by truck routinely. The constraint is more often yard and slipway
capacity than supply, particularly outside the wet season when the fleet is
working.

## What to check before you commit

- The engine's duty rating against your actual operating pattern
- Your surveyor's view, obtained before purchase
- Parts holding in North Queensland for the family you choose
- Slipway or hardstand availability, booked well ahead
- Whether the warranty covers commercial use

## Frequently asked questions

**Where do I buy a marine diesel engine in the Whitsundays?**
Airlie Beach and the Abell Point precinct hold the local dealer and marine
engineering capability, oriented heavily towards the commercial charter fleet.

**What engine rating does a charter boat need?**
A rating appropriate to continuous or heavy commercial duty. Recreational
ratings do not suit charter use and can affect both warranty and survey.

**Should I talk to a surveyor before buying an engine?**
Yes. Changing the propulsion machinery changes the surveyed installation, and
that conversation is far cheaper before the purchase.

**Do high hours matter on a Whitsundays boat?**
They are expected. What matters is the maintenance record and how the hours
were accumulated, not the number alone.

**When should I schedule major engine work here?**
Around the cyclone season and outside peak charter periods, when both the fleet
and the yards have capacity.
