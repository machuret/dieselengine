# Buy a marine diesel engine in Brisbane

Brisbane is the repower capital of the east coast. It has less of the new-boat
glamour of the Gold Coast an hour south, and more of the workshops that take old
engines out and put new ones in.

## What the local market looks like

The trade clusters around the river and the bay: Rivergate and Newstead on the
Brisbane River, Manly at Moreton Bay, and Scarborough on the Redcliffe
peninsula. Between them they hold brand agencies, refit yards and a strong
independent marine engineering trade.

Two things distinguish this market:

**Depth in repower and refit rather than in new boat sales.** If your project is
taking a tired engine out of a fifteen-year-old flybridge and putting a modern
one in, this is a market that does that work constantly and quotes it
confidently.

**Genuine commercial capability.** The river carries workboats, and the
engineering firms that support them can handle heavier installations than a
purely recreational precinct.

You are also close enough to the Coomera precinct that Gold Coast quotes are a
realistic comparison, which is useful leverage that owners further south do not
have.

## What local conditions do to the choice

Moreton Bay is shallow, sandy and, over much of it, turbid. Raw water circuits
here pick up sand. Strainers need clearing more often than owners expect, and
heat exchangers silt.

The Brisbane River adds its own condition: it runs muddy, and after heavy rain
it runs very muddy, with debris. Boats berthed up the river deal with a
markedly dirtier raw water supply than boats in the bay.

If you are choosing an engine and installation, the questions that follow from
this are practical:

- How accessible is the heat exchanger for cleaning?
- Is there room for a properly sized strainer that you can clear underway?
- On a boat used up the river, is keel cooling worth considering? For workboats
  operating in silt it often is — see our comparison of
  [raw water and keel cooling](/guides/raw-water-vs-keel-cooling/).

Sub-tropical heat also means engine room ventilation is worth checking rather
than assuming.

## Flood risk is a buying consideration

The river floods. Boats and yards along it have been affected more than once,
and it shapes both insurance and where owners choose to keep a boat during the
wet season. If you are buying a used engine locally, its flood history is a
legitimate question.

## What to check before you commit

- Whether the quote covers the exhaust, propeller and instruments
- Lift and hardstand availability at the yard, with dates
- For a river-berthed boat, the strainer and heat exchanger access
- Whether the gearbox is included or excluded
- A Gold Coast comparison quote, since it is an hour away

## Frequently asked questions

**Where do I buy a marine diesel engine in Brisbane?**
Rivergate and Newstead on the river, Manly on Moreton Bay, and Scarborough at
Redcliffe hold most of the dealer and marine engineering capability.

**Is Brisbane a good place to repower?**
It has unusual depth in repower and refit work, and it is close enough to the
Coomera precinct that you can get comparable quotes from two markets.

**Does Moreton Bay water affect engine choice?**
It is shallow, sandy and often turbid. Favour an installation with an
accessible heat exchanger and a strainer you can clear easily.

**Should I consider keel cooling on the Brisbane River?**
For a workboat operating in silty river water it is worth genuine
consideration. For a recreational boat in the bay a heat exchanger is normally
adequate.

**Does flood history matter when buying a used engine here?**
Yes. It is a legitimate and important question about any second-hand engine or
boat sourced along the river.
