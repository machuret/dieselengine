# Buy a marine diesel engine in Hervey Bay and Bundaberg

This stretch of coast has two things few other regional markets have: a large
commercial whale watching and charter fleet, and a genuine reputation as the
place cruising boats stop to get work done.

## What the local market looks like

**Hervey Bay** — Urangan Boat Harbour, with the whale watching fleet, charter
operators and a substantial recreational fishing fleet working the Great Sandy
Strait and Platypus Bay.

**Bundaberg** — the Burnett River and Bundaberg Port Marina, which is the
first Australian landfall for a great many international cruising yachts and a
regular stop for coastal cruisers. That has built a service trade used to
transient boats needing work done properly and reasonably quickly.

The result is a regional market with better capability than its population
suggests, and with the useful habit of quoting work for people who cannot wait
around indefinitely.

## The cruising stopover advantage

If you are buying an engine while passing through, this is one of the better
places on the east coast to do it:

- Hardstand and slipway capacity that is not permanently full
- Workshops used to scheduling around a departure date
- Reasonable road freight from Brisbane, a few hours south
- Costs below the south-east Queensland corridor

The constraint is dealer breadth. For an unusual engine family, confirm parts
availability before committing to a timeline.

## What local conditions do to the choice

**The Great Sandy Strait is shallow, sandy and tidal.** Boats work in
skinny water, and raw water intakes pick up sand. This is one of the more
demanding places in Queensland for strainers and heat exchangers.

**The Burnett River** runs muddy, and after rain it runs very muddy. A boat
berthed up the river deals with a substantially dirtier raw water supply than
one in the bay.

**Whale season** — roughly winter into spring — is when the commercial fleet is
flat out and workshop capacity is tightest. Plan major work outside it.

**Sub-tropical heat** without the full intensity of the Far North. Cooling
conditions are moderate, corrosion is brisk.

## Commercial operators

The whale watching and charter fleet operates under survey. If your vessel is
one of them, duty rating and the surveyed installation are the conversation to
have before purchase — see
[engine requirements for survey](/guides/commercial-vessel-survey-requirements/).

## What to check before you commit

- Parts availability for the engine family, particularly if you are transient
- A realistic completion date in writing if you have a departure to make
- Strainer and heat exchanger access, given sandy and muddy water
- Whether the job falls in whale season, when capacity is short
- Whether the quote covers exhaust, propeller and instruments

## Frequently asked questions

**Where do I buy a marine diesel engine in Hervey Bay?**
Urangan Boat Harbour holds the local capability, with Bundaberg Port Marina and
the Burnett River adding a service trade experienced with cruising boats.

**Is Bundaberg a good place to get work done in passage?**
It is one of the better regional stops on the east coast — capacity is
generally available and the trade is used to working to a departure date.

**Does the Great Sandy Strait affect servicing?**
Shallow, sandy water is hard on raw water intakes. Strainer access matters more
here than in deeper water.

**When is workshop capacity tightest?**
Whale season, when the commercial fleet is at full operation.

**Does my whale watching boat need a different engine rating?**
A commercial vessel needs a rating appropriate to its duty, and the installation
forms part of survey. Speak to your surveyor before buying.
