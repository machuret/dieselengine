# Buy a marine diesel engine in Australia

We do not sell engines. This section exists because "buy a marine diesel engine"
is a decision most owners make once or twice in a lifetime, usually under time
pressure, and usually with far less information than the amount of money
involved deserves.

What follows is what to work out before you contact anyone, and then a page for
each major boating region in Australia covering what that market is actually
like — because buying an engine in Broome and buying one on the Gold Coast are
not the same exercise.

## Four ways to buy

**New, through a dealer.** Full warranty, current emissions certification,
factory support and diagnostics. The dealer network is the constraint: some
brands have depth in every capital and some do not.

**Remanufactured on exchange.** You buy a factory-reconditioned unit and return
your old engine as the core. Often the best value on an older but well-supported
engine where the rest of the installation is sound.

**Independent rebuild or reconditioned unit.** Quality varies enormously with
the workshop. Ask for the specification in writing — what was measured,
machined and replaced.

**Used.** Sensible as a core or as a parts source. As a running engine it is a
gamble without real history and an inspection.

Our guide to [new versus reconditioned engines](/guides/new-vs-reconditioned-marine-engine/)
goes through the differences properly, and
[repower or rebuild](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/) covers whether you should be
buying an engine at all.

## The engine is not the whole cost

This is the single most common shock. A repower quote includes removal, engine
beds, alignment, exhaust, wiring, instruments, controls, cooling plumbing,
often the gearbox and often the propeller — and on many boats that work exceeds
the price of the engine.

Read [what a repower costs](/guides/marine-repower-cost-australia/) before you
start comparing engine prices, or you will compare the wrong numbers.

## What to establish before you call anyone

1. **Power required**, from the naval architect's figure or the original
   specification — not from what the neighbouring boat has.
2. **Physical envelope.** Height, length, width, and the route the engine takes
   in and out of the boat.
3. **Gearbox and reduction ratio**, and whether the existing propeller still
   suits.
4. **Duty rating.** Manufacturers rate engines for recreational, light
   commercial or continuous duty. Using an engine outside its rating affects
   warranty and, in survey, compliance.
5. **Whether the vessel is or may become a commercial vessel**, which changes
   the requirements — see
   [engine requirements for survey](/guides/commercial-vessel-survey-requirements/).
6. **Local service and parts support** for the brand where you keep the boat.
   This matters more the further you are from a capital.

## Support beats specification

The brand with the best figures on paper is the wrong choice if the nearest
technician with its diagnostic tool is a thousand kilometres away.

For a boat that stays in a capital city, almost any major brand is well
supported. For a boat based in the Kimberley, on the Gulf, or working remote
sections of the Queensland coast, parts availability and who can plug into the
engine are the deciding factors — not fuel curves.

That is why the regional pages below exist.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I buy a marine diesel engine in Australia?**
Through brand dealers, marine engineering workshops that hold agencies,
remanufacturers offering exchange units, and the used market. Which is right
depends on your engine, your boat and where you keep it.

**Is it cheaper to import a marine engine?**
Sometimes on the engine alone, and it can cost more in the end. Check
certification, parts availability, warranty validity in Australia and who will
service it before comparing prices.

**Do I need the same power as my old engine?**
Not necessarily, but changing it changes the propeller and possibly the
gearbox. Work from the vessel's design figures rather than matching a number.

**Can I fit a different brand?**
Often, subject to the engine beds, the space, the gearbox and the exhaust. It
is an installation question rather than a catalogue one.

**What is the most common mistake?**
Choosing on engine price alone, and discovering that installation, propeller,
exhaust and instruments cost as much again.
