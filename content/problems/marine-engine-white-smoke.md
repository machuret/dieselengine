# Marine engine blowing white smoke

White smoke is either unburnt fuel that has not ignited, or water being turned
to steam. On a marine diesel the second possibility is the one that matters,
because it means seawater or coolant is getting somewhere it should not be.

## Is it safe to keep going?

**Depends which it is, and you should establish that before continuing.**

White smoke on a cold start that clears as the engine warms is usually normal.
White smoke that persists once warm, or that smells sweet, or that comes with
coolant loss — shut down.

## Telling them apart

| | Unburnt fuel | Water or coolant |
|---|---|---|
| When | Cold start, clears when warm | Persists when warm |
| Smell | Raw diesel | Sweet, or odourless steam |
| Coolant level | Unchanged | Dropping |
| Other signs | Rough running cold | Bubbles in header tank, milky oil |

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| Normal cold-start condensation and unburnt fuel | Very common | Clears within a minute or two |
| Water in the fuel | Common | Separator bowl sample |
| Glow plug failure on small engines | Common | Cold starts only, clears when warm |
| Corroded exhaust elbow putting water into a cylinder | Common | Hard starting after sitting, engine hard to turn |
| Perforated heat exchanger or aftercooler | Occasional | Coolant loss, pressure test |
| Head gasket or cracked head | Occasional | Combustion gas test, coolant loss |
| Injection timing or injector fault | Occasional | Specialist test |
| Low compression on a cylinder | Less common | Leak-down test |

## What you can check yourself

- Does it clear once warm? If yes, and the coolant level is steady, it is
  probably normal.
- Drain the water separator and look at what comes out
- Check the coolant level once cool, and look for bubbles in the header tank
- Check the oil for milkiness
- Note whether the engine has been hard to start after sitting

## When to call a mechanic

If it persists when warm, if coolant is disappearing, or if the oil is milky.
Ask for a cooling system pressure test and a combustion gas test before anyone
quotes to remove a head — on a marine engine the heat exchanger and aftercooler
are more common culprits and much cheaper.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is white smoke on a cold start normal?**
Usually yes, if it clears within a minute or two as the engine warms.

**Sweet-smelling white smoke — what is that?**
Coolant being burnt. That means it is entering a cylinder: head gasket, cracked
head, or a perforated cooler.

**White smoke and hard starting after the boat sits — connected?**
Very likely. Both point at water entering a cylinder through the exhaust while
stopped.

**Could it just be water in the fuel?**
Yes, and it is a common and cheap cause. Sample the separator first.
