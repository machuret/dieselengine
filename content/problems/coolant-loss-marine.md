# Losing coolant with no visible leak

A closed cooling circuit that keeps needing topping up, with nothing in the
bilge, is going somewhere specific. On a marine diesel there are four places it
can go, and they differ enormously in cost.

## Is it safe to keep going?

Cautiously, and not far. Topping up and carrying on is how a cheap repair
becomes an expensive one — the underlying fault gets worse and the engine runs
progressively hotter.

Stop immediately if the oil looks milky, if there are bubbles in the header
tank, or if the engine is also overheating.

## Where it is actually going, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it | Relative cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure cap not holding | Common | Cap fails a pressure test; coolant expelled on heat cycles | Trivial |
| **Perforated heat exchanger tube** | **Common** | Pressure test; coolant going overboard with the exhaust, invisible | Moderate |
| Perforated oil cooler | Occasional | Coolant in the oil, or oil in the coolant | Moderate |
| Weeping circulating pump seal | Occasional | Salt or coolant crust at the weep hole | Moderate |
| Hose or clamp seeping under pressure only | Occasional | Pressure test finds it; dry when cold | Minor |
| Head gasket or cracked head | Less common | Combustion gas test positive; bubbles in coolant | Major |

**The second row is the one people miss.** A perforated heat exchanger tube
pushes coolant into the seawater side, where it goes overboard with the exhaust
and is completely invisible. It produces exactly the symptoms of a head gasket
and costs a fraction to fix.

## Test before anyone removes a head

Two tests separate the cheap causes from the expensive one, and both are quick:

- **Cooling system pressure test** — finds caps, hoses, coolers and exchanger
  tubes
- **Combustion gas test on the coolant** — detects combustion gases in the
  cooling system, which is what a head gasket actually does

Insist on both before authorising head removal. On a boat, head access is often
poor and the job is expensive. Ruling out a pressure cap — one of the cheapest
parts on the engine — before authorising that is not excessive caution.

## What you can check yourself

Once cool: coolant level and its condition, the pressure cap's rubber seal, and
the pump weep hole for crust. Check whether the coolant is actually disappearing
or just moving to an overflow bottle — that is a surprisingly common false alarm.

Never open a hot pressure cap.

## When to call a mechanic

Once you have confirmed real loss. Milky oil, bubbles in the header tank, or
white smoke means call now rather than after another top-up.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can coolant go if there is nothing in the bilge?**
Overboard through the exhaust via a perforated heat exchanger tube, into the oil
via a failed cooler, into the cylinders via a head gasket, or out past a failed
pressure cap on heat cycles.

**Is it always a head gasket?**
No, and assuming so is expensive. On a marine engine the heat exchanger is a
more common culprit and much cheaper.

**Can I keep topping it up?**
Only to get somewhere safe. The fault worsens and the engine runs hotter each
time.

**Does coolant matter if it never freezes here?**
Yes. Its job in Australia is corrosion protection. Diluting it with water lets
the engine corrode internally.
