# Hard starting after layup

The first start of the season is when a layup's damage shows up. Nothing was
wrong when the boat was parked; the damage happened while it sat.

## Is it safe to keep going?

Getting it started is fine. **Getting it started and then going straight out is
not.** Everything that made it hard to start also makes it likely to fail under
load an hour offshore.

Run it at the berth, watch temperature and exhaust flow, then sea trial before
you rely on it.

→ [Recommissioning after layup](/services/recommissioning-service/)

## One thing to establish first

If the engine is **hard to turn** rather than just reluctant to fire, stop.
Water can siphon into a cylinder through the exhaust while a boat sits,
especially with a marginal exhaust installation. Cranking against it bends rods.

→ [Marine engine hydrolock](/marine-engine-problems/marine-engine-hydrolock/)

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| Flat or sulphated batteries | Very common | Load test |
| Degraded fuel, or water in the tank | Very common | Tank bottom sample |
| Blocked filters from stirred-up sludge | Very common | Filter bowl |
| Air drawn in through a dried seal | Common | Bleeding fixes it |
| Impeller set hard against the cam | Common | No water at exhaust after start |
| Corroded connections | Common | Voltage drop |
| Rodent damage to wiring | Occasional | Visible in the engine bay |
| Water in a cylinder via the exhaust | Occasional | Engine hard to turn |

**Fuel and batteries account for most of it.** Both degrade with time rather
than use, which is exactly what a layup provides.

→ [Diesel bug in the fuel tank](/marine-engine-problems/diesel-bug-symptoms/) ·
[Fuel polishing and tank cleaning](/services/fuel-polishing/)

## What you can check yourself

Before turning the key at all:

- **Every bung, plug and seal fitted during layup is out** — especially the
  exhaust. Running an engine with a blocked exhaust does immediate damage.
- Seacocks open
- Batteries load tested, not just voltage checked
- Fuel filters changed and the separator drained
- Belts and hoses checked for perishing
- Engine bay checked for rodent damage

Then start, and confirm water at the exhaust within seconds.

## When to call a mechanic

If the tank has sat untreated, if the engine is hard to turn, or if it starts
badly and runs rough. A recommissioning service is cheaper than the tow that
follows skipping it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does this happen every season?**
Because the causes are time-based, not use-based. Proper layup in autumn
prevents most of it.

**Should I have laid the boat up differently?**
Full treated tank, clean oil in before it sits, batteries on a maintainer and
the impeller relieved covers most of it.

**My engine started but overheated straight away — why?**
The impeller took a set, or the strainer or seacock is blocked or closed. Shut
down.

**Is it worth paying for a recommissioning service?**
For a boat that sat months, yes. It finds the failures at the dock instead of at
the heads.
