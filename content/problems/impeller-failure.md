# Raw water impeller has failed

The impeller is a rubber-bladed rotor that pumps seawater through your engine.
It is a wear item, it fails without warning, and when it goes the engine loses
its cooling immediately.

## Is it safe to keep going?

**No.** Without the impeller there is no seawater flow, and a marine diesel
without cooling water damages itself in minutes. The exhaust hose can also melt.
Shut down.

## How you know it has failed

- No water at the exhaust, or a sharp drop in flow
- Temperature climbing, or the overheat alarm
- Blades found broken off when the pump cover comes off
- Blades hardened, cracked, or taking a permanent set from sitting
- On a boat that has just been launched or run on the hard: almost certainly this

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Run dry — closed seacock, blocked strainer, run on the hard | Very common | Blades melted, glazed or shredded after seconds |
| Normal wear | Very common | Cracked, hardened blades; some broken off |
| Taking a set over a layup | Common | Blades bent to one side, pumps poorly on the first start |
| Age, even unused | Common | Rubber hardened and cracked with no hours on it |
| Debris — sand, silt, shell | Common in shallow water | Abraded blades, scored wear plate |
| Worn cam or wear plate | Occasional | New impellers keep failing early |

## The part that matters most

**Every blade must be accounted for.**

When blades break off they travel downstream into the heat exchanger inlet, the
oil cooler and the exhaust elbow. Fit a new impeller without finding them and
the engine overheats again within hours — and the new impeller gets the blame.

Count the blades on the old one. If your impeller has six and only five come
out, the sixth is in your cooling system, and the job is not finished until it
is found. That usually means opening the heat exchanger end cap.

This is the single best question to ask a mechanic about this job.

## What you can check yourself

On many engines this is a job an owner can do, and it is the most worthwhile one
to learn. Close the seacock first. Inspect the wear plate and cam for grooving
while the cover is off — a scored housing eats new impellers. Replace the cover
gasket or O-ring; do not reuse it.

## When to call a mechanic

If blades are missing and you cannot access the heat exchanger, or if the engine
overheated before you shut down, or if new impellers keep failing — that last
one means the pump housing itself is worn.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should the impeller be replaced?**
On schedule rather than on failure. Interval depends on water conditions and how
long the boat sits as much as on hours — ask a local operator what they use.

**My impeller looks fine — do I still replace it?**
If it is at its interval or more than a few years old, yes. Rubber hardens with
age even unused, and a set impeller pumps poorly without looking damaged.

**What happens if I leave a broken blade in the system?**
It restricts flow downstream, usually at the heat exchanger inlet, and the
engine overheats again quickly.

**I ran the engine with the seacock closed — is the impeller gone?**
Almost certainly, even if only for seconds. Replace it rather than hoping.

**Why did a brand new impeller fail?**
Usually a worn cam or wear plate in the pump housing, or debris still in the
system from the last failure.
