# Marine engine blowing black smoke

Black smoke is unburnt fuel. The engine is being given more fuel than it can
burn with the air available — so either it is getting too much fuel, not enough
air, or it is being asked to do more work than it can.

On a boat, the third one is the usual answer.

## Is it safe to keep going?

Briefly, at reduced load. An engine smoking black is running hot and wasting
fuel, and if the cause is overload it is wearing itself out. Reduce throttle and
see whether the smoke clears — that alone tells you a lot.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Overloaded engine — fouled bottom** | **Very common** | Will not reach rated WOT RPM | Antifoul |
| Over-pitched or damaged propeller | Very common | Same symptom, haul-out inspection | Repitch |
| Blocked exhaust elbow | Common | Back pressure, power loss, soot on transom | Replace |
| Dirty air filter or restricted intake | Common | Inspection | Clean |
| Turbo or aftercooler fault | Common | Low boost | Repair |
| Worn or leaking injectors | Occasional | Bench test | Injectors |
| Injection timing or pump fault | Occasional | Specialist test | Specialist |
| Excess load — overloaded vessel, towing | Occasional | Obvious in context | Free |

**Rule out overload before touching the fuel system.** A boat with a dirty
bottom or the wrong prop smokes black and cannot reach revs, and no amount of
injector work fixes it. This is the most common expensive mistake made on this
symptom.

## What you can check yourself

- When was the bottom last antifouled, and what does it look like?
- Does the engine reach its rated WOT RPM? If not, that is your answer.
- Air filter condition and intake clear
- Has the propeller been changed or damaged?
- Does the smoke clear at reduced load, or is it there at idle too?

Black smoke **only under load** points at overload or air restriction. Black
smoke **at idle as well** points more toward injection.

## When to call a mechanic

Once overload and air restriction are ruled out. Ask for injectors to be tested
rather than replaced on suspicion.

## Frequently asked questions

**A little black smoke on acceleration — normal?**
A brief puff as load comes on is normal on many diesels. Continuous black smoke
at cruise is not.

**Will new injectors fix it?**
Only if the injectors are the cause, which on a boat they usually are not. Check
the bottom and the prop first.

**Why does it smoke more with a dirty bottom?**
Drag means the engine must produce more power for the same speed. Past a point
it cannot burn the extra fuel cleanly.

**Is black smoke damaging?**
The smoke is a symptom; the overload behind it is what does the damage — heat,
cylinder pressure and accelerated wear.
