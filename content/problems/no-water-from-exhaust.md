# No water coming out of the exhaust

Your engine is raw-water cooled. Seawater is drawn in through the hull, pumped
through the engine, and discharged with the exhaust. If water has stopped coming
out of the transom, the engine has stopped being cooled — and a marine diesel
with no cooling water destroys itself in minutes, not hours.

## Shut down now

**Stop the engine.** This is not a fault to investigate while running.

Beyond the engine damage, the exhaust hose on most installations is rubber, kept
alive only by the water flowing through it. Without that water it can melt or
burn through, which puts hot exhaust gas into the engine bay and opens a hole
below or near the waterline. Overheating here is a fire and flooding risk, not
only a mechanical one.

If you are under way and this is your only engine, anchor if you can before you
begin diagnosing.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blocked raw water strainer | Very common | Clear bowl full of weed, plastic or growth | Clear it — minutes |
| Seacock closed | Common | Handle not aligned with the hose | Open it, then inspect the impeller |
| Failed impeller | Very common | Blades missing or set hard | Replace; recover every fragment |
| Blocked intake or scoop | Common | Strainer clear but no flow when hose cracked open | Clear the skin fitting |
| Collapsed suction hose | Occasional | Soft hose, flow fine at idle only | Replace hose |
| Blocked heat exchanger | Occasional | Flow drops progressively over weeks | Service the stack |
| Pump shaft or drive failure | Less common | Pump turns but no flow; salt weep at the seal | Rebuild or replace pump |
| Blocked exhaust elbow | Less common | Back pressure, soot, power loss beforehand | Replace elbow |

**Start at the top of that list.** The first two are free and account for a
large share of cases. The most expensive possibilities are also the least
likely.

## What you can check yourself

With the engine **off**:

- **The seacock.** Is it open? A closed seacock is the single most common cause
  after a trip on the hard, and running even briefly against it destroys the
  impeller.
- **The strainer.** Close the seacock first, open the strainer, clear it, close
  it, reopen the seacock. Weed, plastic bags and shell are the usual finds.
- **The impeller.** If you carry a spare and know the engine, this is the most
  worthwhile repair to be able to do yourself. **Account for every blade** — a
  missing one is downstream in the heat exchanger and will cause the same
  problem again within hours.

Do not run the engine to "see if it clears". Every second without water is
damage.

## When to call a mechanic

If the strainer, seacock and impeller are all good and there is still no flow,
stop. The remaining causes — a blocked exchanger, a failed pump, a collapsed
hose, a blocked elbow — need someone who can pressure-test the circuit rather
than guess at it.

Call immediately, rather than after another attempt, if the engine reached
operating temperature or the alarm sounded while running dry.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I motor a short distance without cooling water?**
No. Damage begins within a minute or two, and the exhaust hose can melt. Anchor
or get a tow.

**How long can a marine diesel run dry?**
Impellers are damaged almost immediately. Serious engine damage follows within
minutes. Treat it as zero.

**Water was coming out and then stopped — what changed?**
Most often the strainer blocked with something the boat passed through, or the
impeller finally shed its blades. Both are found in the first ten minutes.

**There is water but much less than usual — same problem?**
Yes, earlier in its progression. Reduced flow is the warning; act on it now
rather than after it stops entirely.

**Should I carry a spare impeller?**
Yes, with the gaskets and the tools. It converts the most likely breakdown you
will have into an inconvenience.
