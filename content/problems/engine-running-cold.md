# Marine engine running too cold

An engine that never reaches operating temperature is not a harmless quirk. It
is a fault with a real cost, and because nothing alarms and nothing overheats,
it usually runs for years before anyone addresses it.

## Is it safe to keep going?

Yes, in the short term — this will not strand you. But it is damaging the engine
continuously, and the damage is cumulative and permanent.

## What running cold actually does

**Glazes the bores.** Piston rings need heat and cylinder pressure to bed and to
seal. An engine that never warms properly glazes its bore surfaces, and the
result is permanent oil consumption and blow-by that only a rebuild fixes.

**Builds carbon.** Cold combustion is incomplete combustion. Carbon accumulates
on valves, in the exhaust and in the elbow, accelerating the blockage that causes
overheating later.

**Dilutes the oil with fuel.** Unburnt fuel washes past the rings into the sump,
thinning the oil and reducing its ability to protect bearings.

**Corrodes internally.** Combustion produces water. An engine that reaches
temperature boils it off; one that does not leaves acidic condensate in the
crankcase.

This is why "it only ever idles in and out of the marina" is a maintenance
problem, not a boast about low hours.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| Thermostat stuck open or missing | Very common | Temperature never rises past cool, even under load |
| Wrong thermostat fitted | Common | Opens at too low a temperature |
| Thermostat removed by a previous owner | Common | Often done "to fix overheating" — it fixes nothing |
| Only ever run at idle or very light load | Very common | Temperature normal under load, low in use |
| Faulty sender or gauge | Common | Engine feels normal; reading does not match |

That third row is worth calling out. Removing a thermostat to cure overheating
treats the gauge, not the cause, and creates this problem in exchange.

## What you can check yourself

Run the engine under genuine load — not at idle — and watch whether temperature
reaches the normal band. Check the oil for a fuel smell or an unusually thin
feel. Confirm the thermostat is actually fitted, and to the right rating.

## When to call a mechanic

If a new thermostat does not fix it, or if the oil smells of fuel, or if the
engine has been running cold for years — in which case ask for a compression or
leak-down test to find out whether the bores are already glazed.

## What diagnosis costs

A thermostat is one of the cheapest repairs on the engine. The consequences of

## Frequently asked questions

**Is running cold actually a problem?**
Yes. It glazes bores, builds carbon, dilutes the oil with fuel and lets the
engine corrode internally. The damage is slow and permanent.

**Someone removed my thermostat to stop it overheating — is that ok?**
No. It masks the real cause and creates a running-cold problem. Fix the cooling
fault and refit the correct thermostat.

**My engine only idles in and out of the marina — does that matter?**
It is the same problem. Run it under genuine load periodically; that is
maintenance, not wear.

**How do I know if the bores are already glazed?**
A compression or leak-down test, and excessive blow-by is the giveaway.
