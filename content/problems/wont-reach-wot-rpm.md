# Won't reach wide open throttle RPM

Every marine engine has a rated maximum RPM. Reaching it at full throttle under
normal load means the engine and propeller are correctly matched. Falling short
means the engine is overloaded — and a chronically overloaded diesel runs hot,
smokes and wears out early.

This is the single most informative measurement on a boat, and most owners have
never taken it.

## Is it safe to keep going?

Yes, but not indefinitely. An overloaded engine runs at higher exhaust
temperature and cylinder pressure than it was designed for, and the cost arrives
as bores, rings and turbos rather than as a breakdown.

## Likely causes, ranked — cheapest first

| Cause | How common | Confirms it | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fouled bottom** | **Very common** | When was it last antifouled? | Antifoul |
| Fouled or damaged propeller | Very common | Haul-out inspection | Clean or repair |
| Over-pitched propeller | Common | Never reached rated RPM since a prop change | Repitch |
| Excess weight or trim | Common | Loaded differently than usual | Free |
| Blocked exhaust elbow | Common | Back pressure, soot | Replace elbow |
| Fuel starvation — filters, vent, air | Common | Filter condition, vacuum gauge | Filters |
| Dirty air filter or restricted intake | Occasional | Inspection | Clean |
| Turbo or aftercooler fault | Occasional | Boost measurement | Repair |
| Injection fault | Occasional | Bench test | Specialist |
| Slipping gearbox | Occasional | Revs rise without speed | Gearbox |
| Worn engine, low compression | Less common | Leak-down test | Major |

**Check the bottom first.** It costs nothing to consider and it is the answer
more often than everything below it combined. A workshop that quotes injectors
without asking when the boat was last antifouled is guessing.

## What you can check yourself

- When was the bottom last cleaned? What does it look like?
- Has the propeller been changed, repaired or damaged?
- Is the boat loaded unusually?
- What do the fuel filters look like, and is there a vacuum gauge reading?
- Does the exhaust smoke black at full throttle? That is an overloaded engine.

Take the measurement properly: full throttle, normal load, long enough to
stabilise, on a clean bottom. Record it — it is the baseline for every future
decision.

## When to call a mechanic

If the bottom is clean, the prop is undamaged and it still will not reach rated
RPM. Bring the actual number and the conditions you measured it in.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should my engine's WOT RPM be?**
The engine maker's rated figure, on its plate or in the manual. Reaching it
under normal load is the target.

**Is running over rated RPM a problem?**
It means under-propped — the engine cannot deliver its designed power and the
boat is slower than it should be. Less damaging than overloaded, still wrong.

**How much difference does a fouled bottom make?**
A great deal. It is routinely the entire explanation.

**I repowered and now it will not reach revs — why?**
Almost certainly the propeller. A carried-over prop rarely suits a new engine's
power and RPM.
