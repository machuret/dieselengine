# Excessive blow-by

Blow-by is combustion gas escaping past the piston rings into the crankcase.
Every engine has some. When it becomes obvious — vapour or oil mist from the
breather or the oil filler — the rings are no longer sealing properly.

## Is it safe to keep going?

Usually, in the short term. Blow-by does not strand you; it tells you the engine
is worn or glazed and heading toward a rebuild.

Stop if it comes with low oil pressure, or if oil consumption is high enough to
run the engine low.

## How to judge it

With the engine warm and running, remove the oil filler cap:

- A light haze or slight puffing is normal
- Steady vapour is significant
- Pulsing that will push the cap off is severe
- Oil mist being blown out is severe

A leak-down or compression test quantifies it properly and identifies whether
one cylinder is responsible or all of them.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Glazed bores from prolonged light-load running** | **Very common on boats** | A low-hours failure mode |
| Worn piston rings | Very common | High-hour engines |
| Worn bores | Common | Often with the above |
| Blocked crankcase breather | Common | Cheap; check first |
| Broken ring or damaged piston | Occasional | Usually one cylinder |
| Turbo seal leaking into the intake | Occasional | Mimics blow-by |

**Check the breather before anything else.** A blocked crankcase breather
pressurises the crankcase and produces symptoms identical to worn rings, for a
fraction of the cost. It is the cheap thing that gets missed.

**The first row is the marine-specific one.** An auxiliary that only ever idles
in and out of a berth never reaches the load and heat the rings need to seal.
The bores glaze, the rings stop sealing, and the engine has high blow-by at low
hours. It has not worn out — it has never been worked.

## What you can check yourself

- The breather — blocked, or full of oil?
- Oil consumption rate, tracked over a few trips
- Whether the engine has spent its life at light load
- Blue smoke under load

## When to call a mechanic

Once the breather is ruled out. Ask for a leak-down test rather than a straight
compression test — it identifies whether gas is escaping past rings, valves or
a gasket, which decides the size of the job.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much blow-by is normal?**
A light haze from the filler is normal. Steady vapour, pulsing, or oil mist is
not.

**Can glazed bores be fixed without a rebuild?**
Sometimes a controlled hard run under load will re-bed marginal rings, but
established glazing usually needs the bores honed and rings replaced.

**My engine has very low hours — how can it have blow-by?**
Light-load running glazes bores. Hours measure use, not the kind of use.

**Is a compression test enough?**
A leak-down test tells you more: where the gas is going, which decides whether
it is rings, valves or a gasket.
