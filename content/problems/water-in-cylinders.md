# Water in the cylinders

Water above a piston is the fault that turns a consumable part into a rebuild —
but only if the engine gets turned over against it.

## Do not crank the engine

Water does not compress. Cranking bends connecting rods and can crack a piston.

An engine caught before anyone tried to start it is usually recoverable. The
same engine after several start attempts usually is not. If it will not turn,
stop and find out why.

→ [Marine engine hydrolock](/marine-engine-problems/marine-engine-hydrolock/)

## How you know

- The engine will not turn, or turns partway and stops solid
- Water or steam from the exhaust on a start attempt
- Hard starting after the boat has sat, then rough running that clears
- Water on the dipstick
- One cylinder with no compression
- Rust on a bore seen through the injector hole

That third symptom is the early warning. An engine that is reluctant after
sitting, then runs fine, is often taking a small amount of water in each time it
stops — and the amount grows.

## How it gets there

| Route | How common |
|---|---|
| Corroded exhaust elbow letting seawater into the exhaust passage | Very common |
| Exhaust riser too low or an inadequate loop, water siphoning back at rest | Common |
| Cranking a non-starting engine, pumping raw water into the exhaust | Common |
| Following sea forcing water up the exhaust while stopped | Common |
| Perforated aftercooler feeding water into the intake | Occasional |
| Head gasket, cracked head or liner seal | Occasional |

**The geometry matters as much as the parts.** If your engine has ingested water
once, have the exhaust installation checked — riser height, loop, and where the
waterlift sits — not just the elbow replaced. A marginal installation will do it
again.

→ [Blocked or corroded exhaust elbow](/marine-engine-problems/blocked-exhaust-elbow/)

## What to do

Remove the injectors or glow plugs, turn the engine by hand to expel water
through the open ports, fill the cylinders with oil to stop the bores rusting,
change the oil repeatedly, and get a mechanic. Bare cylinder bores rust within
hours in salt air.

## Frequently asked questions

**The engine is hard to start after sitting, then runs fine — is that this?**
It can be, and it is the early stage. Have the exhaust elbow inspected before it
becomes a locked engine.

**Can I blow the water out by cranking?**
No. That is the action that bends rods. Open the cylinders first.

**How much water does it take to cause damage?**
Very little. A cylinder full is not required; enough to exceed the clearance at
top dead centre is.

**Why does it keep happening?**
The exhaust system. Replacing the elbow without correcting riser height or loop
geometry leaves the same path open.
