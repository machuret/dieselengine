# Propeller shaft vibration

Vibration through the hull that rises with shaft speed is coming from the
driveline, not the engine. It is worth chasing: vibration destroys cutless
bearings, shaft seals and gearbox output bearings, and it usually gets worse.

## Is it safe to keep going?

At reduced speed, usually. Severe vibration that started suddenly — after a
grounding or a rope strike — should be treated as a damaged shaft or propeller
and taken slowly.

Stop if it comes with water ingress at the stern gland, or with a noise that
changes.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it | Needs haul-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fouled propeller | Very common | Growth or debris on the blades | Yes (or a dive) |
| Damaged or bent propeller blade | Very common | Inspection | Yes |
| Rope or line around the shaft | Common | Inspection | Sometimes |
| Worn cutless bearing | Common | Shaft moves vertically in the bearing | Yes |
| Engine and shaft misalignment | Common | Alignment check afloat | No |
| Sagging or perished engine mounts | Common | Excess engine movement | No |
| Bent shaft | Occasional | Runout measured on rollers | Yes |
| Unbalanced propeller after a repair | Occasional | Balance check | Yes |
| Worn gearbox output bearing | Occasional | Play at the coupling | No |

**The first three are cheap and account for most of it.** A rope strike or a
season's growth on the blades produces immediate, obvious vibration. Check
before assuming anything structural.

## Vibration or misfire?

If it changes with **shaft speed** — appears at a particular speed, worsens with
throttle in gear, disappears in neutral — it is the driveline.

If it changes with **engine speed** regardless of gear, it is the engine.

→ [Rough idle or uneven running](/marine-engine-problems/marine-rough-idle/)

## Alignment must be checked afloat

Hull shape changes between a hard stand and the water. An alignment set on the
hard is not the alignment the boat has floating, and a yard that offers to align
during a haul-out should be asked how they handle that. Correct practice is
afloat, after the boat has settled.

→ [Engine alignment and coupling](/services/engine-alignment/)

## What you can check yourself

- When was the bottom last cleaned? Has anything been around the prop?
- Engine mounts for sag, cracking and movement under load
- Coupling bolts tight
- Whether the vibration appeared suddenly or built up gradually

## When to call a mechanic

If it appeared suddenly, if there is play in the shaft at the cutless bearing,
or if the stern gland has started leaking or running hot — those are consequences
of vibration and they compound.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it the prop or the alignment?**
Both feel similar. A haul-out inspection settles it, and both are usually checked
at the same time because the access is shared.

**It started after I hit something — what now?**
Assume a bent shaft or damaged blade and run slowly. Get it inspected at the next
haul-out, sooner if it is severe.

**Can vibration damage anything?**
Yes — cutless bearing, shaft seal, gearbox output bearing and the shaft itself.
It compounds.

**Why does alignment need doing in the water?**
The hull changes shape between the hard stand and floating. Alignment set on the
hard drifts once the boat is back in.
