# Fuel filters keep blocking

Changing a filter and having the new one block shortly after is not a filter
problem. It is a tank problem, and the filter is doing exactly its job —
catching what is coming out of the tank.

## Is it safe to keep going?

You can usually get home on a fresh filter. But a tank producing this much
contamination will block filters again, and it always seems to happen in rough
weather when motion stirs the tank up.

Carry spares and know how to bleed the system.

## What is actually coming out of the tank

Look at what the filter caught — it names the cause.

| What you see | What it is | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dark slime, jelly, stringy mats | **Microbial growth (diesel bug)** | Biocide, then polish or clean the tank |
| Free water below the fuel | Condensation, deck fill or vent leak, bad fuel | Remove water, fix the ingress |
| Black or rust-coloured particulate | Tank sediment or internal corrosion | Tank clean |
| Fine dark powder | Asphaltene from aged fuel | Polish, replace fuel |
| Only blocks in rough weather | Settled sludge stirred off the tank floor | Tank clean |

That last row is the giveaway most owners recognise: perfect in flat water,
filters die in a seaway. The contamination has been sitting undisturbed on the
tank bottom the whole time.

## Why marine tanks do this

Boats have large tanks that sit part-full for long periods in humid conditions.
Air space condenses moisture; water settles under the fuel; microbes live at the
interface and feed on the fuel. Then motion suspends the lot.

Cars do not do this because their tanks empty and refill weekly.

## What you can check yourself

- Take a sample from the tank bottom — a drain valve, or through the inspection
  port if there is one
- Drain the water separator and inspect the bowl
- Check the deck fill seal and the tank vent for water ingress
- Fit a vacuum gauge to the primary filter if there is not one; it warns you
  before a filter blocks completely

## When to call a mechanic

When you have confirmed contamination. Polishing handles the fuel; if there is
established growth on the walls and baffles, the tank needs opening and
cleaning, and polishing alone gives a temporary result.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I fit a finer filter to catch more?**
No. A primary finer than the engine maker specifies restricts flow and starves
the engine at high revs. Fix the tank instead.

**Why only in rough weather?**
Motion lifts settled sludge off the tank floor and sends it to the filters.
Calm-water running never disturbs it.

**Will a biocide fix it?**
It kills the organisms but leaves the dead biomass and the water — and that dead
material blocks filters too. Treatment plus polishing is the complete job.

**How do I stop it coming back?**
Keep water out: full tanks over layup, a sound deck fill and vent, and routine
separator draining.
