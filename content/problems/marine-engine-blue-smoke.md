# Marine engine blowing blue smoke

Blue smoke is burning engine oil. Something is letting oil into the combustion
chamber, and on a marine diesel the possibilities range from a turbo seal to a
worn-out engine.

## Is it safe to keep going?

Usually yes in the short term, while watching the oil level closely — an engine
that burns oil can run itself low. But blue smoke does not fix itself, and the
underlying cause generally gets worse.

Stop if oil consumption is rapid, or if it comes with low oil pressure.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turbo seal failure | Common | Oil in the intake or aftercooler; whine change | Turbo |
| Glazed bores from prolonged light-load running | Common | Blow-by, poor compression, low-hours engine | Rebuild |
| Worn piston rings | Common | Blow-by, compression test | Rebuild |
| Valve stem seals | Occasional | Blue puff on start, clears | Head work |
| Overfilled sump | Occasional | Dipstick above max | Free |
| Crankcase breather blocked or oil carryover | Occasional | Oil in the intake tract | Cheap |
| Worn valve guides | Occasional | Head inspection | Head work |

**The second row deserves attention on a boat.** An auxiliary that only ever
idles in and out of a berth never reaches proper load, and bores glaze. Rings
then cannot seal, oil passes, and the engine burns it. This is a low-hours
failure mode — the engine has not worn out, it has never been worked.

## What you can check yourself

- Oil level — is it overfilled? Is it dropping, and how fast?
- When does it smoke: on start, at idle, under load, or on deceleration?
- Is there oil in the intake pipework or the aftercooler? That points at the
  turbo or the breather.
- Is there excessive blow-by from the breather or oil filler?

**Blue on start-up only** points at valve stem seals. **Blue under load** points
at rings or turbo. **Blue all the time** with blow-by points at engine condition.

## When to call a mechanic

Once you have observed when it smokes. Ask for a compression or leak-down test
before committing to anything major — it separates a turbo job from a rebuild.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much oil consumption is normal?**
Some is normal and varies by engine. A sudden change in the rate matters more
than the absolute figure — track it.

**Blue smoke only when I start up — serious?**
Usually valve stem seals. Less serious than rings, and a smaller job.

**Can a turbo cause blue smoke?**
Yes, and it is worth ruling out first — a turbo rebuild is far cheaper than an
engine rebuild.

**My engine has very low hours but burns oil — how?**
Prolonged light-load running glazes the bores. The engine has not worn out; it
has never been worked properly.
