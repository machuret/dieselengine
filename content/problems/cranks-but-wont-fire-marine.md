# Cranks but won't fire

The engine turns at normal speed and never catches. On a diesel that means one
of three things is missing: fuel, air, or compression — and on a boat it is
almost always fuel.

## Before you keep cranking

On a raw-water-cooled engine the pump runs whenever the engine turns. Prolonged
cranking pumps seawater into the exhaust, where it can back up into a cylinder
and hydrolock the engine.

**Crank in short attempts with diagnosis in between**, not continuously.

→ [Marine engine hydrolock](/marine-engine-problems/marine-engine-hydrolock/)

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| Air in the fuel system | Very common | Bleeding restores starting |
| Blocked fuel filters | Very common | Filter bowl full of sludge or water |
| Fuel valve closed, or empty tank | Common | Look before assuming |
| Blocked tank vent | Common | Opening the filler cap changes things |
| Air leak on the suction side | Common | Perished separator bowl seal, loose fitting |
| Stop solenoid or cable stuck | Common | Fuel never reaches the injectors |
| Failed lift pump | Occasional | No fuel arriving at the filter |
| Water or contamination in the fuel | Occasional | Tank bottom sample |
| Glow plug failure (small engines, cold) | Occasional | Only in the cold |
| Injection pump or timing fault | Less common | Specialist territory |
| Low compression | Less common | Leak-down test |

**Air is the answer far more often than anything expensive.** A perished seal on
a filter bowl, or a filter change that was not bled properly afterwards,
accounts for a large share of these.

## What you can check yourself

- Fuel in the tank, and the fuel valve open
- The stop control fully returned — a stuck stop solenoid is a classic
- Open the filler cap and try again: if it fires, the tank vent is blocked
- Drain the water separator and look at what comes out
- Change the primary and secondary filters, then **bleed the system properly**
- Check the separator bowl seal and every fitting on the suction side for air

Knowing how to bleed your particular fuel system before you need to is the most
useful thing you can learn about your engine.

## When to call a mechanic

If bleeding and fresh filters do not fix it. Beyond that point you are into lift
pump, injection pump, injectors and compression, which need proper testing.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I bleed a marine diesel fuel system?**
It differs by engine — many modern engines self-bleed, older ones need bleed
screws opened in a set order. Find out for yours before you need it.

**It fired then died — what does that mean?**
Usually air, or fuel starvation. It ran on what was in the lines and then found
nothing behind it.

**It starts if I open the fuel filler cap — why?**
Blocked tank vent. The engine was drawing against a vacuum. Cheap fix.

**How long can I crank before doing damage?**
Short attempts only. Beyond the starter and battery, cranking pumps water into
the exhaust on a raw-water-cooled engine.
