# Saltwater in the engine oil

Seawater in the sump is the most time-critical fault on this site. Damage is
measured in hours, and what you do in the first day decides whether this is an
oil change or a rebuild.

## Is it safe to keep going?

**No. Do not run the engine.**

Salt water in oil destroys bearings continuously while it sits there. Running
the engine circulates it through every bearing surface under load. Every hour
counts, including the hours it sits at a berth waiting for someone.

## How you know

- Oil on the dipstick looks milky, grey or like coffee with milk
- Oil level has risen
- A gritty or salty feel rubbing the oil between your fingers
- Water droplets in the oil filler or on the underside of the cap
- Low oil pressure

Milky oil can also mean fresh coolant rather than seawater — a different and
less urgent problem. An oil sample tested for sodium and chloride distinguishes
them definitively.

## Where it came from, ranked

| Route | How common |
|---|---|
| Corroded or blocked exhaust elbow, water back into a cylinder | Very common |
| Perforated seawater-cooled oil cooler | Common |
| Perforated aftercooler on a turbocharged engine | Common |
| Perforated heat exchanger tube | Occasional |
| Head gasket, cracked head or liner seal | Occasional |
| Swamping, or water down the exhaust in a following sea | Occasional |

The cause must be found. A fresh oil fill on an unrepaired cause is contaminated
again on the next run.

## What to do now

1. **Do not start it.**
2. **Drain the oil immediately** — do not wait for a mechanic to be available.
   Getting the salt water out is the single most valuable thing you can do.
3. **Refill with cheap oil of the right grade, turn the engine by hand, drain
   again.** Repeat. You are flushing, not servicing.
4. **Leave it full of clean oil** rather than empty, so surfaces are not bare.
5. **Get a mechanic**, and tell them how long it sat and whether it was run.

→ [Saltwater ingress and hydrolock repair](/services/saltwater-damage-repair/)

## When to call a mechanic

Immediately. While you wait, do the flushing above — it is the difference
between recoverable and not.

## Frequently asked questions

**How quickly do I need to act?**
Immediately. Salt water corrodes bearings the whole time it sits. Hours matter,
not days.

**Can I just change the oil and carry on?**
Only as flushing, and only alongside finding the cause. Otherwise it comes back
on the next run with more damage done.

**Is milky oil always seawater?**
No — fresh coolant does the same thing and is less urgent. An oil analysis for
sodium and chloride tells you which.

**Will the engine be alright?**
If it was not run and was flushed within a day, often yes. Left a week, usually
not. Bearings are the deciding factor.
