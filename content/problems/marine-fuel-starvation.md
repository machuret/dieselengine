# Fuel starvation at high revs

The engine idles and cruises fine, then falls flat or cuts when you ask for full
power. Fuel demand rises with load, so a restriction that goes unnoticed at low
flow becomes decisive at high flow.

## Is it safe to keep going?

At reduced revs, usually. But an engine that cuts out under load is an engine
that can cut out entering a bar or a marina, which is where you least want it.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blocked primary or secondary filter | Very common | Vacuum gauge rising; filter bowl | Filters |
| **Blocked tank vent** | **Common** | Opening the filler cap restores power | Free |
| Air leak on the suction side | Common | Perished bowl seal, loose fitting | Cheap |
| Collapsing suction hose | Common | Soft hose; only fails at high demand | Hose |
| Blocked tank pickup or strainer | Common | Sludge at the pickup | Tank clean |
| Failing lift pump | Occasional | Low supply pressure | Pump |
| Sludge stirred up in a seaway | Common | Only happens when rough | Tank clean |
| Injection pump wear | Less common | Bench test | Specialist |

**Test the vent first, because it is free.** If the engine recovers when you
open the fuel filler cap, the tank has been pulling a vacuum. That is a blocked
or submerged vent, and it costs nothing to confirm.

## A vacuum gauge is worth fitting

A vacuum gauge on the primary filter shows how hard the engine is having to
suck. A rising reading warns you days before a filter blocks completely, and it
distinguishes a filter problem from an air leak or a pump problem instantly.

It is inexpensive and it is the single most useful instrument you can add to a
marine fuel system.

## What you can check yourself

- Open the fuel filler cap while it is struggling
- Filter condition, and vacuum gauge reading if fitted
- Squeeze the suction hoses — soft ones collapse under vacuum
- Separator bowl seal and every fitting on the suction side
- Whether it only happens in rough water — that points at the tank

## When to call a mechanic

If filters, vent and air leaks are ruled out. Beyond those you are into the lift
pump, the tank pickup and the injection pump.

## Frequently asked questions

**It runs fine until I open the throttle — why?**
Fuel demand rises with load. A restriction invisible at cruise becomes the
limiting factor at full power.

**The engine recovers if I open the fuel cap — what is that?**
A blocked tank vent creating a vacuum. Free to confirm, cheap to fix.

**Only in rough weather — why?**
Motion suspends settled sludge and sends it to the filters.

**Is it the injectors?**
Rarely on this symptom. Injectors produce rough running and smoke, not clean
starvation under load.
