# Low oil pressure alarm

Oil pressure is what keeps metal off metal. Without it, bearings fail in
minutes, and the damage is not repairable by topping up afterwards.

## Shut down now

**Stop the engine immediately.** Do not run it to get somewhere. Do not run it
while you look for the dipstick.

Every second at low oil pressure is bearing damage, and bearing damage is a
rebuild. This is one of the few faults where the difference between stopping now
and stopping in two minutes is measured in thousands of dollars.

If you are in immediate danger and must move, understand that you are trading
the engine for the situation — and make that trade deliberately.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| Low oil level | Very common | Dipstick |
| Failed sender or gauge | Very common | Pressure tested against a mechanical gauge |
| Corroded connection or bad earth | Common | Other instruments odd too |
| Oil diluted with fuel or water | Common | Oil looks thin, smells of diesel, or is milky |
| Blocked oil filter or suction strainer | Occasional | Inspection |
| Worn bearings | Occasional | Pressure low and hot; knock |
| Failing oil pump | Occasional | Pressure test |
| Wrong oil grade | Occasional | Service records |

**Sender failures are genuinely common**, which tempts people to assume that is
what it is. Do not assume. Confirm with a mechanical gauge before running the
engine again — the cost of being wrong is the whole engine.

## What you can check yourself

With the engine **off**:

- Oil level and condition. Is it thin? Milky? Does it smell of fuel? Is the
  level *higher* than it should be — which means something is diluting it?
- Obvious leaks in the bilge
- Wiring to the sender, and the earth

Low **and** milky means water is getting in and it is urgent.
Low **and** thin with a fuel smell means fuel dilution.

## When to call a mechanic

Before running the engine again. This is not a symptom to test by starting it
and watching.

pressure loss is most often an oil condition problem before it is a wear
problem.

## Frequently asked questions

**It is probably just the sender — can I keep going?**
No. Senders fail often, and so do bearings. Confirm with a mechanical gauge
before running it again.

**Pressure is fine cold and low when hot — what is that?**
Classic bearing wear, or oil that is too thin for the temperature. It needs
testing, not watching.

**The oil level is above maximum — is that a problem?**
Yes. Something is diluting it — fuel or water — and both cause low pressure and
bearing damage.

**How long can an engine run at low oil pressure?**
Assume none. Damage begins immediately.
