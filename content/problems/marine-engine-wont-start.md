# Marine engine won't start

Most no-starts on a marine diesel are electrical or fuel-related and fixable at
the berth. But there is one thing to establish before you do anything else.

## Before you crank it repeatedly

**If the engine will not turn over — as opposed to turning but not firing —
stop.** It may be hydrolocked, and forcing it bends connecting rods.

And on a raw-water-cooled engine, prolonged cranking pumps seawater into the
exhaust, where it can back up into a cylinder. **Cranking a marine diesel that
will not start can create a much worse problem than the one you began with.**

Diagnose between attempts rather than cranking through them.

→ [Marine engine hydrolock](/marine-engine-problems/marine-engine-hydrolock/)

## First: does it turn over?

| | Meaning | Go to |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing at all, no click | Battery, isolator, connection, ignition switch | Electrical, below |
| Click, no turn | Battery low, starter solenoid, bad earth | Electrical, below |
| Turns slowly | Battery, cables, earths — or a mechanical drag | Electrical, below |
| Turns normally, will not fire | Fuel, air, compression | [Cranks but won't fire](/marine-engine-problems/cranks-but-wont-fire-marine/) |
| Will not turn, or stops solid | **Possible hydrolock — stop** | Hydrolock, above |

## Electrical causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| Flat or failed battery | Very common | Load test — resting voltage proves little |
| Corroded terminal or bad earth | Very common | Voltage drop test across connections |
| Battery isolator off or failed | Common | No power anywhere |
| Failed starter solenoid | Common | Click, no crank |
| Engine stop solenoid or cable | Common | Turns fine, no fuel delivered |
| Failed starter motor | Occasional | Everything else tests good |
| Safety interlock — gear not in neutral | Occasional | Shift lever position |

**Batteries first.** A marine battery can read 12.6V at rest and collapse under
cranking load. Resting voltage is not a test; a load test is.

## What you can check yourself

- Isolator on, gear lever in neutral
- Battery terminals clean and tight; earth strap sound
- Voltage while cranking, if you have a meter
- Fuel in the tank, and the fuel valve open — more common than anyone admits
- The stop control fully returned

After a layup, expect the batteries first and the fuel second.
→ [Hard starting after layup](/marine-engine-problems/hard-starting-after-layup/)

## When to call a mechanic

If it will not turn at all, if it turns but will not fire after checking fuel,
or if you are on a mooring and running out of light. A mobile marine mechanic
can diagnose at the berth.

→ [Mobile marine mechanic](/services/mobile-marine-mechanic/)

## Frequently asked questions

**It cranks strongly but will not fire — where do I look?**
Fuel and air, in that order.
→ [Cranks but won't fire](/marine-engine-problems/cranks-but-wont-fire-marine/)

**Is it safe to keep cranking?**
No. On a raw-water-cooled engine, cranking pumps seawater into the exhaust where
it can enter a cylinder. Short attempts with diagnosis in between.

**The batteries read 12.6V — can they still be the problem?**
Yes, easily. Resting voltage tells you very little; a battery can read fine and
collapse under load.

**It started fine last month — what changed?**
If it has sat since, batteries and fuel. Both degrade with time rather than use.
