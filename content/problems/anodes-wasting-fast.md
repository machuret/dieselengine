# Anodes wasting away too fast

Anodes are supposed to be consumed — that is the job. But a sudden change in how
fast they go is diagnostic. Something has changed, and until you find out what,
the thing being protected is next.

## Is it safe to keep going?

Yes, but do not simply fit more anodes and carry on. Accelerated wastage means
either your protection is failing or something is actively driving corrosion, and
in the second case propellers, shafts and drive legs can be consumed in months.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| **Stray current from an electrical fault** | Common | Corrosion survey; check with shore power off |
| Galvanic interaction through shore power | Common | Wastage started when berth or neighbour changed |
| Wrong alloy for the water | Common | Magnesium in salt water disappears fast |
| Warmer water | Common | Tropical berths legitimately consume faster |
| Poor electrical contact at the anode | Common | Anode intact but the metal it protects corroding |
| Too few anodes for the metal being protected | Occasional | Survey |
| A neighbouring vessel's fault | Occasional | Wastage changes when you move berth |

**Stray current is the one to rule out.** An electrical fault putting current
through the water accelerates metal loss dramatically, and it does not stop at
the anodes. It needs electrical diagnosis, not more zinc.

## Alloy matters

| Alloy | Use |
|---|---|
| Zinc | Traditional salt water |
| Aluminium | Salt and brackish; now widely used |
| Magnesium | **Fresh water only** — wastes very rapidly in salt |

A boat that moves between salt and fresh water — up a river system, for instance
— needs this considered rather than assumed.

→ [Why sacrificial anodes matter](/guides/why-anodes-matter/)

## The anodes people never check

Hull and drive anodes are visible and usually get changed. The engine's internal
anodes are not, and are routinely untouched for the life of a boat:

- Heat exchanger pencil anode
- Aftercooler anode on many turbocharged engines
- Oil and gearbox cooler anodes on some installations

A boat with immaculate hull anodes and an untouched heat exchanger anode is
common — and the heat exchanger is what is quietly dissolving.

## What you can check yourself

- Rate of wastage compared with previous seasons
- Whether it changed when you changed berth, or when a neighbour arrived
- Whether the anodes are the right alloy for your water
- Whether the mating faces are clean bare metal — a painted or corroded contact
  face means the anode is decorative
- Whether the internal engine anodes have ever been changed

## When to call a mechanic

If wastage has accelerated with no obvious explanation, or if you are losing
metal from the propeller or drive leg rather than the anodes. That needs a
corrosion survey and an electrical check.

## Frequently asked questions

**How fast should anodes waste?**
There is no universal figure. Replace at about half consumed. A change in the
rate matters more than the rate itself.

**Should I just fit more anodes?**
Not until you know why. If stray current is driving it, more anodes buys time
without fixing anything.

**Zinc or aluminium?**
Aluminium is widely used in salt and brackish water; zinc remains common in
salt. Magnesium is fresh water only.

**Can I paint over anodes when antifouling?**
No. A painted anode provides no protection.

**Does my engine have anodes inside it?**
Very likely — a pencil anode in the heat exchanger at minimum. It is the most
commonly neglected item on a marine engine.
