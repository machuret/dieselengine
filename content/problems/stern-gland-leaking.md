# Stern gland leaking or overheating

The stern gland is where the propeller shaft passes through the hull. It is the
fitting most likely to sink a boat at its berth, which is why a change in its
behaviour deserves attention rather than a mental note.

## Is it safe to keep going?

Depends on which type you have and what it is doing.

**A traditional packing gland dripping a little is correct.** A few drips per
minute under way lubricate and cool the packing.

**A dripless seal should not drip at all.** Any water from one needs
investigating now — the bellows that holds it in compression is a rubber
component with a service life, and when it fails the ingress rate can exceed the
bilge pump.

**A gland that is hot** is over-tightened, running dry, and scoring your shaft.

## Which type do you have?

| | Traditional packing gland | Dripless mechanical seal |
|---|---|---|
| Normal behaviour | Drips slowly under way | No drips |
| Adjustment | Compression nut | None — it is a wear item |
| Wear part | Packing material | Rubber bellows, carbon face |
| Failure mode | Gradual increase in drip | Sudden, and fast |
| Warning | Plenty | Little |

## Likely causes, ranked

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Packing gland dripping heavily | Packing worn | Adjust, then repack |
| Packing gland hot | Over-tightened | Back it off before it scores the shaft |
| Any water from a dripless seal | Bellows perished, or seal faces worn | Investigate now |
| Squealing after launch | Trapped air, seal running dry | Vent the seal |
| Salt crust or rust staining | Slow leak | Service |
| Leak worse with vibration | Misalignment or worn cutless bearing | Alignment and bearing |

## The maintenance almost nobody does

**A dripless seal's bellows has a service life**, specified by the maker and
usually measured in years. It is rubber, underwater, under compression, in a hot
engine bay. Owners routinely run them well past their interval because nothing
appears wrong — right up until it does.

If yours has never been replaced and the boat is not new, find out how old it
is.

## What you can check yourself

- Which type you have, and whether its behaviour matches the table above
- Whether the packing gland is hot after a run — it should be warm, not hot
- Whether the dripless bellows looks perished, cracked or swollen
- Whether the seal was vented after the last launch

## When to call a mechanic

For any water from a dripless seal, for a gland you cannot get to stop dripping
by adjustment, or if the shaft is scored. Most of this work needs a haul-out and
is sensibly combined with the cutless bearing and alignment.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should my stern gland drip?**
A traditional packing gland should — a few drips per minute under way. A dripless
seal should not.

**My stuffing box is hot — is that normal?**
No. It is over-tightened and not being cooled, and it is scoring the shaft. Back
it off.

**How often does a dripless bellows need replacing?**
On the maker's interval, in years. It is a safety item, not a suggestion.

**Why does it squeal after launching?**
Trapped air. Many dripless seals need venting after launch or they run dry.
