# Sterndrive bellows failure

The bellows are rubber boots sealing the openings where the drive shaft, exhaust
and shift cable pass through the transom. When the drive bellows splits, water
enters the boat.

They fail from age and UV, not from use — which is why an unused boat is not a
safe boat here.

## Is it safe to keep going?

**No, if water is entering.** Get the boat out of the water or to somewhere it
can be monitored, and do not leave it unattended at a berth.

## How you know

- Water in the bilge with no other explanation, worse under way
- Rumbling or squealing from the transom — the gimbal bearing failing behind a
  failed bellows
- Visible cracking, splitting or perishing on inspection
- Milky drive oil — a different seal, but the same neglect pattern
- The drive feels or sounds different

## The chain that costs owners the most

This is the clearest cheap-prevention-versus-expensive-failure sequence on the
site:

1. A bellows perishes with age. Nothing appears wrong.
2. Water enters the gimbal housing.
3. The gimbal bearing corrodes and fails.
4. Its failure damages the drive shaft and U-joints.
5. What began as an inexpensive rubber part becomes a drive rebuild — and in the
   worst case a boat taking water at its berth.

**Replace bellows on the manufacturer's interval**, commonly a small number of
years, regardless of hours.

## What you can check yourself

- Visual inspection with the drive trimmed — look for cracking at the folds
- Water in the bilge that you cannot otherwise account for
- Drive oil condition; milky means water past a seal
- Any new noise from the transom

Full inspection needs the boat out and the drive off, which is why this is
haul-out work.

## When to call a mechanic

At the replacement interval, at the first sign of perishing, or immediately if
water is entering. Ask for the gimbal bearing and U-joints to be assessed at the
same time — if the bellows has been leaking, they have been getting wet.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should bellows be replaced?**
On the maker's interval — commonly a few years — regardless of hours. They fail
from age and UV.

**My boat is barely used — do I still need to?**
Yes, and arguably more so. Unused rubber still perishes, and an unattended boat
is the worst place for a leak to start.

**Can bellows be inspected without hauling out?**
Partially, with the drive trimmed. Proper inspection needs the drive off.

**What does a rumble from the transom mean?**
Usually a failing gimbal bearing, which is normally the consequence of a bellows
that let water in.
