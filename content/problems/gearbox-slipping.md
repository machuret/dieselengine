# Marine gearbox slipping or not engaging

Engine revs rise but the boat does not accelerate, or drive comes in late,
harshly, or in one direction only. The gearbox is a separate component from the
engine, with its own oil, its own cooler and its own service interval — and it
is the most commonly forgotten item on a marine drivetrain.

## Is it safe to keep going?

At reduced load, briefly, to get in. Continuing to run a slipping gearbox burns
the clutch plates and turns an adjustment into a rebuild.

If drive is intermittent, get alongside while you still have it.

## Check these before condemning the gearbox

Three of the five most common causes are cheap.

| Cause | How common | Confirms it | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low oil level** | **Very common** | Dipstick | Trivial |
| **Wrong oil type** | **Common** | Some boxes take engine oil, some ATF | Oil change |
| **Cable or linkage not reaching full travel** | **Very common** | Selector does not hit its stops | Adjustment |
| Blocked oil cooler | Common | Gearbox overheating too | Cooler service |
| Worn clutch packs | Common | Everything else checks out | Rebuild |
| Failed damper plate | Common | Rattle at idle, no drive | Damper |
| Misalignment loading the output bearing | Occasional | Vibration, seal leaks | Alignment |

**The cable one destroys gearboxes.** A linkage that does not move the selector
through its full travel gives partial engagement, which slips, which burns the
plates. It is cheap to adjust and expensive to ignore — and it is why a gearbox
that has been "slipping a bit for a while" often needs a rebuild by the time it
is looked at.

## What you can check yourself

- Oil level, and oil condition — milky means water, burnt smell means slipping
- **Oil type against the plate on the box.** Using engine oil where ATF is
  specified, or the reverse, causes exactly this symptom.
- Does the selector at the box reach its stops when you move the lever?
- Does it slip in both directions, or only ahead?
- Is there a rattle at idle that stops in gear? That is the damper plate.

## When to call a mechanic

If oil, oil type and cable adjustment are all correct. Rebuilds are specialist
work — general workshops remove and refit, a transmission specialist does the
internals. Ask who is doing which.

On smaller boxes an exchange unit can cost little more than a rebuild. Get both
prices.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a slipping gearbox always need a rebuild?**
No. Oil level, oil type and cable adjustment fix a large share — but left
slipping, the clutches burn and then it does.

**What oil does my gearbox take?**
Depends on the box. Check the plate or the manual; the wrong one causes
slipping.

**Drive in reverse but not ahead — what does that mean?**
One clutch pack is worn or not being engaged fully. Check cable travel before
assuming the worst.

**Rattle at idle that stops in gear?**
Failed damper plate, not the gearbox — though reaching it usually means
separating the box from the engine.
