# Water in the fuel

Water is the root of almost every marine fuel problem. It corrodes the tank,
feeds microbial growth, and destroys the injection system — which relies on
diesel itself for lubrication and gets none from water.

## Is it safe to keep going?

If the separator is catching it, yes, cautiously, while you sort out the source.
If water is reaching the injection system, no — every hour of running is wear on
a pump and injectors that cost far more than the fix.

## How it gets in

| Route | How common |
|---|---|
| Condensation in a part-full tank | Very common |
| Leaking or perished deck fill seal | Very common |
| Failed or submerged tank vent | Common |
| Contaminated fuel at the dock | Common |
| Water taken over the deck into a poor fill | Common |
| Corroded tank | Occasional |

Marine tanks condense far more than road tanks because they sit part-full for
months in humid air. **A full tank over a layup is the single most effective
prevention**, because there is no air space to condense.

## What it causes

- Filters blocking, and the separator filling
- Microbial growth at the fuel-water interface
  → [Diesel bug in the fuel tank](/marine-engine-problems/diesel-bug-symptoms/)
- High-pressure pump and injector wear, from loss of lubrication
- Corrosion inside the tank
- Rough running, power loss, hard starting, white smoke

## What you can check yourself

- **Drain the water separator.** Routinely, and act on the first water you see.
  This is the cheapest sixty seconds on the boat.
- Take a tank-bottom sample and let it settle in a clear jar — water sits below
  the diesel
- Check the deck fill seal — squeeze it, look for perishing
- Check the tank vent is clear and not taking spray
- If a water-in-fuel warning is fitted, never ignore it

→ [Fuel filter and water separator service](/services/marine-fuel-filter-service/)

## When to call a mechanic

If there is significant water in the tank, if it keeps returning after draining,
or if the engine has been running rough. Draining the separator is a stopgap;
the tank is the problem.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much water is too much?**
Any reaching the injection system. That is exactly what the separator exists to
prevent, which is why draining it matters.

**Should I store the tank full or empty?**
Full. Air space condenses moisture, and moisture is what feeds every other fuel
problem.

**The separator keeps filling — is that the separator's fault?**
No, it is doing its job. Something is putting water in the tank; find it.

**Can water damage injectors?**
Yes. Diesel lubricates the injection system; water does not, and it corrodes.
