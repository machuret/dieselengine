# Engine alarm buzzer won't stop

An alarm that will not clear is either telling you something real, or it has
failed in a way that is about to cost you your only warning system.

## Treat it as real first

Before deciding it is a fault, rule out the two things the alarm exists for:

- **Water at the exhaust?** If not, shut down immediately.
  → [No water coming out of the exhaust](/marine-engine-problems/no-water-from-exhaust/)
- **Oil pressure?** A low oil pressure alarm is never to be ridden out.
  → [Low oil pressure alarm](/marine-engine-problems/marine-low-oil-pressure/)

An alarm sounding with the engine **cold and stopped** is an electrical fault.
An alarm sounding with hot metal and no cooling water is not.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| Genuine fault the alarm is reporting | Common | Check flow, temperature, oil pressure |
| Failed sender | Very common | Reading impossible or jumping instantly |
| Corroded connection or bad earth | Very common | Other instruments odd too |
| Water in a connector | Common | Salt crust, intermittent behaviour |
| Failed alarm module or panel | Occasional | Multiple alarms behaving oddly |
| Low coolant tripping a level alarm | Occasional | Header tank |

## Do not disconnect the buzzer

This is the important part.

A nuisance alarm trains you to ignore the sound. When the real one comes — an
overheat that will destroy the engine in minutes, or an oil pressure alarm —
it sounds identical and you have already learned to dismiss it.

Owners who disconnect a buzzer to stop the noise have removed the only warning
system the engine has. **Fix the fault instead.** It is usually a corroded
connection or a cheap sender.

→ [Engine alarms and instrumentation](/services/engine-alarms-instruments/)

## Test that your alarms actually work

The alarms should sound at key-on, before the engine starts. That is the
self-test.

If yours do not sound at key-on, the circuit is not working and you have been
running with no protection — which is a more urgent problem than a buzzer that
sounds too much. This takes five seconds and almost nobody does it. Make it part
of your start routine.

## What you can check yourself

- Water at the exhaust; temperature and oil pressure readings
- Whether the alarm sounds with the engine cold and stopped
- Connections at the sender — salt crust, green corrosion, loose spades
- The main earth
- Whether alarms self-test at key-on

## When to call a mechanic

If the alarm is genuine, or if you cannot tell. Diagnosing a suspect sender needs
a reading from something other than the instrument under suspicion — a mechanical
gauge, or an infrared thermometer on the thermostat housing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I just disconnect the buzzer?**
No. You would be removing your only warning of a fault that destroys engines in
minutes.

**The alarm sounds but the gauge looks fine — which do I believe?**
Shut down and check for water at the exhaust. Gauge and alarm often use
different senders, and the alarm is designed to be conservative.

**My alarms do not sound at key-on — is that a problem?**
Yes. They should self-test before the engine starts. If they do not, the circuit
is faulty and you have no protection.

**What is the most useful alarm to add?**
An exhaust temperature alarm. It detects loss of raw water flow before coolant
temperature moves.
