# Overheat alarm sounding

The alarm is the last warning you get before an engine destroys itself. Treat it
as real until proven otherwise.

## Is it safe to keep going?

**No. Reduce load and shut down.**

The most common mistake is to keep going while deciding whether the alarm is
genuine. On a marine diesel that decision costs minutes you do not have — heads
warp, gaskets fail, and the exhaust hose can melt through.

Shut down first. Diagnose second. If a lee shore makes that impossible, run at
the absolute minimum load that keeps you safe and accept the risk knowingly.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | How common | Confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| Genuine overheat from loss of raw water flow | Very common | No or reduced water at the exhaust |
| Blocked heat exchanger or fouled intake | Common | Good flow, still hot, worse under load |
| Failed temperature sender | Common | Gauge reads impossibly high or jumps instantly |
| Corroded connection or bad earth | Common | Intermittent alarm, other instruments odd too |
| Low coolant in the closed circuit | Occasional | Header tank low once cool |
| Thermostat stuck closed | Occasional | Overheats quickly from cold start |
| Alarm circuit fault | Occasional | Sounds with the engine cold |

**An alarm with the engine cold** is an electrical fault. **An alarm with hot
metal and no water at the exhaust** is real, and every second counts.

## The alarm you have learned to ignore

If your alarm has been sounding spuriously and you have got used to it, you have
lost your warning system. A nuisance alarm trains you to dismiss the sound, and
the real one sounds identical.

Fix the fault. **Do not disconnect the buzzer** — an engine with no working
alarm gives you no warning at all, and the failure it would have caught is the
one that ends the engine.

## What you can check yourself

Once shut down and cool:

- Water flow at the exhaust on a brief restart, watching the gauge
- Strainer and seacock
- Coolant level, once cool — never open a hot pressure cap
- Whether the alarms self-test at key-on. If they do not sound before starting,
  the circuit is faulty and you have had no protection.

## When to call a mechanic

If the alarm is genuine and flow is good, or if you cannot tell whether it is
genuine. Diagnosing a sender against a real overheat needs a reading from
something other than the instrument that is under suspicion.

## Frequently asked questions

**The alarm sounds but the gauge looks normal — which do I believe?**
Shut down and check for water at the exhaust. The gauge and the alarm often use
different senders, and the alarm is the one designed to be conservative.

**Can I just silence it and carry on?**
No. If it is genuine you will destroy the engine; if it is a fault you have
disabled your only warning.

**My alarms do not sound at key-on — is that a problem?**
Yes. They should self-test before the engine starts. If they do not, the circuit
is not working.

**Is an exhaust temperature alarm worth adding?**
It is the most useful alarm you can add. It detects loss of raw water flow
before the coolant temperature has moved, which is meaningfully earlier warning.
