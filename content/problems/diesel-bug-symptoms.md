# Diesel bug in the fuel tank

"Diesel bug" is microbial contamination — bacteria, yeasts and fungi living at
the boundary between the diesel and any water beneath it. They feed on the fuel
and need the water to survive.

It is far more a marine problem than a road problem, for one reason: boats have
large tanks that sit part-full for months in humid conditions, which is exactly
what the organisms need.

## Is it safe to keep going?

You can usually get home. But the organisms produce acids that attack the tank,
and the sludge blocks filters — typically at the worst moment, because motion is
what stirs it up.

## How you know

| Sign | What it means |
|---|---|
| Dark slime, jelly or stringy mats in the filter bowl | Established growth |
| Filters blocking repeatedly, worse in rough weather | Sludge on the tank floor |
| Sour or sulphurous smell from the tank | By-products |
| Black specks in a tank bottom sample | Biomass and sediment |
| Water in the separator | The habitat |
| Loss of power under load, clearing at idle | Restriction from partial blockage |

A tank-bottom sample and a test kit confirm it. Several other faults block
filters, and it is worth knowing which one you have before treating.

## Treating it properly — four steps, not one

**1. Remove the water.** Without water the organisms cannot live. This step
decides whether the problem returns.

**2. Shock dose with a marine biocide.** Correct dose, full contact time.
Underdosing selects for resistant organisms rather than killing them.

**3. Remove the dead biomass.** Killing a large colony converts it into a large
quantity of sludge — which then blocks your filters. **This is why boats often
have worse filter problems immediately after treatment.** Polishing or tank
cleaning after treatment is part of the job, not an optional extra.

**4. Fix how water is getting in.** A leaking deck fill seal, a failed tank
vent, condensation from long part-full storage, or contaminated fuel at the
supplier. Skip this and the cycle restarts.

## What you can check yourself

Take a bottom sample. Drain the separator and look at the bowl. Check the deck
fill seal and the vent. A test kit is inexpensive and removes the guesswork.

## When to call a mechanic

When growth is confirmed and established. Treatment is straightforward;
removing the biomass from a tank with baffles is not.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will biocide alone fix it?**
No. It kills the organisms and leaves the corpses and the water behind — both of
which still cause problems.

**Why did my filters get worse right after treatment?**
Expected. The treatment released a dead colony into the fuel as sludge. Polish
and change filters afterwards.

**Can I prevent it completely?**
Keep water out and it cannot establish. Full tanks over layup, sound deck fill
and vent, routine separator draining.

**Is it worse in northern Australia?**
Yes. Warm, humid conditions favour growth considerably over southern waters.
