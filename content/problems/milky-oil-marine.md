# Milky oil on the dipstick

Milky, grey or coffee-coloured oil means water has got into it. On a marine
engine that water can be seawater or coolant, and the difference decides both
the urgency and the cost.

## Is it safe to keep going?

**No.** Water in oil destroys bearings, and if it is seawater the damage is
running continuously. Do not use the engine until you know which it is.

## Seawater or coolant? Find out first

| | Seawater | Coolant |
|---|---|---|
| Urgency | Critical — hours matter | Serious, but not hourly |
| Oil appearance | Grey, gritty, sometimes salty | Creamy tan or beige |
| Coolant level | Usually unchanged | Dropping |
| Confirms it | Oil analysis: sodium and chloride | Oil analysis: glycol |

An oil sample settles it, and it is inexpensive relative to the decisions that
follow.

## Likely causes, ranked

| Cause | Water type | How common |
|---|---|---|
| Perforated seawater-cooled oil cooler | Seawater | Common |
| Corroded exhaust elbow, water into a cylinder | Seawater | Common |
| Perforated aftercooler | Seawater | Common |
| Head gasket | Coolant | Common |
| Perforated heat exchanger tube | Either | Occasional |
| Cracked head or block | Coolant | Occasional |
| Condensation from short, cold running | Fresh water | Common, and benign |

That last row matters. A light film of emulsion under the oil filler cap on an
engine that only ever idles in and out of a berth is condensation, not a failure
— and the fix is to run the engine under proper load, not to open it up.

**Emulsion under the cap only, with clean oil on the dipstick,** is usually
condensation. **Milky oil on the dipstick itself** is not.

## What you can check yourself

Check the coolant level — if it is dropping, that points at coolant. Look at
whether the oil level has risen. Check whether the emulsion is only under the
cap or throughout the oil. Do not run the engine to "circulate it".

## When to call a mechanic

Once you have milky oil on the dipstick. Ask for an oil analysis and a cooling
system pressure test before anyone quotes to remove a head — a perforated oil
cooler produces the same symptom for a fraction of the cost.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is milky oil always serious?**
Emulsion under the filler cap alone, on a short-trip engine, is usually
condensation. Milky oil on the dipstick is always serious.

**How do I tell seawater from coolant?**
An oil analysis. Sodium and chloride mean seawater; glycol means coolant.

**Can I change the oil and see if it comes back?**
Only if you are also finding the cause. Running it to test costs bearings.

**Is it definitely the head gasket?**
No, and on a marine engine it usually is not. Oil coolers, aftercoolers and heat
exchangers are more common and much cheaper.
