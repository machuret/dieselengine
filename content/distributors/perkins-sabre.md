# Perkins and Sabre marine distributors in Australia

Perkins is one of the great names in the Australian marine fleet, largely
because of how many of its engines are still running decades after they were
fitted.

{{DEALERS:perkins-sabre}}

## An installed base, not a new-engine market

Most Perkins conversations in Australian boating are about supporting an existing
engine rather than buying a new one. The 4.108, the 4.236 and their relatives are
still working in cruising yachts, timber boats and older motor cruisers all over
the country.

That shapes the support picture:

**Parts for the classic families remain available** through Perkins channels and
through a substantial aftermarket and specialist trade. Australia's industrial
and agricultural use of these engines helps considerably.

**Independent knowledge is deep.** These are mechanical engines with no
electronics, and a good independent marine engineer is frequently the better
value on them than a dealer.

**Marine-specific componentry** — heat exchangers, marine manifolds, sea water
pumps — is the harder half, as it always is on a marinised industrial engine.

## The Sabre connection

Sabre marinised Perkins blocks for marine use, and Sabre-badged engines are
common in older British-built and British-influenced boats in the Australian
fleet. Support runs through related channels and the same specialist trade.

## The honest ownership question

On a forty-year-old Perkins the real question is not whether you can get parts
today. It is whether the whole installation is worth continuing to support: the
engine, the exhaust, the raw water circuit, the loom, the panel and the mounts
are all the same age.

That is exactly the comparison in our
[repower or rebuild guide](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/), and for boats in this
position it is usually the right conversation to have properly rather than
patching one component at a time.

## What to ask before booking

1. Do you work on the classic mechanical families regularly?
2. Are the parts genuine, aftermarket or reconditioned?
3. What is the lead time on marine-specific componentry?
4. Is the block worth rebuilding — has it been bored before?
5. What else in the installation is at the end of its life?

## Frequently asked questions

**Are Perkins marine engines still supported in Australia?**
The classic families remain supported through Perkins channels, a substantial
aftermarket, and a deep specialist trade.

**What is a Sabre marine engine?**
Sabre marinised Perkins blocks for marine use. They are common in older
British-influenced boats and supported through related channels.

**Should I use a dealer or an independent?**
For mechanical engines of this era an experienced independent is frequently the
better value and equally capable.

**Which parts are hardest to get?**
Marine-specific componentry — heat exchangers, marine manifolds, sea water pumps
— rather than base engine items.

**Is it worth rebuilding a forty-year-old Perkins?**
Sometimes, but the honest comparison includes the exhaust, loom, panel, mounts
and raw water circuit, which are the same age.
