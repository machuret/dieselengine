# Kubota marine base engines and parts in Australia

Kubota does not sell marine engines. It sells the industrial blocks that sit
underneath a large share of the small marine diesels in Australian yachts —
Beta, Nanni, Solé, Vetus and others — and understanding that is worth real money
to an owner.

{{DEALERS:kubota-marine}}

## Why this page exists

If your yacht has a Beta, a Nanni, a Solé or a Vetus auxiliary, roughly speaking
you own a Kubota with marine parts bolted to it.

The engine block, crankshaft, pistons, valves, oil pump, injection pump,
injectors, timing components, oil filters and many gaskets are Kubota industrial
items with an Australian support network built for agriculture, construction and
groundcare. That network is vastly larger than any marine one and reaches into
country towns with no marine trade at all.

**The marinizer's own parts** — heat exchanger, marine exhaust manifold, sea
water pump, wiring loom, instrument panel, gearbox adaptor, saildrive leg where
fitted — come through the marine channel and are the expensive, slower half.

## What this means practically

**Ask which half a part belongs to.** A workshop quoting a full marine price for
an item available as a Kubota industrial part is either not aware or not saying.
You are entitled to ask.

**Identify the base engine.** Your marinizer's documentation, or the block
casting and plate, will identify the Kubota family. That is what an industrial
supplier needs.

**For remote cruising this is a genuine advantage.** Base engine servicing items
can be sourced in a lot of places a marine part cannot.

**It does not extend to everything.** Do not assume an industrial supplier can
help with cooling, exhaust or electrical items in marine configuration.

## Servicing implications

Kubota's industrial service intervals and the marinizer's marine intervals are
not the same document, and where they differ the **marine schedule wins** —
because the marine environment adds the raw water circuit, the anodes and the
salt that the industrial manual never considered.

Use the marinizer's manual for intervals, and the Kubota parts network for the
base engine items it lists.

## What to ask before booking

1. Which parts on this quote are base engine, and which are marine-specific?
2. What Kubota family is my engine built on?
3. Can any base engine items be sourced industrially?
4. Are you using the marinizer's service schedule?
5. What marine-specific spares should I carry?

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Kubota make marine engines?**
Not as such. It makes industrial blocks that marinizers such as Beta, Nanni,
Solé and Vetus convert for marine use.

**Can I buy Kubota parts for my Beta or Nanni?**
For base engine items, frequently yes, through the industrial network. Marine
componentry comes through the marine channel.

**How do I find out which Kubota is under my engine?**
The marinizer's documentation, or the block casting and plate. An industrial
supplier will need that identification.

**Which service schedule should I follow?**
The marinizer's. It accounts for the raw water circuit, anodes and salt
environment that the industrial manual does not.

**Is this an advantage for remote cruising?**
A real one. Base engine servicing items are available far more widely than
marine parts.
