# Marine diesel engine distributors in Australia

**We do not publish dealer lists we have not verified.** Fabricated or stale
listings send people to businesses that have moved, closed or never held the
agency, and that is worse than no list at all.

What these pages do instead is explain how each brand's Australian network
actually works — who imports it, what an authorised dealer can do that an
independent cannot, where parts come from, and how to confirm that whoever you
are talking to is what they say they are. That is the part that is hard to find
and does not go out of date.

## How marine engine distribution works here

Almost every marine diesel brand in Australia runs the same three-layer
structure, with different names:

**1. The importer or national distributor.** One company holds the Australian
agency for the brand. They import engines, hold the national parts inventory,
run warranty administration and appoint the dealer network. For some brands this
is a subsidiary of the manufacturer; for others it is an independent Australian
company.

**2. Authorised dealers and service agents.** Appointed businesses with factory
training, access to the diagnostic tools, warranty authority and a parts account.
The network's density varies enormously by brand and by region.

**3. Independent marine engineers.** Not appointed, often extremely capable,
frequently better value on older mechanical engines. They buy parts through the
same chain or through aftermarket suppliers.

All three are legitimate. Which you want depends on the job.

## When the authorised dealer matters

- **Warranty work.** Only an authorised business can perform it and be paid for
  it. Work done elsewhere during the warranty period can put the warranty at
  risk — check the terms rather than assuming.
- **Electronic engine diagnostics.** Modern common-rail engines need the
  manufacturer's tool. An independent may have it; many do not.
- **Software updates and parameter changes**, which are dealer-only.
- **A new engine purchase**, which comes through the network.
- **Commissioning after a repower**, where the warranty starts.

## When an independent is often the better choice

- **Older mechanical engines**, where there is no software and the knowledge is
  general
- **Routine servicing** outside a warranty period
- **Mobile work** at your berth or mooring
- **Rebuild and machining work**, which is a specialist trade in its own right
- **Value**, frequently

## How to verify what you are being told

The manufacturer's own dealer locator is the authoritative list — every major
brand publishes one, and it is updated when appointments change. Use it rather
than a search result.

Then ask directly:

1. **Are you an authorised dealer or an independent?** Both are fine answers.
   An evasive one is not.
2. **Do you have the diagnostic tool for my engine family?**
3. **Can you perform warranty work on this engine?**
4. **Where do the parts come from, and what is the lead time?**
5. **Are you insured, and accredited to work in my marina?**

Our guide to [choosing a marine mechanic](/guides/how-to-choose-a-marine-mechanic/)
covers the rest.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why don't you list marine engine dealers?**
Because we have not verified them. A stale or invented listing sends someone to a
business that has moved, closed or never held the agency.

**Where is the authoritative list of dealers for my engine?**
The manufacturer's own dealer locator. It is maintained by the people who make
the appointments.

**Does servicing outside the dealer network void my warranty?**
It depends on the terms. Warranty work itself must be done by an authorised
business. Check the written terms rather than assuming either way.

**Is an independent mechanic as good as a dealer?**
For older mechanical engines and routine servicing, frequently better value and
equally capable. For electronic diagnostics, software and warranty, the dealer
has authority and tools the independent may not.

**What is a national distributor?**
The company holding the Australian agency for a brand — importing engines,
holding national parts stock, administering warranty and appointing dealers.
