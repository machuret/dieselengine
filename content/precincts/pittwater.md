# Marine diesel at Pittwater

Pittwater holds the largest concentration of cruising and sailing boats in
Australia, and the engine trade here has specialised accordingly. If your boat
has a saildrive, this is the water where people see them every day.

## A sailing fleet's engine problems

A cruising yacht's auxiliary has a life almost opposite to a powerboat's: small,
worked in short bursts, and switched off for long periods while the boat sails
or sits on a mooring.

That produces a predictable set of problems:

**Low hours over a long calendar.** The meter flatters and the internals do not.
Oil degrading by acid and moisture, water condensing in the fuel tank, impellers
taking a set — none of it depends on running time.

**Bore glazing from light loading.** Motoring out of the moorings and back at
low revs, never under real load, polishes cylinder walls so the rings stop
sealing.

**Saildrive legs, and the diaphragm nobody can date.** A rubber diaphragm below
the waterline with a time-based replacement interval and a haul-out attached.
On a second-hand boat this is the single most important thing to establish, and
"I don't know" is the common answer.

**Leg anodes** consumed briskly in warm water, protecting an aluminium casting
worth more than a full service.

## Moorings change how you engage the trade

Most Pittwater boats sit on swing moorings rather than in berths, and that
decides everything about attendance:

**Mobile is the default.** Servicing, diagnostics, impellers and fault-finding
happen where the boat lies, worked from a tender. There is no reason to move the
boat for most jobs, and the local operators are set up for exactly this.

**Anything below the waterline means a yard.** Leg work, diaphragms, shafts and
antifouling need the boat hauled, and slipway capacity is the booked resource
rather than the workshop diary.

**Tender access is a real variable.** Weather that stops a tender stops the
job. Local mechanics build that into scheduling; a mainland-style expectation of
a fixed appointment does not survive a strong southerly.

## What to ask a Pittwater operator

- Do you work on saildrives, and which families?
- Can you programme leg work with the slipway rather than separately?
- What do you hold for my engine, and what comes from elsewhere?
- How do you handle a weather day?

## Frequently asked questions

**Is mobile servicing normal at Pittwater?**
It is the default. Most of the fleet is on swing moorings and the trade works
from tenders.

**My yacht has very low hours. Is that good?**
Not by itself. Oil, fuel and impellers deteriorate on the calendar, and light
motoring glazes bores. Low hours over many years is its own risk.

**How often does a saildrive diaphragm need changing?**
At the manufacturer's time-based interval for your leg, with a haul-out. Treat
unknown history as overdue.

**Why do my leg anodes go so fast?**
Warm water, shore power where fitted, and an aluminium leg in an aggressive
galvanic environment. A sudden change in rate is worth investigating.

**What gets delayed here?**
Anything needing a haul-out, and anything needing a tender in bad weather.
