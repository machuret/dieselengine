# Marine diesel at Sandringham

A metropolitan Port Phillip harbour with a large mixed fleet — yachts, cruisers
and trailer boats — and the classic Melbourne maintenance profile in its purest
form.

## The layup problem, concentrated

Sandringham's fleet is recreational, metropolitan and seasonal. Boats are used
through summer and sit from about May, and that produces deterioration that has
nothing to do with running hours:

- **Oil degrading** by acid formation and moisture absorption
- **Water condensing in the fuel tank** as the hull heats and cools daily
- **Impellers taking a set** and splitting on the first turn of the season
- **Coolant inhibitors depleting** on the calendar
- **Anodes dissolving** on the mooring with the engine off

An eighteen-year-old boat here with modest hours needs more scepticism than one
with three times as many worked properly.

**Service by the calendar, not the meter.** In a seasonal fleet that is the
single most valuable habit, and the one most often skipped because the hour
reading looks reassuring.

## The spring rush is real

The whole fleet discovers its problems in the same few weeks each spring, and
the local trade is fully committed exactly when everybody wants their boat.

Booking servicing before the season rather than during it is worth more than any
negotiation on rate. An owner who books in August is a different customer from
one who calls in November.

## Shallow and cold

The bay here is shallow near shore and the water is cold by Australian
standards. Cooling systems have an easy time — silted heat exchangers are a much
smaller share of the local complaint list than in Queensland.

What cold water is hard on is **starting**. Battery capacity, glow systems and
oil grade all matter more, and a flat battery in spring often masks a second
fault rather than being the whole story.

## Going outside

Port Phillip Heads is one of the more demanding entrances in the country, and it
is unforgiving of an engine chosen at the bottom of its design range. For a boat
that goes outside, power margin and primary fuel filtration are not optional.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to service if I barely used the boat?**
Yes. Oil, fuel, coolant, impellers and anodes all degrade on the calendar
regardless of the hour meter.

**When should I book?**
Before the season. The whole fleet books in spring and the trade is fully
committed exactly when you want your boat.

**Why won't it start after winter?**
Cold water and months of sitting. Battery capacity, glow system and oil grade
are the usual causes, and a flat battery often masks a second fault.

**Is overheating common here?**
Much less than in Queensland. Cold, comparatively clean water is easy on heat
exchangers.

**What do I need for going outside the Heads?**
Power margin and serious primary fuel filtration.
