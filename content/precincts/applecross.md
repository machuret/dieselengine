# Marine diesel at Applecross

Swan River berthing, close to central Perth, in water that is brackish for much
of the year and behaves nothing like the ocean fleet's.

## The river is a different electrolyte

This is the defining fact about keeping a boat here. The Swan is brackish
upstream and increasingly saline towards Fremantle, and the balance shifts with
rainfall, season and river flow.

A boat that stays on the river and a boat that runs down to the ocean regularly
are in materially different corrosion environments, and the anode alloy should
reflect that:

- **Magnesium** is for fresh water only and wastes rapidly in salt
- **Zinc** passivates in brackish and fresh water and stops protecting
- **Aluminium** spans the range and generally suits a river boat that goes out

An owner whose anodes last far longer than expected is not saving money — a
passivated zinc protects nothing. See
[why anodes matter](/guides/why-anodes-matter/).

## River water in the raw water circuit

The Swan carries more suspended material than the ocean, and after winter rain
considerably more. Strainers fill faster than an ocean-berthed boat's, and heat
exchangers collect what gets past.

The water is also warmer than the ocean through summer, which gives the cooling
system less margin than a Fremantle boat has.

## A flat-water fleet

River boats do a great deal of low-load cruising in sheltered water. That is
pleasant boating and it is the pattern that glazes bores and builds carbon.

Running the engine up under genuine load — which on the river means deliberately,
or on a run downstream and out — is the mitigation, and it needs to be a habit
rather than an accident.

## Going out the river

A boat running from Applecross to the ocean passes through a changing
electrolyte, transitions from flat water to Indian Ocean swell, and meets the
afternoon sea breeze on the way home. The fuel-stirring failure that defines
this coast applies, and it applies to a tank that has been sitting in flat water
undisturbed.

If the boat rarely goes out, deal with the fuel before a trip rather than
discovering the tank's contents in the swell.

## Frequently asked questions

**What anodes should a Swan River boat use?**
Usually aluminium for a boat that moves between river and ocean. Zinc passivates
in brackish water and magnesium is fresh-only.

**My anodes last ages. Is that good?**
Not necessarily. A passivated zinc in brackish water protects nothing while
looking healthy.

**Does river water affect cooling?**
It carries more suspended material than the ocean and is warmer in summer, so
the raw water side needs attention on a shorter cycle.

**Is flat-water river cruising bad for the engine?**
Prolonged light loading glazes bores. Run the engine up under real load
deliberately.

**What should I do before a trip outside?**
Deal with the fuel system. A tank that has sat in flat water gives up its
contents in ocean swell.
