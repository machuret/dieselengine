# Marine diesel at Botany

Botany Bay and the Georges River hold a different Sydney fleet from the harbour:
more trailer boats, more sportfishers, more sterndrives, and a service trade
shaped accordingly.

## A trailered and sterndrive fleet

A large share of boats here are trailered or kept on lifts rather than
permanently in the water, and that changes the maintenance profile entirely:

**Less immersion, less growth, less galvanic exposure.** Anodes last longer and
hull fouling is not the constant problem it is for a moored boat.

**Flushing discipline replaces most of it.** Running fresh water through the raw
water circuit after each use is what prevents salt drying inside the engine and
the leg. It is simple, it is the single most effective habit available to this
fleet, and it is the thing most often skipped.

**Sterndrive maintenance dominates.** Bellows on a time-based interval, gimbal
bearings, universal joints and leg oil condition. For a boat kept on a lift
rather than in the water the corrosion pressure is lower, but the mechanical
wear items are unchanged — and bellows failure sinks boats regardless of how the
boat is stored.

## The bay and the river

**Botany Bay is shallow and sandy** across much of it, and launching and
shallow-water running put sand through raw water intakes. Impellers abrade and
heat exchangers collect it.

**The Georges River** carries runoff from a large urban catchment, and after
rain it carries a great deal. A boat kept up the river draws a dirtier supply
than one in the bay.

**Industrial foreshore.** Port Botany is a working container port, and boats
berthed near industrial shore power are in an environment where stray current is
worth watching.

## Offshore, quickly

The bay opens directly to the ocean, and the fleet fishes well out. The familiar
Sydney pattern applies: a tank that behaves in the bay finds its sludge in a
swell, and the filters block where it matters.

For a trailered boat that sits between uses, this is compounded — fuel ages in
the tank on the trailer just as it does on a mooring.

## Frequently asked questions

**What matters most for a trailered boat here?**
Flushing the raw water circuit after every use. It prevents most of what
otherwise goes wrong, and it is the habit most often skipped.

**How often do sterndrive bellows need replacing?**
At the manufacturer's time-based interval, regardless of how the boat is stored.
Failure sinks boats.

**Does shallow launching damage anything?**
Sand drawn through the raw water intake abrades impellers and settles in heat
exchangers. It is a real local factor.

**Why does my engine struggle offshore but not in the bay?**
Motion stirs settled water and sludge into the fuel pickup. Bay testing will not
reproduce it.

**Does fuel age on a trailer?**
Yes. Time in the tank is what matters, not whether the boat is in the water.
