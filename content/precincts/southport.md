# Marine diesel at Southport

The southern Broadwater, the Seaway, and a fleet that includes more moorings and
more offshore use than the canal marinas further north.

## The Seaway changes the fleet

Southport sits near the Gold Coast Seaway, which is the fleet's access to open
ocean. That gives the boats here a different working life from a purely
Broadwater boat:

**Offshore running under real load**, rather than displacement-speed canal
cruising. That is better for the engine in every respect except cooling, which
works harder.

**The bar-crossing discipline applies.** The Seaway is engineered and far more
forgiving than a natural river bar, but it is still where a boat meets ocean
swell, and it is still where a fuel tank gets stirred. If there is water or
sludge on the tank bottom, that is when it reaches the filters.

**Power margin matters.** An engine chosen at the bottom of its design range is
working hardest exactly on the run home.

## Moorings as well as berths

Unlike Hope Island or Sanctuary Cove, a meaningful part of the Southport fleet
sits on moorings. That means:

- **Mobile servicing from a tender** for routine work, with the weather
  dependency that comes with it
- **Delivery to a facility** for anything below the waterline
- **More boats sitting between uses**, and therefore more time-based
  deterioration than wear

## Warm, shallow water

The Broadwater here is warm and shallow, and anodes are consumed faster than in
cooler southern water. The pencil anode inside the heat exchanger remains the
item most commonly never replaced on a Queensland engine.

Heat exchanger cleaning belongs on a shorter cycle than the manual's generic
figure, and it is worth doing before symptoms appear rather than after an
overheat.

## The advantage of the postcode

Coomera's engineering is twenty minutes away. For an owner here, that means
specialist work and comparison quotes are genuinely accessible without moving
the boat far — the same structural advantage the whole coast enjoys.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does going out the Seaway change what my engine needs?**
It puts the engine under real load, which is good for it, and works the cooling
system harder. It also stirs the fuel tank, which is where tank contamination
announces itself.

**Is mobile servicing available?**
Yes, and for moored boats it is the norm — with the weather dependency that
tender-based work carries.

**Why are my anodes going so fast?**
Warm, shallow water accelerates galvanic action. Check the pencil anode inside
the heat exchanger too; it is the one most often never replaced.

**How often should the heat exchanger be cleaned?**
On a shorter cycle than a generic manual interval, and before symptoms rather
than after an overheat.

**Where do I go for major work?**
Coomera, twenty minutes north, which holds the region's heavy engineering.
