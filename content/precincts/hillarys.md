# Marine diesel at Hillarys

A purpose-built boat harbour serving Perth's large northern-suburbs fleet, with
the Indian Ocean immediately outside the entrance.

## No sheltered water to warm up in

A boat leaving Hillarys is in open ocean within minutes. There is no estuary, no
bay, no gradual transition — the breakwater and then the swell.

That has a specific engine consequence that catches owners moving from a river
berth: **the engine goes from cold to working load very quickly**, and there is
nowhere to limp back through if something is wrong.

It also means the afternoon sea breeze is a design condition rather than a
weather event. Through summer it builds a short, steep chop with complete
reliability, and the fleet comes home in it.

## The fuel failure that defines this coast

A tank that behaves on the way out finds its sludge on the way home. Motion
stirs settled water and debris into the pickup, and the filters block in the
worst conditions of the day, under load, with the boat working.

This is the single most common Perth engine failure and it is not an engine
fault. A primary water-separating filter with a vacuum gauge lets you see
restriction building before it becomes a stall, and it is worth more here than
almost any other addition.

The second consequence is power. An engine chosen at the bottom of its design
range is working hardest exactly when the sea breeze is up and you want to be
home.

## Rottnest and the islands

Boats from here run to Rottnest and the offshore islands routinely, which puts
them further from help than their length suggests.

Carry the spares that matter — impeller and cover gasket, fuel filters, belts,
hoses — and the tools to fit them. Distance from assistance is the
Perth-specific factor and it applies from this harbour more than most.

## Parts, as everywhere in WA

Anything not held in Western Australia comes across the Nullarbor. Ask a
workshop what is on the shelf for your engine family before authorising work
rather than after.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my engine struggle on the way home?**
The afternoon sea breeze stirs the fuel tank. Settled water and sludge reach the
pickup and the filters block. It is a tank problem, not an engine fault.

**What should I fit for this coast?**
A primary water-separating filter with a vacuum gauge, and adequate power
margin. There is no sheltered water to limp home through.

**What spares should I carry for Rottnest?**
Impeller and cover gasket, fuel filters, belts, hoses, and the tools to fit
them.

**How long do parts take?**
What is not held in WA comes across the Nullarbor. Ask what is on the shelf
before authorising work.

**Is the entrance hard on engines?**
The issue is the transition — cold to working load in minutes, with no
sheltered water outside.
