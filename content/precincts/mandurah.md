# Marine diesel at Mandurah

An estuary and canal system with ocean access, an hour south of Perth, holding
one of Western Australia's largest recreational fleets.

## Two waters, and the anode question

Mandurah's boats live in the Peel-Harvey estuary, the canal developments, or on
the ocean side — and those are genuinely different electrolytes.

The estuary is **brackish and variable**, changing with rainfall, river inflow
and the state of the entrance. The ocean side is full salinity. A boat that
moves between them, which many do, sits in a shifting environment.

That makes anode alloy a decision rather than a default:

- **Magnesium** is fresh water only and wastes very fast in salt
- **Zinc** performs poorly in brackish and fresh, becoming passivated
- **Aluminium** works across a wider range and usually suits a boat that moves

If your anodes are behaving oddly — lasting far longer than expected, or
disappearing far faster — the alloy and the water are the first place to look.
See [why anodes matter](/guides/why-anodes-matter/).

## Shallow, warm estuary water

The estuary is shallow and warm, which is an easier cooling environment than the
tropics but harder than the open ocean. It also carries more suspended material
than the ocean side, so strainers and heat exchangers need attention on a
shorter cycle for an estuary-berthed boat than an ocean-berthed one.

## Canal running is light loading

The same pattern as every canal estate: slow runs through the system, a period
at anchor or offshore, slow runs back. Prolonged light loading glazes bores and
builds carbon.

Boats that go out through the entrance and work offshore have a healthier engine
life than boats that never leave the canals, and the owners of the latter should
run their engines up deliberately.

## The entrance

Mandurah's ocean access opens to the Indian Ocean, and the same rules apply as
at Fremantle and Hillarys: the sea breeze builds, the tank gets stirred, and
power margin and fuel filtration matter.

## Parts

An hour from Perth, which is where parts come from — and Perth is where things
come to from across the Nullarbor. Two links in the chain rather than one. Ask
about lead time before authorising work.

## Frequently asked questions

**What anode alloy should I use at Mandurah?**
It depends where the boat actually sits and whether it moves between estuary and
ocean. Aluminium usually suits a boat that moves; magnesium is fresh-only and
zinc performs poorly in brackish.

**Is estuary water harder on cooling than the ocean?**
It is shallower, warmer and carries more suspended material, so raw water
circuits need attention on a shorter cycle.

**Is canal running bad for the engine?**
Prolonged light loading glazes bores and builds carbon. Run the engine up under
real load regularly.

**How long do parts take here?**
They come from Perth, and Perth gets what it does not hold from across the
Nullarbor. Ask about lead time before work starts.

**What do I need for going outside?**
Power margin and a primary water-separating filter with a vacuum gauge. The
Indian Ocean rules apply.
