# Marine diesel at Coomera

Coomera is where Australian boats go when the job is too big for the berth. The
stretch of the Coomera River between the M1 and the Broadwater holds the
country's densest concentration of marine industry, and the reason owners travel
to it is simple: this is where engines come out.

## Why boats come here

In most Australian ports, the hard part of an engine job is not the engine — it
is getting it out of the boat and back in. Travel lifts, hardstand, crane
access and crews who do that weekly are the scarce resource nationally, and
here they are ordinary.

That changes which jobs are practical. A repower that means a six-week wait for
a lift elsewhere is scheduled work here, and a rebuild does not stall waiting
for components to travel to a machine shop.

## The water

The Coomera River is a working waterway rather than a scenic one: shallow in
places, tidal, and carrying silt from upstream. Boats berthed along it draw a
dirtier raw water supply than a Broadwater-moored boat does, and after heavy
rain considerably dirtier.

For an engine that means the raw water circuit is the thing to watch. Strainers
fill faster than owners expect, and heat exchangers foul from the inside on a
shorter cycle than a manual's generic interval assumes.

The river is also **draft-limited in sections**. If your boat is deep, confirm
access to the specific facility and the tide window before committing to a
booking — this is a real constraint here and it catches visiting owners.

## What the concentration gives you

**Specialists rather than generalists.** Injection, turbocharger, machining and
gearbox work exist as separate trades here, each with people who do only that.
In most Australian ports one workshop covers all of it competently; here you can
go to someone who does nothing else.

**Comparison quotes without travel.** On a job of any size, two or three are
available within a short radius. Most owners underuse this.

**A used and exchange market that actually functions.** Constant repower work
produces cores, and cores are what make exchange pricing possible.

## Getting the timing right

The precinct runs on a calendar. Boat show periods and the build-up to summer
fill diaries, and a job wanted in November has been competing for a slot since
August.

The quieter months are when yards have capacity and quote sharply. For an owner
prepared to plan, that is the single largest saving available here.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do owners travel to Coomera for engine work?**
Because engine removal is routine here. Travel lifts, hardstand and crews who do
it weekly are the constraint in most other ports.

**Is there a draft limit on the Coomera River?**
Sections are shallow and tidal. Confirm access and the tide window for your
specific facility before booking if your boat is deep.

**Does river berthing affect servicing?**
Yes. The river carries silt, especially after rain, so strainers and heat
exchangers need attention on a shorter cycle than a Broadwater boat.

**When should I book?**
Outside the summer build-up and boat show periods. Booking a spring slot in late
winter is normal practice.

**Can I get specialist work done here?**
Unusually easily. Injection, turbo, machining and gearbox work exist as separate
trades rather than as sidelines.
