# Marine diesel at Middle Harbour

Sheltered, deep, close to the city and almost entirely mooring-based. Middle
Harbour is where a great many Sydney boats sit for most of their lives, and how
they sit is what shapes their engine problems.

## A fleet that mostly waits

Boats here are used in bursts — a weekend, a run down the harbour, a trip out
through the Heads a few times a season. Between those, they sit on a mooring
with the engine off.

That produces the deterioration pattern this site returns to constantly, and
Middle Harbour concentrates it:

**Time-based damage, not wear.** Oil degrading by acid and moisture absorption,
water condensing in the fuel tank as the hull heats and cools each day,
impellers taking a set, coolant inhibitors depleting. The hour meter records
none of it.

**Anodes dissolving with the engine off.** Galvanic action does not pause for
the owner's schedule.

**A first-start problem every season**, which is where a winter of doing nothing
announces itself.

Servicing by the calendar rather than by the meter is the single most useful
habit for a boat kept here.

## Mooring access decides the job

There is limited on-site workshop capacity around Middle Harbour, and most work
happens where the boat lies:

**Mobile is the norm.** Servicing, diagnostics, impellers and fault-finding are
done from a tender, and the local trade is set up for exactly that.

**Weather is a scheduling variable.** A strong southerly that stops a tender
stops the job. Build that into expectations rather than treating a rescheduled
appointment as a failure.

**Anything below the waterline means moving the boat.** There is no slipway
here; the yard is elsewhere and the delivery passage is part of the job.

## Going outside

Boats from here reach the Heads quickly, and the water on the other side is
nothing like the harbour. The failure that catches owners is fuel: a tank that
has sat for months, stirred by ocean motion, delivering its sediment to the
filters at the least convenient moment.

If the boat has been sitting and you are planning a trip outside, deal with the
fuel before the trip rather than after the tow.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is mobile servicing normal at Middle Harbour?**
It is the default. Most of the fleet is on moorings and there is limited on-site
workshop capacity.

**Why does weather delay my service?**
Tender-based work depends on being able to get to the boat safely. It is a
normal constraint here, not poor scheduling.

**My boat barely runs. Does it still need servicing?**
Yes, and more than an owner expects. Oil, fuel, coolant, impellers and anodes
all degrade on the calendar with the engine off.

**What happens when I take it outside the Heads?**
Ocean motion stirs a tank that has been still for months. If the boat has sat,
deal with the fuel system before the trip.

**Where do I go for haul-out?**
Elsewhere. There is no slipway here, so the delivery passage is part of the job.
