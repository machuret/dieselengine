# Marine diesel engines for Beneteau yachts

Beneteau and Jeanneau between them account for an enormous share of the
Australian production cruising fleet, and although the two are now part of the
same group, the boats reach a workshop with slightly different habits.

## The usual arrangement

Beneteau's cruising range has been delivered predominantly with **Yanmar
saildrive auxiliaries**, with Volvo Penta on a number of models and in certain
markets and periods. The First performance range and the Oceanis cruising range
do not always share the same package.

Confirm yours from the plate. Options existed, model years changed, and
Australian deliveries are not always identical to European ones.

## What comes up on these boats

**The saildrive diaphragm.** As with any saildrive boat, this is the item that
matters most and the one owners most often cannot date. It is a rubber component
below the waterline holding back the sea, with a published time-based
replacement interval and a haul-out to change it.

**Leg corrosion.** Aluminium leg, warm marina water, and often shore power.
Anodes go quickly, and the wrong antifouling on the leg accelerates the problem
rather than solving it.

**Sail-drive versus shaft on older hulls.** Some earlier Beneteaus, and some
larger and more traditional models, are shaft-driven. That changes the
maintenance profile completely — stern gland and cutless bearing instead of a
diaphragm — so establish which you have before applying any general advice.

**Engine bay ventilation.** These are boats designed for European conditions and
sailed in Australian ones. On a hot day in a Queensland marina an engine box
that is adequate in the Mediterranean can run hotter than intended. Worth
checking rather than assuming.

**Motoring hours versus sailing hours.** A cruising Beneteau often motors more
than its owner admits — in and out of marinas, through calms, up rivers — and
much of that is at low load. Prolonged light loading glazes bores. Running the
engine up to a real working load regularly is worth doing deliberately.

## Repowering a Beneteau

Saildrive boats are constrained to the drive family unless you are prepared for
hull and mounting work. Shaft boats are much more open.

The practical questions:

- Leg condition, and whether it is included in the quote
- Diaphragm replacement as part of the job
- Whether the existing propeller — often folding or feathering — still suits
- Instrument panel and loom
- Engine bay access and whether the companionway structure has to come out

## Frequently asked questions

**What engine is in my Beneteau?**
Most Australian Beneteaus carry Yanmar saildrive auxiliaries, with Volvo Penta
on a number of models. Check the plate on the block.

**Is my Beneteau a saildrive or a shaft boat?**
Look under the engine: a saildrive drops vertically through the hull with the
propeller directly below and behind; a shaft runs aft at an angle through a
stern gland. The maintenance is completely different.

**How often should the saildrive diaphragm be replaced?**
At the manufacturer's time-based interval for your leg. Unknown history should
be treated as overdue.

**Why do my leg anodes disappear so fast?**
Warm marina water, shore power and sometimes a neighbouring boat. If the rate
changed suddenly, investigate for stray current rather than fitting more.

**Should I worry about motoring at low revs?**
Prolonged light loading glazes bores and builds carbon. Give the engine real
load regularly rather than only idling in and out of the marina.
