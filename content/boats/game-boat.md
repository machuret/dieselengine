# Marine diesel engines for game boats

A game boat is used in a way that sits much closer to commercial operation than
to recreational boating, and almost every engine decision follows from that one
fact.

## The duty cycle

A day's game fishing looks like this: a long run offshore at cruise, hours of
low-speed work while trolling or fighting a fish, and a long run home, often into
weather. Repeat through a season.

That is sustained load punctuated by extended low-load running, in warm water,
with real distance from help. Compare it with a harbour cruiser doing two hours
at a third of rated power on a Sunday.

## What follows from it

**Duty rating.** Manufacturers rate engines for recreational, light commercial or
continuous duty. A game boat used seriously is asking for light commercial
duty at minimum. Buying a recreational-rated engine and using it this way affects
warranty and, if there is any charter work, compliance.

**Hours accumulate quickly** — and under real load, which is generally kinder to
an engine than idling. The hour meter on a game boat means something different
from the same number on a cruiser.

**Cooling has to work hard.** Sustained high load in warm water is the hardest
condition a heat exchanger meets. Servicing intervals should follow local
conditions, not the generic manual figure.

**The trolling problem.** Hours at very low load, in gear, are exactly the
pattern that glazes bores and builds carbon. It is unavoidable in this fishery,
which is why running the engines up properly on the way home matters.

**Fuel filtration is a safety system.** Offshore, in a seaway, with a tank being
stirred — this is where contamination reaches the filters. Primary
water-separating filters with vacuum gauges, and a tank you have actually looked
inside. See
[marine diesel fuel quality and storage](/guides/marine-fuel-quality-australia/).

**Redundancy.** Twins are the norm for a reason. On a single, the get-home plan
needs to be real.

## Buying and repowering

- Size for the design, with margin for coming home into weather
- Match the duty rating to actual use, honestly
- Twin propellers, twin panels, twin looms — quote per engine
- Exhaust systems on older game boats are frequently at the end of their lives
- Establish the fuel tank condition before blaming the engine

## Frequently asked questions

**What engine rating does a game boat need?**
Light commercial duty at minimum for serious use. Recreational ratings do not
match the operating pattern.

**Do high hours matter on a game boat?**
Less than on a cruiser. Hours under real load are kinder than long idle periods.
Records and condition matter more than the meter.

**Why does trolling damage engines?**
Extended low-load running glazes bores and builds carbon. It is inherent to the
fishery — run the engines up properly on the way home.

**What is the most common offshore engine failure?**
Fuel starvation from a stirred, contaminated tank blocking filters. It is a tank
problem presenting as an engine problem.

**Does charter work change anything?**
Substantially. Duty rating, warranty and, if the vessel is in survey, the whole
installation. Tell the dealer honestly what the boat will do.
