# Marine diesel engines for Grand Banks trawlers

Grand Banks covers two quite different eras of boat, and the engine advice
diverges completely between them.

## The two eras

**The classic timber and early GRP trawlers**, many decades old now, were built
as displacement cruisers with modest power. Ford Lehman, Perkins and Caterpillar
units are common in the older Australian fleet, generally on shaft drive,
single or twin.

**The modern Grand Banks**, semi-displacement and faster, carries considerably
more power, commonly **Cummins, Caterpillar or Volvo Penta**, on shaft.

Which one you own changes almost everything below. Check the plate.

## Classic Grand Banks: the parts question

An engine that has been out of production for decades raises a real ownership
issue: **can you still get parts?**

Some of these engines remain remarkably well supported through specialists and
the industrial base of the block they were derived from. Others are getting hard.
This is the single most important thing to establish before committing to a
rebuild rather than a repower, and it is exactly the situation our
[repower or rebuild guide](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/) is about.

Other classic-era considerations:

- **Timber hull movement** affects shaft alignment more than a GRP hull does.
  Alignment checks matter more, not less.
- **Original wiring looms and instruments** are at the end of their lives.
- **Raw water systems** decades old have often been repaired rather than
  renewed.
- **Sound insulation** is frequently degraded and sometimes hazardous to
  disturb on very old boats — worth a professional opinion before you start
  pulling it out.

## Modern Grand Banks

Conventional twin shaft installation with contemporary engines and good dealer
support. The considerations are the standard ones for a semi-displacement
cruiser: alignment, raw water side servicing on an Australian schedule, and
twin-engine costs.

## Access

Grand Banks engine rooms, in both eras, are better than most production boats of
comparable size — the trawler layout puts the machinery under the saloon with
real access. That is a genuine saving on labour.

## Frequently asked questions

**What engine is in my Grand Banks?**
The classic boats commonly carry Ford Lehman, Perkins or Caterpillar; the modern
ones Cummins, Caterpillar or Volvo Penta. The plate on the block is
authoritative and the eras are very different.

**Can I still get parts for a classic Grand Banks engine?**
It depends entirely on the engine. Some are well supported through specialists;
others are getting difficult. Establish this before choosing rebuild over
repower.

**Does a timber hull affect the engine installation?**
It affects alignment. A hull that moves needs alignment checked more often than
a rigid GRP one.

**Is engine access good?**
Better than most production boats of similar size, in both eras. The trawler
layout puts the machinery where you can reach it.

**Is repowering a classic Grand Banks worth it?**
Often, when parts for the original engine are becoming scarce and the
surrounding systems are the same age. Compare it properly rather than on engine
price alone.
