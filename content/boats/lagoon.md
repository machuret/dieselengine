# Marine diesel engines for Lagoon catamarans

Lagoon is the largest production cruising catamaran builder in the world, and a
substantial part of the Australian cruising cat fleet, including a good deal of
ex-charter tonnage.

## The usual arrangement

Lagoons are delivered with **twin engines on saildrives**, most commonly
Yanmar, with Volvo Penta on parts of the range and in certain periods. Engine
sizes step up with model size across the range.

Verify from the plates — there are two, one per hull, and they should match.

## Ex-charter boats are the local issue

A large share of the Lagoons sold in Australia have been through a charter fleet
somewhere in the world. That is not automatically bad — charter boats are
serviced to a schedule because downtime costs money — but it produces a specific
condition profile:

- **High hours**, often accumulated in warm tropical water
- **Heavy low-speed manoeuvring**, which is hard on gearboxes and control cables
- **A lot of engine-charging hours**, which is low-load running
- **Legs that have worked hard** in warm marina water
- **Documented servicing that stops at handover**, with a gap afterwards

The question on any ex-charter Lagoon is not the hour meter. It is the records,
the leg condition, the diaphragm dates and what happened between the fleet
selling it and you looking at it.

## What comes up on these boats

**Two diaphragms, and their age.** The single most important thing to establish.

**Leg anodes in the tropics.** Ex-charter boats from the Caribbean, the Med or
Southeast Asia have spent their lives in warm water. Anode and leg condition
should be assessed, not assumed.

**Engine bay heat.** Lagoons place engines in the aft sections of each hull.
Access is generally good; ventilation on a hot Australian day is worth checking,
particularly on a boat that motors in still air.

**The generator, where fitted.** A separate engine with its own raw water
circuit, impeller, anodes and service schedule, and it is routinely forgotten
until it stops.

**Charging strategy.** Owners who run engines for power rather than propulsion
accumulate low-load hours, which glazes bores. If that is your pattern, run them
up under real load regularly.

## Repowering a Lagoon

Twin saildrive installation, so both engines and both legs are in scope, and the
drive family constrains the engine choice unless you take on hull work.

Ask for the quote itemised per hull, and establish whether legs, diaphragms,
propellers, panels and looms are included.

## Frequently asked questions

**What engines do Lagoon catamarans have?**
Twin saildrive auxiliaries, most commonly Yanmar with Volvo Penta on parts of
the range. Check both plates — there is one engine per hull.

**Is an ex-charter Lagoon a bad buy?**
Not automatically. Charter fleets service to a schedule. What matters is the
records, the leg and diaphragm condition, and what happened after the fleet sold
it.

**How do I assess a Lagoon's saildrives?**
Diaphragm replacement dates, leg oil condition, anode wastage, and whether the
legs have been antifouled with an aluminium-safe system.

**Why do the engines have high hours but low sea miles?**
Charter and liveaboard use accumulates hours charging and manoeuvring at low
load, which is not the same duty as propulsion.

**Should I service the generator at the same time?**
Yes. It is a separate engine with its own raw water circuit, impeller and
anodes, and it is the most commonly neglected item on these boats.
