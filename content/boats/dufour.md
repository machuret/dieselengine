# Marine diesel engines for Dufour yachts

Dufour sits slightly to the performance side of the French production builders,
and the Australian fleet is smaller than Jeanneau's or Beneteau's — which is
itself the most practically important fact about owning one here.

## The usual arrangement

Dufour cruisers are delivered predominantly with **Volvo Penta or Yanmar
saildrive auxiliaries**, sized to the boat. Both appear across the range
depending on model and period.

Check the plate on the block.

## The smaller-fleet consequence

A less common boat means fewer workshops that have seen one, and it shows up in
two places:

**Boat-specific knowledge is thinner.** Where a mechanic can tell you from
memory how a Jeanneau's exhaust elbow comes out, the same person may be working
it out on your Dufour. Budget the diagnostic time honestly.

**Engine parts are not the problem.** The engines themselves are mainstream
Volvo Penta and Yanmar units with full Australian support. It is boat-specific
items — panels, mouldings, proprietary fittings — that are slower.

That is a reasonable trade. The expensive parts are well supported; the
inconvenient ones are the trim.

## What comes up

**The saildrive diaphragm**, time-based interval, haul-out job, and frequently
undated on a second-hand boat.

**Leg anodes and antifouling.** Aluminium leg in warm water; aluminium-safe
antifouling only.

**Performance-cruiser use pattern.** Dufour owners tend to sail. Low motoring
hours over a long calendar life is the local risk, not high hours.

**Engine access** through the companionway, with the usual difficulty at the
back of the engine.

## Repowering a Dufour

Saildrive constrains you to the drive family unless you take on hull work. On a
less common boat it is worth choosing a workshop that will document what they
did, because the next mechanic will not have seen one either.

## Frequently asked questions

**What engine is in my Dufour?**
Volvo Penta or Yanmar saildrive auxiliaries are both common. The plate is
authoritative.

**Are Dufours harder to service in Australia?**
The engines are mainstream and well supported. Boat-specific parts and
boat-specific familiarity are thinner than for the larger production brands.

**Does the saildrive limit my repower options?**
Yes — the leg and engine are a family, and changing brand usually means changing
the leg and hull mounting.

**What is the main risk on a second-hand Dufour?**
An undated saildrive diaphragm, and time-based deterioration from low motoring
hours over a long life.

**Should I keep records?**
More than usual. On a less common boat, your documentation is what the next
mechanic will work from.
