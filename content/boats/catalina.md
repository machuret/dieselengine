# Marine diesel engines for Catalina yachts

Catalina is the great American production cruiser, and the boats in Australia
differ from their European counterparts in one structurally important way:
**many of them are shaft-driven, not saildrive.**

## The usual arrangement

Catalina's cruising range has commonly used **shaft-driven auxiliaries**, with
Universal (Kubota-based), Yanmar and Westerbeke units appearing across the years.
Some models and later boats use saildrives.

Establish which you have. It changes the maintenance profile entirely.

## Why shaft drive matters here

A shaft-driven auxiliary has:

- **No rubber diaphragm** holding back the sea, and therefore no time-based
  replacement item with a haul-out attached
- **A stern gland and cutless bearing** as the wear items instead
- **Far more freedom in a repower** — you are not tied to a drive family
- **Alignment** as a periodic requirement

For an owner, this generally means lower and more predictable below-waterline
maintenance than the French saildrive fleet. It is one of the genuine
attractions of these boats.

## The Kubota point

Many Universal engines in this fleet are marinised Kubota industrial blocks. That
is good news for parts: the base engine has an enormous industrial support
network in Australia, and many wear items are available well beyond the marine
channel.

Marine-specific parts — heat exchanger, exhaust manifold, raw water pump — come
through the marine chain. Knowing which is which saves real money.

## What comes up

**Stern gland condition.** Traditional packing glands drip by design; dripless
seals do not. Establish which you have and service it accordingly.

**Cutless bearing wear**, which shows as shaft play and vibration.

**Raw water side on an older engine.** These boats are often decades old and the
cooling circuit has frequently been repaired rather than renewed.

**Engine access.** Under the companionway steps, with the usual constraint at the
back of the engine.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is my Catalina a saildrive or a shaft boat?**
Many are shaft-driven, which is unusual among production cruisers of the era.
Look under the engine: a shaft runs aft at an angle through a stern gland.

**What engine is in my Catalina?**
Universal, Yanmar and Westerbeke units all appear. Many Universals are marinised
Kubota blocks.

**Does a Kubota base engine mean cheaper parts?**
For base engine items, often yes — the industrial support network is large.
Marine-specific parts still come through the marine chain.

**Is a shaft boat cheaper to maintain than a saildrive?**
Generally, below the waterline. No diaphragm interval, and the wear items are a
stern gland and cutless bearing.

**Can I repower more freely on a shaft boat?**
Yes. You are not tied to a drive family, so the engine choice is far more open.
