# Marine diesel engines for Leopard catamarans

Leopard cats are built in South Africa and a very large proportion of them have
been through a charter fleet. For an Australian buyer, that history is the whole
story.

## The usual arrangement

Twin engines, one per hull, predominantly **Yanmar saildrive auxiliaries**, with
Volvo Penta appearing across parts of the range. Sizes step with model.

Check both plates.

## The charter fleet reality

Leopard's relationship with the large charter operators means much of the fleet
enters private ownership after several seasons of commercial use. That produces
a specific and predictable condition profile:

**High hours in warm water.** Caribbean, Bahamas, Mediterranean, Southeast Asia.
Warm water is the hardest condition for heat exchangers and the fastest for
anode consumption.

**Enormous low-load charging hours.** Charter guests use power. Engines run at
anchor to charge, which is exactly the light-load pattern that glazes bores.

**Heavy manoeuvring wear.** Charter crews and guests manoeuvre on differential
thrust constantly. Gearbox clutch packs and control cables take that.

**Legs that have worked.** Warm water, shore power, and antifouling applied
under time pressure at turnaround.

**A documentation cliff.** Fleet service records are usually good and usually
stop at handover. The gap between fleet sale and your inspection is the unknown.

## What to check on an ex-charter Leopard

1. **Diaphragm dates on both legs.** The single most important item.
2. **Leg oil on both** — milky means the lower seal is compromised.
3. **Anode wastage**, and what alloy has been used.
4. **Antifouling on the legs** — copper-based on bare aluminium is damaging.
5. **Both gearboxes** — engagement speed, oil condition, slip under load.
6. **Compression or leak-down**, given the charging hours.
7. **The generator**, which has usually worked harder than either main engine.
8. **Heat exchangers**, after years in tropical water.

## Is it a bad buy?

No — an ex-charter cat that is properly assessed and properly recommissioned can
be excellent value, and the fleet maintained it to a schedule that many private
owners do not match. What is a bad buy is an ex-charter cat bought on the
brochure and the hour meter.

## Frequently asked questions

**What engines are in Leopard catamarans?**
Twin saildrive auxiliaries, predominantly Yanmar with Volvo Penta on parts of the
range. Check both plates.

**Should I avoid an ex-charter Leopard?**
No. Assess it properly — legs, diaphragms, gearboxes, compression, heat
exchangers and the generator — and price the recommissioning honestly.

**Why are the hours so high relative to the miles?**
Charging at anchor. Charter use accumulates low-load engine hours that have
nothing to do with distance travelled.

**What is the most expensive thing to get wrong?**
The saildrive legs. Warm-water corrosion on an aluminium casting is expensive,
and the diaphragms are a safety item.

**Does the generator matter?**
On a charter cat it has usually worked harder than the main engines. Assess it
as seriously.
