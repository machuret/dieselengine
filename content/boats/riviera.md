# Marine diesel engines for Riviera boats

Riviera is Australia's largest luxury boat builder, built at Coomera on the Gold
Coast, and the domestic fleet is large enough that most marine workshops on the
east coast see them constantly.

## The usual arrangement

Riviera flybridge and sports yachts are delivered with **twin shaft-driven
diesels**, and the supplier has varied across the range and across the years —
**Volvo Penta, Cummins and Caterpillar** are all well represented in the
Australian fleet, with Volvo Penta IPS on parts of the more recent range.

The specific package depends on model, year and what the original owner
specified. The plates on the blocks are the answer.

## What is good about these boats

**They were built for these conditions.** A boat designed and built on the Gold
Coast for Australian owners has engine room ventilation, cooling and
installation detail suited to warm water and hot engine bays. That is not true of
every import.

**Local support is deep.** Parts, service knowledge and factory proximity are
all better than for most imported brands, particularly on the east coast.

**Access is generally considered.** Engine rooms on the larger models are
genuinely workable spaces rather than afterthoughts.

## What comes up

**Twin everything.** Two engines, two gearboxes, two shafts, two sets of running
gear. Servicing is quoted per engine and the total surprises people.

**Sustained cruise load.** These are boats that run at cruise for hours on
passage up and down the coast. That is what the engines are for, and it means
the raw water side works hard and the servicing intervals for cooling matter.

**Shaft alignment.** Shaft-driven flybridge boats need alignment checked
periodically, after any grounding and after new mounts. Symptoms show up as
vibration, a stern gland that will not settle, and coupling bolts loosening.

**Warm water cooling.** Gold Coast, Moreton Bay and Queensland cruising water is
warm and often silty. Heat exchangers and strainers need attention on the local
schedule, not the generic one.

**IPS models are different.** Pod drives are proprietary, dealer-serviced and
have their own schedule. If your boat has IPS, the drive maintenance is a
separate and significant line — see
[shaft vs sterndrive vs IPS](/guides/shaft-vs-sterndrive-vs-ips/).

## Repowering a Riviera

An older Riviera with tired engines is a common repower candidate, and it is a
substantial job: twin installation, shaft work, exhaust, panels and looms,
usually propellers.

The advantage is that the local trade does these regularly and quotes them with
confidence. Get the quote itemised per engine and confirm the shaft, propeller
and exhaust scope.

## Frequently asked questions

**What engines are in Riviera boats?**
Twin shaft diesels, commonly Volvo Penta, Cummins or Caterpillar depending on
model and year, with Volvo Penta IPS on parts of the recent range. The plates
are authoritative.

**Are Riviera boats well supported in Australia?**
Unusually so. They are built at Coomera and the east coast trade sees them
constantly, so parts and service knowledge are readily available.

**How often should a Riviera's cooling system be serviced?**
On a schedule suited to warm, sometimes silty Queensland water rather than a
generic figure. Heat exchangers and strainers are the items that matter.

**Why does my Riviera vibrate at cruise?**
Shaft alignment, propeller damage or mount condition are the usual candidates on
a shaft-driven flybridge boat. It is worth chasing rather than living with.

**Is repowering an older Riviera worth it?**
It is a common and well-understood job locally. Compare it against a rebuild
with the deferred items added — see our repower or rebuild guide.
