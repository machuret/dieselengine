# Marine diesel engines by boat brand

The engine in your boat was chosen by the builder, not by you, and that choice
came with a set of consequences you inherited: how the engine is accessed, what
drive it turns, how it is cooled, and which jobs on it are easy or miserable.

These pages cover the pattern for each builder and hull type — what engines
they typically carry in Australia, how the installation is arranged, what tends
to need attention, and what to know before repowering.

## Read this before the brand page

**Your boat's actual engine is on the plate, not on this page.** Builders
changed suppliers between model years, offered options, and sold the same hull
with different packages in different markets. Australian-delivered boats
sometimes differ from European or US ones.

So every one of these pages describes a pattern, and every one of them tells you
the same thing: find the engine's serial number plate and work from that.
Anyone quoting parts will ask for it.

## What actually varies between builders

**The drive.** Saildrive or shaft on a sailing boat; shaft, sterndrive, IPS or
pod on a powerboat. This is the single biggest difference in what you will
spend on maintenance — see
[shaft vs sterndrive vs IPS](/guides/shaft-vs-sterndrive-vs-ips/) and
[how a saildrive works](/guides/how-a-saildrive-works/).

**Access.** Some builders put the engine under a lifting saloon sole with room
to stand beside it. Others buried it under a companionway step where you cannot
reach the back of it. Access determines the labour on every job for the life of
the boat, and it is the first thing to look at.

**Single or twin.** Redundancy, manoeuvring, and double the servicing.

**Engine room ventilation and insulation**, which vary enormously and matter
more in Australian conditions than in the European ones many of these boats
were designed for.

**How much the builder integrated.** A boat with proprietary panels, looms and
controls constrains what you can repower to. A boat with a generic installation
does not.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find out what engine is in my boat?**
Look for the manufacturer's plate on the engine block — it carries the model and
serial number. That is the authoritative answer, and it is what any parts
supplier will ask for.

**Why does the engine in my boat differ from what these pages say?**
Builders changed suppliers between model years and offered options, and
Australian-delivered boats sometimes differ from those sold elsewhere. These
pages describe the common pattern, not your specific hull.

**Can I fit a different brand of engine to my boat?**
Often, subject to the beds, the space, the drive, the exhaust and the controls.
It is an installation question rather than a catalogue one — see
[repower or rebuild](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/).

**Does the boat builder affect what servicing costs?**
Substantially, through access. The same job on the same engine can take twice
as long in a boat where you cannot reach the back of it.

**Are these pages a substitute for the owner's manual?**
No. They cover what is common across a builder's boats and what to watch for.
Your engine's own manual sets the intervals and specifications.
