# Marine diesel engines for Steber boats

Steber has been building at Taree in New South Wales for decades, and the boats
turn up in commercial survey, charter, patrol and rescue service around the
Australian coast as well as in private hands.

## The usual arrangement

Stebers are **shaft-driven**, single or twin depending on model and role, and
the engine package varies widely because so many were built to a customer's
specification for a particular job. **Cummins, Caterpillar, Volvo Penta,
Yanmar, Iveco and Scania** all appear in the fleet.

There is no single answer for what is in a Steber. The plate is the answer, and
on a commercial boat the survey documentation should record it too.

## Built for work

The distinguishing thing about these hulls is that a large share of them work
for a living. That changes what matters:

**Duty rating is a real question.** A boat in commercial survey needs an engine
rated for its actual operating pattern. A recreational rating in a working boat
affects both warranty and compliance — see
[engine requirements for commercial vessel survey](/guides/commercial-vessel-survey-requirements/).

**Survey covers the installation, not just the engine.** Fuel shut-offs
accessible from outside the space, guarding, exhaust insulation, bilge pumping
and ventilation are all inspected. Changing the propulsion machinery changes the
surveyed installation, so the surveyor conversation happens before the purchase.

**Hours accumulate at a commercial rate**, and the maintenance record is a
compliance document rather than a nice-to-have.

**Downtime costs money**, which is why parts availability and local service
capability outweigh small differences in specification for an operator.

## Private ownership of an ex-commercial Steber

A common Australian purchase. Points to establish:

- What the boat did, and where
- The maintenance record, which should exist if it was in survey
- Whether the engine is at a commercial or recreational rating
- Condition of the raw water side after years of hard use
- Whether the boat is coming out of survey, and what that means for you

An ex-survey boat with a complete record is often a better proposition than a
private boat of the same age with none.

## Frequently asked questions

**What engine is in a Steber?**
It varies widely, because many were built to a customer specification. Cummins,
Caterpillar, Volvo Penta, Yanmar, Iveco and Scania all appear. Check the plate.

**Are Stebers commercial vessels?**
Many are, in charter, patrol and rescue roles. Private examples exist too, and
plenty are ex-commercial.

**What should I check on an ex-survey Steber?**
The maintenance record, what the boat did and where, the engine's duty rating,
and the condition of the raw water side after commercial use.

**Do I need to talk to a surveyor before repowering?**
If the vessel is or will be in survey, yes. Changing the propulsion machinery
changes the surveyed installation.

**Is an ex-commercial boat a good buy?**
Often, because survey requires a maintenance system. A documented ex-survey boat
can be a better proposition than an undocumented private one.
