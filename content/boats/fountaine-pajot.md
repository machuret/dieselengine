# Marine diesel engines for Fountaine Pajot catamarans

Fountaine Pajot builds both cruising sail cats and a power catamaran range, and
the two are entirely different engine propositions under the same badge.

## The sailing catamarans

Twin engines, one per hull, predominantly **Volvo Penta or Yanmar on
saildrives**, sized to the model. This is the standard cruising cat arrangement
and everything in our
[catamaran engines page](/marine-diesel-engine-for/catamaran/) applies: two of
every service item, two diaphragms, two sets of leg anodes.

Access in the aft hull engine bays is generally good, which offsets some of the
doubled cost.

## The power catamarans

A different proposition: considerably more power per hull, and the boats are
used more like a motor yacht — sustained cruise, real load, real hours.

For these, the things that matter shift towards what matters on any planing or
semi-displacement powerboat: cooling capacity under sustained load, fuel system
condition, and the drivetrain.

## The charter question

A meaningful share of Fountaine Pajots in the market have charter history, in the
Caribbean, the Mediterranean, Southeast Asia or the Whitsundays. As with any
ex-charter cat, the profile is high hours in warm water with heavy low-speed
manoeuvring and a lot of engine-charging time.

That is not disqualifying — charter fleets service to a schedule — but it makes
the leg condition, the diaphragm dates and the post-fleet maintenance gap the
things to investigate.

## What comes up

**Two diaphragms**, each with a time-based interval and a haul-out.

**Warm-water leg anodes**, particularly on boats coming from tropical charter
grounds.

**Uneven engine hours**, from favouring one side for manoeuvring or charging.

**The generator**, a separate diesel with its own raw water circuit, impeller and
anodes, and routinely forgotten.

**Gearbox and control cables**, worked hard by differential-thrust manoeuvring.

## Frequently asked questions

**What engines do Fountaine Pajot catamarans have?**
The sailing cats carry twin Volvo Penta or Yanmar saildrive auxiliaries. The
power cats carry considerably larger engines. Check both plates.

**Is an ex-charter Fountaine Pajot a good buy?**
It can be. Charter fleets maintain to schedule. Investigate the leg condition,
the diaphragm dates and what happened after the fleet sold it.

**Why do my two engines have different hours?**
Owners and charter crews favour one side for manoeuvring and charging. The
lower-hour engine has been sitting.

**Do the power cats need different servicing?**
Yes. They run at sustained cruise load, so cooling capacity and fuel system
condition matter in a way they do not on a sail cat.

**What is most often neglected?**
The generator, and the second engine's saildrive leg.
