# Marine diesel engines for Caribbean boats

Caribbean is one of the longest-running Australian production names, built in
Sydney, and the fleet is spread right along the east coast doing exactly what it
was built for: offshore fishing and family cruising in real water.

## The usual arrangement

Caribbeans span a wide range of sizes and eras, and the engine packages vary
accordingly — **shaft-driven diesels, single or twin**, with Volvo Penta,
Yanmar, Cummins and others appearing, and a proportion of the smaller fleet
running petrol.

Many of the older boats have been repowered at least once. The plate is the only
reliable answer.

## An Australian boat, and what that means

These hulls were designed and built here for local conditions and are supported
by a trade that has seen thousands of them. Practically:

**Boat-specific knowledge is deep.** A workshop on the east coast has almost
certainly worked on your model. That reduces diagnostic time and it means the
awkward jobs are known quantities.

**Parts for boat items** — not engine parts, but the boat's own fittings — are
easier than for an import.

**The hulls outlive their engines.** A well-maintained Caribbean hull is
frequently on its second or third engine, which is why repowering these boats is
routine work rather than an unusual project.

## What comes up

**Repower history.** On any older Caribbean, establish what has been changed and
when. A mixed history of engine, exhaust, tanks and wiring from different decades
is common and worth mapping before you spend money.

**Fuel tanks.** Original tanks in older boats are a frequent source of the
persistent filter blockage and power loss that gets blamed on the engine.

**Exhaust and mixing elbows**, corroding as they do on every wet exhaust, and
often older than the engine.

**Offshore use.** These boats cross bars and fish offshore. Power margin, fuel
filtration and cooling under sustained load all matter more than they would on a
sheltered-water boat.

**Alignment**, on a shaft-driven hull that has been driven hard.

## Repowering a Caribbean

Routine, well understood by the local trade, and usually the right answer on an
older boat with a tired engine and a sound hull.

Get the scope stated: engine, beds, exhaust, tanks if they need it, wiring,
panels, propeller and shaft. On a boat this age the surrounding items are often
the larger half.

## Frequently asked questions

**What engine is in my Caribbean?**
It varies by size, era and repower history — shaft-driven diesels from several
makers, and petrol in parts of the smaller fleet. The plate is the only reliable
answer.

**Are Caribbeans easy to get serviced?**
Unusually so on the east coast. The trade has seen a great many of them.

**Is it worth repowering an older Caribbean?**
Frequently yes — the hulls outlast their engines and the job is routine work
locally.

**Why does my older Caribbean lose power in a chop?**
Very often the fuel tank rather than the engine. Motion stirs settled water and
sludge off the tank bottom into the pickup.

**What should a repower quote cover?**
Engine, beds, exhaust, wiring, panels, propeller and shaft, and tanks if they
need attention. On an older boat those often exceed the engine.
