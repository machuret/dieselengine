# Yanmar & Volvo Saildrives in Australia

A saildrive is not an engine — it is the leg that takes an engine's output
straight down through the hull. On most Australian production cruising yachts it
is the component that will cost you the most and worry you the least until it
does not.

## The common legs

**Yanmar SD20, SD25 and SD60**, and **Volvo Penta 130S** and relatives, under a
very large share of the Jeanneau, Beneteau, Bavaria, Hanse, Lagoon and Fountaine
Pajot fleet.

## The diaphragm is the whole subject

The leg passes through a hole in the hull, and what keeps the sea out is a
**rubber diaphragm** clamped between the leg housing and the hull.

It has a manufacturer-specified replacement interval measured in **years, not
hours**, and changing it is a haul-out job requiring the leg to be separated
from the hull.

On a second-hand production cruiser, its date is the single most important thing
to establish, and "I don't know" is the usual answer. See
[how to check a saildrive diaphragm](/how/how-to-check-a-saildrive-diaphragm/).

## The other four things

**Leg anodes.** An aluminium casting in warm Australian marina water, consumed
fast, protecting something worth more than a full engine service.

**Antifouling.** Copper-based antifouling on a bare aluminium leg causes exactly
the corrosion the anodes are fighting. Aluminium-safe systems and correct
priming matter.

**Leg oil.** Milky means water is entering, usually past the lower seal behind
the propeller. Fishing line wrapped behind the prop is a very common cause.

**The engine and leg are a family.** Repowering a saildrive boat generally means
staying within the drive manufacturer's range, or accepting hull and mounting
work. See [how a saildrive works](/guides/how-a-saildrive-works/).

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should a saildrive diaphragm be replaced?** At the manufacturer\u2019s interval for your leg, which is time-based rather than hours-based. Treat unknown history as overdue.

**Can the diaphragm be changed in the water?** No. It is a haul-out job.

**Why is my leg oil milky?** Water is entering, most often past the lower seal behind the propeller. Fishing line is a common cause.

**Can I antifoul the leg normally?** No. Copper-based antifouling on bare aluminium causes corrosion. Use an aluminium-safe system with correct priming.

**Can I fit a different engine brand to my saildrive?** Not straightforwardly. The leg and engine are a family, and changing brand usually means changing the leg and hull mounting.
