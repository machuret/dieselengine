# Mercruiser and Mercury Diesel in Australia

Mercury's Australian dealer network is one of the densest in boating — and most
of it exists to serve outboards and petrol sterndrives, which matters when you
are booking diesel work.

## Where you find them

**TDI 3.0 and 4.2, and the 3.6 QSD** — diesel sterndrive and inboard packages in
cruisers and sport boats.

The engines are built around blocks from established diesel manufacturers,
marinised and packaged with Mercury drives and controls. The engine and the
drive come from different engineering traditions, which means two support
questions rather than one.

## What owners should know

**Not every Mercury dealer does diesel.** Ask specifically. A workshop that
services outboards all day may have limited experience with a common-rail diesel
sterndrive package. See
[Mercruiser and Mercury Diesel distributors](/distributors/mercruiser/).

**The drive is where the maintenance is.** Most of these installations are
sterndrives, which carry the highest routine below-waterline maintenance of any
common arrangement:

- **Bellows** on a time-based interval — failure floods the boat
- **Gimbal bearing and universal joints** as wear items in salt water
- **Anodes** consumed rapidly on a permanently moored boat
- **Leg oil condition** as a diagnostic — milky means water is entering

See [shaft vs sterndrive vs IPS](/guides/shaft-vs-sterndrive-vs-ips/).

**Moored or trailered changes everything.** A sterndrive that lives in the water
needs a discipline a trailered one does not, and the difference in ownership cost
is substantial.

## What comes up in workshops

Bellows and gimbal service deferred past interval; anode consumption on moored
boats; leg oil showing water ingress; and diagnostic access for the
electronically controlled diesel packages.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is every Mercury dealer able to service my diesel sterndrive?**
No. Much of the network is oriented to outboards and petrol sterndrives. Confirm
diesel capability before booking.

**How often do bellows need replacing?**
At the manufacturer's time-based interval. A bellows failure sinks boats, so
unknown history should be treated as overdue.

**Are diesel sterndrives expensive to run?**
They carry the highest routine below-waterline maintenance of the common drive
arrangements, and deferring it has severe consequences.

**Does it matter if the boat is moored or trailered?**
Substantially. A moored leg corrodes and consumes anodes far faster.

**Where do the engines come from?**
Blocks from established diesel manufacturers, marinised and packaged with
Mercury drives and controls.
