# Bukh in Australia

Bukh built small marine diesels with a reputation for being almost impossible to
stop, and they remain in Australian cruising yachts and in lifeboat service
decades on.

## Where you find them

**DV10, DV20 and DV24** — small cruising yacht auxiliaries, and the lifeboat
engines that built the brand's reputation for starting in any conditions.

## What owners should know

**These are mechanical engines**, entirely free of electronics, which means an
experienced independent can work on one and there is no software access to
negotiate.

**Parts are the consideration.** The Australian network is thin and some parts
come from Europe. Establish availability and lead time before committing to a
job, and carry spares for offshore work.

**They are over-engineered for their output**, which is why so many survive.
Owners frequently find the surrounding installation — exhaust, loom, panel,
mounts, raw water circuit — has aged faster than the engine.

**The honest question on an old Bukh** is the same as on an old Perkins: whether
the whole installation is worth continuing to support, not whether the engine
will keep going. It probably will. See
[repower or rebuild](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Are Bukh parts still available in Australia?** The network is thin and some parts come from Europe. Confirm availability and lead time before booking work.

**Can an independent service a Bukh?** Yes. They are entirely mechanical with no software to license.

**Why do so many survive?** They were built substantially for their output, originally for lifeboat service where starting was non-negotiable.

**What usually needs attention first?** The installation around the engine — exhaust, loom, panel, mounts and raw water circuit — rather than the engine.

**Should I repower?** Often the engine is the soundest part of the boat. Compare the whole installation rather than the engine alone.
