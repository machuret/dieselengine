# Marine Generator Sets marine engines in Australia

A generator is a second diesel engine on your boat, with its own raw water
circuit, its own impeller, its own anodes and its own service schedule — and it
is the most reliably neglected machine aboard.

## The common names

**Onan, Northern Lights, Fischer Panda and Kohler** are the sets most often
encountered in the Australian fleet, across everything from cruising yachts to
large motor yachts.

## Why they get neglected

The generator does not move the boat, so nothing about a bad day forces you to
think about it. Then on a hot night with guests aboard and the air conditioning
running, it stops.

On a boat with full accommodation, the generator frequently accumulates **more
hours than either main engine**, and it does so at a steady load in a hot,
enclosed space.

## What it needs

- **Its own oil and filter schedule**, not the main engine's
- **Its own impeller** — a separate raw water pump with a separate interval
- **Its own anodes**, including any in its heat exchanger
- **Its own raw water strainer**, often a separate one
- **Load.** A generator run continuously at very light load wet-stacks, building
  unburnt fuel and carbon. Manufacturers specify a minimum useful load for a
  reason.

## What comes up in workshops

Impellers past interval; heat exchangers never cleaned; anodes never replaced;
wet stacking from prolonged light-load running; and exhaust systems corroding
exactly as a main engine's does.

Ask explicitly whether the generator is included in a service quote. It usually
is not — see [how to get a quote you can compare](/how/how-to-get-a-marine-engine-quote-you-can-compare/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the generator included in an engine service?** Usually not. It is a separate engine with a separate schedule. Ask explicitly.

**Why does my generator have more hours than the main engine?** On a boat with air conditioning and accommodation it runs at anchor and alongside, often far more than the mains.

**What is wet stacking?** Unburnt fuel and carbon accumulating from prolonged running at very light load. Generators need a minimum useful load.

**Does the generator have its own impeller?** Yes, and its own raw water pump, strainer and anodes.

**What fails most on marine generators?** Raw water circuits — impellers, heat exchangers and anodes — followed by exhaust corrosion.
