# Beta Marine engines in Australia

Beta Marine is the engine that goes into a boat designed for something else. In
the Australian sailing fleet it is overwhelmingly a repower engine, and that is
what it was built to be.

## The families

**Beta 14, 25, 35, 43 and 60** — cruising yacht auxiliaries, mostly shaft
drive, across a wide range of hulls.

Common in older Catalinas, Adams designs, timber cruisers and anything whose
original auxiliary is now unobtainable.

## Why it fits where others do not

The design decisions are all aimed at the retrofit problem:

**No engine management to integrate.** Mechanical across much of the range,
which means no proprietary network to splice into a boat that never had one.

**Adaptable mounting.** Designed to sit on beds that were cut for a different
engine, which is the single hardest part of most repowers.

**Simple panels and looms** rather than a manufacturer's display network.

**Serviceable by a competent owner**, which matters offshore and matters more
the further you cruise.

That combination is why these engines turn up in boats forty years older than
they are.

## What to know as an owner

**Follow the marine service schedule, not the industrial one.** The base engine
is a Kubota industrial block, and Kubota's intervals never contemplated a raw
water circuit, anodes or salt. Beta's schedule does — see
[Kubota marine base engines](/engine-brands/kubota-marine/) for what that split
means for parts.

**Carry the marine-specific spares.** Impeller and cover gasket, belts, filters.
For offshore work these are the items that are hard to find remotely, not the
base engine consumables.

**Keep your own records.** A repowered boat has a maintenance history that
starts at the repower, and yours is the only copy.

For sourcing and who works on them, see
[Beta Marine distributors](/distributors/beta-marine/).

## At repower time

If you are considering one, the questions that decide it are about the boat
rather than the engine: does it suit the existing beds, shaft angle and exhaust
run; what panel and loom comes with it; and does the output match what the hull
actually needs — see
[repower or rebuild](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are Beta engines popular for repowers?**
Mechanical, adaptable to existing beds and shafts, with simple panels and no
proprietary engine management to integrate into an older boat.

**Which service schedule should I follow?**
Beta's. The industrial schedule for the base engine does not account for the raw
water circuit, anodes or salt.

**What spares should I carry offshore?**
The marine-specific items — impeller and cover gasket, belts, filters — rather
than base engine consumables, which are more widely available.

**Will one fit my boat?**
It depends on the beds, shaft angle, exhaust run and required output. That is an
installation question rather than a catalogue one.

**Can an independent service one?**
Yes. Much of the range is mechanical with no software to license.
