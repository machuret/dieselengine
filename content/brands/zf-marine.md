# ZF marine gearboxes in Australia

ZF makes the gearbox behind a large share of Australian marine diesels. This
page is about the gearbox itself — what the families are, what goes wrong, and
what an owner should check.

## The families

**ZF 25, 45 and 63** — sailboat auxiliaries and small inboards.

**ZF 220 and 280** — larger recreational and light commercial installations.

The range absorbed the **Hurth** designs, so older Hurth-badged boxes are not
orphans — see [Hurth gearboxes](/engine-brands/hurth/).

## What the gearbox actually does

Three jobs: selects ahead, astern and neutral; reduces engine speed to a
propeller speed that works; and takes the propeller's thrust into the boat
through a thrust bearing.

That third job is why alignment matters so much. A box run behind a misaligned
shaft usually fails at the bearing rather than the clutches — see
[how a marine gearbox works](/guides/how-a-marine-gearbox-works/).

## The four things to check

**Oil type.** Some take automatic transmission fluid, some engine oil of a
specified grade. The wrong one causes clutch problems that look like mechanical
failure. The plate on the box is the authority.

**Oil condition.** Milky means water, almost always through a corroded oil
cooler in the raw water circuit — a part to replace, not flush. Dark and
burnt-smelling means clutch slip. Metallic glitter means internal wear.

**Engagement behaviour.** Slow engagement, slipping under load, or a delay of
several seconds points at low oil, a control cable short of full travel, or an
idle too low to build pressure — before it points at worn clutch packs.

**Alignment**, periodically and after any grounding or new mounts.

See [how to check and change marine gearbox oil](/how/how-to-check-and-change-marine-gearbox-oil/).

## The scheduling mistake

Your engine and gearbox usually come from different manufacturers and are
serviced by different specialists, which produces a recurring and expensive
misunderstanding: an owner books an engine service and assumes the gearbox is
included.

**It usually is not.** Ask explicitly.

For who supplies and services these boxes, see

## Frequently asked questions

**Is my Hurth gearbox still supported?**
Yes. Hurth designs were absorbed into the ZF range.

**What oil does my ZF take?**
It depends on the model — some automatic transmission fluid, some engine oil of
a stated grade. The plate on the box is the authority.

**Why is my gearbox oil milky?**
Water is entering, most often through a corroded oil cooler in the raw water
circuit. The cooler needs replacing, not flushing.

**My gearbox is slow to engage. Is it worn out?**
Not necessarily. Low oil, the wrong oil, a control cable short of full travel or
a low idle are all cheaper causes.

**Why do marine gearboxes fail at the bearing?**
The thrust bearing takes the propeller's push. A box run behind a misaligned
shaft fails there before the clutches.
