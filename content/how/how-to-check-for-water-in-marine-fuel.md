# How to check for water in marine fuel

**Drain the bowl on the primary filter or water separator into a clear glass jar
and look at it.** Water is denser than diesel and sits underneath as a distinct
layer, usually clear or straw-coloured with a sharp boundary. Sludge and dark
growth at the interface between them is diesel bug.

This takes two minutes and it is the single most informative check an owner can
make on a fuel system.

## Why boats get water and cars do not

A car empties its tank every week or two. A boat's tank may hold the same fuel
for a season, in a hull that heats through the day and cools at night. Each
cycle draws humid air in through the tank vent and condenses water on the walls.

Water settles under the diesel, and the interface between the two is where
microbial growth lives. See
[marine diesel fuel quality and storage](/guides/marine-fuel-quality-australia/).

## The procedure

1. Find the primary filter or water separator. It is the filter between the tank
   and the engine, usually with a clear or drainable bowl at the bottom.
2. Place a clear glass jar under the drain. Glass, not plastic — you want to see
   the layers clearly.
3. Open the drain valve and take a small sample. Close it again.
4. Let the sample settle for a minute and look at it against the light.
5. Read what you see:
   - **Clear bright diesel, no layer** — nothing to act on today
   - **A clear or straw layer underneath with a sharp boundary** — water
   - **Dark, stringy or slimy material at the boundary** — microbial growth
   - **Fine dark sediment** — tank sludge, asphaltenes or rust
   - **Cloudy fuel throughout** — water in suspension, common with biodiesel
     content
6. If you found water, drain until clean fuel runs, then check again after the
   next trip. Water that returns quickly means there is a lot in the tank.
7. Record what you found and when. A trend is far more useful than a single
   sample.

## Sampling the tank itself

The filter bowl tells you what has reached the filter. The tank bottom tells you
what is waiting.

If you have an inspection hatch, a clean tube lowered to the lowest point and
stoppered with a thumb brings up a bottom sample. That is the real picture, and
it is often considerably worse than the filter bowl suggests.

Boats without inspection hatches cannot be sampled or cleaned properly, which is
why fitting them is worthwhile on a tank that has given trouble.

## What to do about what you find

- **A little water, occasionally** — drain it, keep the tank full over layup to
  reduce condensation
- **Water returning repeatedly** — there is a quantity in the tank. It needs
  removing rather than draining a cup at a time
- **Microbial growth** — biocide kills it and releases the dead biomass into the
  fuel, which then goes to your filters. Dose before a cleaning cycle, not
  before a passage
- **Heavy sludge** — the tank needs opening and cleaning. Fuel polishing cleans
  the fuel, not the tank walls

## The symptom that confirms it

**Fine in flat water, loses power in a chop.** Motion stirs settled water and
sludge off the tank bottom into the pickup. If that is your pattern, the tank is
the problem regardless of what the filter bowl looks like on a calm day.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know if there is water in my diesel?**
Drain the primary filter bowl into a clear glass jar. Water sits underneath the
diesel as a distinct layer with a sharp boundary.

**What is the dark slime at the boundary?**
Microbial growth — diesel bug. It lives at the water and fuel interface and
produces sludge that blocks filters.

**Should I add biocide straight away?**
Biocide kills growth and releases the dead material into the fuel, which then
reaches your filters. Dose before a cleaning cycle, not before a passage.

**Does fuel polishing fix it?**
It cleans the fuel, not the tank walls. A badly fouled tank needs opening and
cleaning.

**Why does my engine only lose power in rough water?**
Motion stirs settled water and sludge into the pickup. It is the clearest single
sign of tank contamination.
