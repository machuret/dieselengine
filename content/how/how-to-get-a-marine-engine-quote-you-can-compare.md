# How to get a marine engine quote you can compare

**Ask every workshop to quote the same defined scope, itemised into parts and
labour, with exclusions stated and a dollar threshold above which they must call
you before continuing.** Two quotes with different totals usually describe
different jobs, and the cheaper one is frequently cheaper because it leaves
something out.

## The questions that make quotes comparable

Ask all of these, of everyone, in writing:

1. **What exactly are you quoting?** For a rebuild: in-frame, out-of-frame,
   partial repair, or an exchange unit? These are four different jobs.
2. **Is this a fixed price or an estimate?**
3. **What is explicitly excluded?** Parts at trade or retail, consumables,
   travel, crane or haul-out, disposal.
4. **What happens if you find more once it is open?** Agree a dollar threshold
   above which they must call you before proceeding. This is the single
   strongest protection you have.
5. **What is the labour rate, and is there a minimum call-out?**
6. **Is diagnostic time charged separately, and credited if I proceed?**
7. **Genuine, aftermarket or reconditioned parts?** All three are legitimate;
   you should know which.
8. **Are the parts in stock, and if not, what is the lead time?** Parts
   availability drives most marine job delays.
9. **Is the gearbox included or excluded?** On engine work it is usually
   excluded. Ask.
10. **Will you sea-trial it afterwards?** For anything affecting cooling, fuel
    delivery or the drivetrain, the job is not finished until it has run under
    load.
11. **What warranty applies to your labour, and is it transferable?**
12. **When will you start and when will you finish?** Get both dates.

## Make the scope identical before you compare

The most common mistake is sending three workshops a symptom and comparing the
three different jobs they propose.

Instead: get one workshop to define the scope, then ask the others to quote
**that** scope. If someone disagrees with the scope, that is useful information
in itself — but it is a separate conversation from price.

## Where the money hides

On major work, these are the items that appear after the quote rather than in
it:

- Machining discovered necessary once the block is stripped
- Engine beds needing modification for a different engine
- New propeller or shaft to match changed power or gearbox ratio
- Exhaust replacement to suit a different outlet
- New instrument panel and loom
- Crane, haul-out or hull opening to get the engine in and out

For a repower, see [what a repower costs](/guides/marine-repower-cost-australia/)
— the engine is frequently less than half of it.

## What to keep

- The written quote with scope and exclusions
- An itemised invoice separating parts and labour
- A written report of what was found and done
- Any test results, before and after
- The old parts, if you asked for them before the job

That file protects your resale value and, in a dispute, is your evidence —
see [your repair rights](/guides/marine-repair-warranty-rights-australia/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are two quotes for the same job so different?**
They usually describe different jobs. Define the scope first, then ask everyone
to quote that scope.

**What is a call-back threshold?**
A dollar figure above which the workshop must contact you before doing more
work. It is the strongest protection against a bill that triples.

**Should I supply my own parts?**
You can, and it sometimes saves money, but most workshops will not warrant
labour on parts they did not supply.

**Is it reasonable to ask for the old parts?**
Yes, and it is a normal request. Ask before the job, not after.

**Is the gearbox included in engine work?**
Usually not. It is a separate schedule with a different oil. Ask explicitly.
