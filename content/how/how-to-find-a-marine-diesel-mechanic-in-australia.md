# How to find a marine diesel mechanic in Australia

**Start with who works on your engine family in your precinct, confirm they have
the diagnostic tool if your engine is electronic, and establish whether they
attend berths and moorings or work only from a yard.** Those three questions
eliminate most of the wrong answers before you have spent anything.

A diesel mechanic who works on trucks is not automatically able to work on your
boat. The block may be a relative of something automotive; the raw water
circuit, the wet exhaust, the gearbox, the galvanic environment and the access
constraints are not.

## The sequence

1. **Identify your engine properly** — model and serial from the plate. See
   [how to identify your marine engine](/how/how-to-identify-your-marine-engine/).
   Every conversation goes better from there.
2. **Decide what kind of attendance you need.** Servicing and diagnostics can
   usually be done where the boat lies. Anything below the waterline needs a
   yard, and the haul-out is the booked resource rather than the workshop.
3. **Ask who works on your engine family locally.** The marina office, the
   chandlery and the boat next door are better sources than a search result.
4. **Check the manufacturer's dealer locator** if your engine is electronic or
   under warranty. It is the authoritative list of who is appointed — see
   [distributors by brand](/distributors/).
5. **Ask the three qualifying questions.** Do you work on this engine family
   regularly? Do you have current diagnostic access for it? Do you attend my
   berth or mooring?
6. **Confirm insurance and marina accreditation.** Many marinas require
   contractors to be registered with them, and finding out on the day wastes
   everyone's time.
7. **Get the scope in writing** before work starts — see
   [how to get a quote you can compare](/how/how-to-get-a-marine-engine-quote-you-can-compare/).

## Dealer or independent

Both are legitimate. Which you want depends on the job:

**A dealer** has factory diagnostics, training and warranty authority. That
matters for electronic engines, software, warranty work, and commissioning after
a repower.

**An independent** is frequently better value on older mechanical engines, often
more flexible about attendance, and in many regions has deeper local knowledge
than the nearest appointed dealer.

## Warning signs

- A diagnosis over the phone with no inspection, followed by a large quote
- Replacing parts sequentially without testing, and billing for each
- Reluctance to put anything in writing
- No mention of the raw water side when quoting an overheating fix
- Cash only with no invoice, which also removes your consumer law protections
- Pressure to decide immediately

## What to have ready before they arrive

You will get a better outcome and a smaller bill with:

- Engine make, model and serial number
- Approximate hours
- What the symptom is, when it started, and what brings it on
- What has been done recently
- Any service records
- Clear access to the engine bay

"It overheats above 2000 rpm after twenty minutes, and it started after I ran
over a rope" is worth an hour of diagnostic time.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do marine mechanics need a licence in Australia?**
Requirements vary by state and by the type of work — electrical and gas work is
separately licensed. Ask about qualifications, insurance and marina
accreditation, and get everything invoiced.

**Should I use a dealer or an independent?**
Dealers for electronic diagnostics, software and warranty. Independents are
frequently better value on older mechanical engines.

**Will a mechanic come to my mooring?**
Many will, for servicing and most diagnostics. Ask specifically — some work only
from a yard.

**What is the most useful thing I can do before calling?**
Get the engine's model and serial number from the plate, and describe the
symptom with the conditions that bring it on.

**How do I check someone is an authorised dealer?**
The manufacturer's own dealer locator. It is maintained by the people who make
the appointments.
