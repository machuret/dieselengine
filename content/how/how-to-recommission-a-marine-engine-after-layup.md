# How to recommission a marine engine after layup

**Open the seacocks, check every fluid, inspect belts and hoses, fit a fresh
impeller, then start and watch for water at the exhaust within seconds.** The
first start of the season is the most informative event of the boating year —
it is where a winter of sitting announces itself.

Do this alongside, with help available, not on the way out through the heads.

## The pre-start checks

1. **Open the seacocks.** Every one. Starting with the raw water seacock closed
   destroys an impeller in under a minute, and it is a genuinely common way to
   begin a season.
2. **Check the engine oil.** Level, colour, and whether it is milky. Milky means
   water got in over the layup and needs investigating before you start.
3. **Check the coolant.** Level and condition, with the engine cold.
4. **Check the gearbox oil.** Separate item, separate oil.
5. **Inspect belts.** Perished, glazed or cracked belts fail on first load.
6. **Inspect hoses and clamps.** Squeeze each hose — a hose that feels hard,
   spongy or crunchy has had its life.
7. **Fit a fresh impeller** if it sat compressed all winter, or at least inspect
   it. See
   [how to change an impeller](/how/how-to-change-a-marine-engine-impeller/).
8. **Clear the strainer**, which may contain a winter's worth of decayed weed.
9. **Check the batteries** — voltage, terminals, and whether they held charge.
10. **Drain the primary filter bowl** and look at the sample. Months of
    condensation collect there.
11. **Look for rodent and insect damage.** Chewed wiring and nests in air
    intakes are more common than owners expect.
12. **Check the anodes**, which have been dissolving all winter with the engine
    off.

## Starting

1. Turn the engine over on the starter with the fuel off or the stop pulled, if
   your engine allows it, to circulate oil before it fires.
2. Start, and immediately look for **water at the exhaust within seconds**. If
   there is none, shut down.
3. Watch oil pressure come up promptly.
4. Listen. Note any sound that is new.
5. Let it warm at idle and watch the temperature stabilise.
6. Check for leaks around everything you touched — filters, hoses, the impeller
   cover.

## Under load, before you rely on it

An engine that idles beautifully alongside can still fail under load. Before a
real trip:

- Run at cruise long enough for temperature to stabilise
- Take it to full throttle briefly and confirm it reaches rated maximum rpm
- Watch exhaust colour under load
- Check gear engagement ahead and astern

**A boat that has sat all winter with fuel in the tank is the classic candidate
for filter blockage under load.** That is far better discovered a mile from the
berth than fifty.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I check first after winter?**
That the seacocks are open. Starting with the raw water seacock shut destroys an
impeller in under a minute.

**Does the impeller need replacing after layup?**
It takes a set if left compressed for months. Many owners fit a new one at
recommissioning as a matter of course.

**Why is the first start so important?**
It is where a winter of deterioration shows — starting difficulty, smoke,
leaks, and whether water reaches the exhaust.

**My engine ran fine alongside but died on the first trip. Why?**
Very often fuel. Months of condensation and settled sludge reach the filters
once motion stirs the tank.

**Should I sea trial before a real trip?**
Yes. Run to cruise and briefly to full throttle close to home before relying on
the engine.
