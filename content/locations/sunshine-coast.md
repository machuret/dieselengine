# Marine Diesel Mechanics on the Sunshine Coast

The Sunshine Coast fleet is concentrated in two places — Mooloolaba and Noosa —
and it is defined by game fishing. The Mooloolaba fleet runs hard offshore to
the shelf and back, which is a demanding duty cycle: sustained high load, long
runs, and engines that are worked properly rather than idled around a bay.

Hard-worked engines are, counterintuitively, often in better condition than
lightly used ones. The Sunshine Coast problems tend to be wear and heat related
— aftercoolers, turbos, injectors, exhaust systems — rather than the neglect
faults that dominate low-hours fleets elsewhere.

The other half of the market is the opposite: seasonal cruisers and retirees'
boats that sit for months between trips, then do a long run north. Those fail on
fuel and idle-related faults.

## Choosing an operator on the Sunshine Coast

**Get a pre-trip inspection before offshore work.** If you cross the bar and run
to the shelf, an engine failure is a very different problem from one in a bay.
Cooling, fuel condition, belts, impeller and exhaust checked before the season
is the highest-value money you will spend.

**Hard-run engines need different attention.** Sustained high load means
aftercoolers, turbos, exhaust systems and oil condition matter more than they
would on a bay boat.

**Seasonal boats need the opposite.** If yours sits for months, the fuel and
the impeller are the risks, not wear.

**Noosa river access has constraints.** Depth and bridge access affect where a
boat can be worked on and hauled. Confirm before committing to a yard.

## Queensland rules that affect you

**Recreational vessels.** Registration and safety equipment requirements are
administered by Maritime Safety Queensland. There is no periodic engine
inspection for recreational craft — engine condition is entirely the owner's
responsibility.

**Commercial vessels.** Domestic commercial vessels operate under the national
system administered by AMSA, carrying survey, maintenance and record-keeping
obligations well beyond recreational requirements.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I check before a run to the shelf?**
Cooling and fuel above all — impeller condition, strainer, coolant, belts, fuel
filters and water separator, plus a look at the exhaust elbow if it is due. A
pre-trip inspection is cheaper than a tow.

**My boat sits for months between trips — what fails?**
Fuel first, then the impeller and the batteries. Microbial growth and water
accumulate in a part-full tank and block filters on the first hard run.
→ [Diesel bug treatment](/services/diesel-bug-treatment/)

**Are hard-run game boats harder on engines?**
They wear differently, not necessarily worse. Regular use at proper load is
better for a marine diesel than long idle periods.

**Can I get work done at Noosa or should I move the boat?**
Depends on the vessel and the job — depth and access constrain some work at
Noosa. Ask the operator before committing.
