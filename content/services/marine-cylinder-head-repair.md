# Marine Cylinder Head Repair in Australia

The cylinder head on a marine diesel carries the valves and injectors and seals
against high combustion pressures, while coolant passes through it — and on many
raw-water-cooled installations, so does seawater at some point in the system.
That combination means marine heads fail from corrosion as often as from heat.

## Symptoms

- Overheating, or coolant loss with no visible leak
- White smoke, sweet-smelling
- Bubbles in the coolant, or the system pressurising quickly
- Oil and coolant mixing
- Low compression on one cylinder
- Salt water in a cylinder or in the oil

## Rule out the cheaper causes first

On a marine engine, several faults produce head-gasket symptoms and cost far
less:

**A perforated heat exchanger tube**, pushing seawater into the coolant or
coolant overboard through the exhaust.
→ [Heat exchanger service](/services/heat-exchanger-service/)

**A perforated oil cooler**, mixing oil and coolant.

**A corroded aftercooler**, putting seawater into the intake.
→ [Aftercooler service](/services/aftercooler-service/)

**A failed exhaust elbow**, letting seawater back into a cylinder.
→ [Exhaust elbow replacement](/services/exhaust-elbow-replacement/)

**A failed pressure cap**, expelling coolant.

Pressure testing the cooling system and a combustion gas test on the coolant
distinguish these from a head problem. Insist on both before anyone removes a
head — on a boat, that job is expensive and the access is often poor.

## The work

Removal, strip and clean, crack testing, pressure testing, flatness measurement
and machining, valve and seat work, new stem seals and guides where worn, and
reassembly with correct clearances.

On many marine engines the head bolts are torque-to-yield and must be replaced.
So must the gasket.

**Corrosion assessment is the marine-specific part.** A head that has been run
with depleted coolant, or that has had seawater through it, may be corroded in
the water jackets beyond economic repair. This is a real outcome and worth
establishing before machining money is spent.

## Fix the cause

Marine heads fail from overheating or from water ingress, and both have causes
that outlive the repair. A new head on an unrepaired cooling system, or behind a
corroded exhaust elbow, fails again.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know it's the head and not something cheaper?**
You do not until the cooling system has been pressure tested and the coolant
tested for combustion gas. On a boat those tests are essential before pulling a
head — a perforated heat exchanger or oil cooler produces the same symptoms for
much less money.

**Can a corroded marine cylinder head be repaired?**
Sometimes. Corrosion into the water jackets frequently ends it. Get that assessed
before paying for machining.

**Do head bolts need replacing?**
On most modern marine diesels, yes — they are torque-to-yield.

**Why did my head fail?**
Overheating or water ingress, almost always. Both have causes that must be fixed
or the repair does not last.
