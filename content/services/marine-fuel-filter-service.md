# Fuel Filter and Water Separator Service in Australia

The filters are the only thing standing between what is in your tank and an
injection system with clearances measured in microns. On a boat, where the tank
is the usual source of trouble, they are doing more work than their automotive
equivalents and deserve more attention.

## The two stages

**Primary filter / water separator.** Mounted between tank and engine, usually
with a clear bowl. It removes water and larger particulate, and the bowl lets you
see what your tank is producing. This is the single most informative component
on a marine fuel system — check it, don't just change it.

**Secondary / engine-mounted filter.** Finer filtration immediately before the
injection system, protecting it from anything the primary missed.

## What the bowl tells you

- **Clear fuel** — the tank is in good order
- **Water beneath the fuel** — condensation, a leaking deck fill or vent, or
  contaminated fuel at the pump
- **Dark slime or jelly** — microbial growth
  → [Diesel bug treatment](/services/diesel-bug-treatment/)
- **Black or rust-coloured particulate** — tank sediment or internal corrosion
- **Repeated blocking in rough weather only** — settled sludge being stirred up

Draining the separator and looking at what comes out takes a minute and is the
cheapest diagnostic on the boat.

## The service

- Replace primary and secondary elements with the correct micron rating for your
  engine. Fitting a finer primary than specified causes fuel starvation at high
  revs; fitting a coarser one passes contamination through.
- Drain and clean the separator bowl
- Inspect and replace bowl seals — a leaking seal draws air, which causes hard
  starting and rough running that gets misdiagnosed as an injection fault
- Check the vacuum gauge if fitted; a rising reading shows a filter loading up
- Bleed the system properly
- Inspect hoses and connections for weeping and for air ingress

## Air leaks: the fault that looks like something expensive

A small air leak on the suction side — a perished bowl seal, a loose fitting, a
cracked hose — causes hard starting, rough running, and power loss at load. It
regularly gets diagnosed as injectors or a pump, both of which cost a great deal
more than an O-ring.

If the symptoms are fuel-related, have the suction side pressure or vacuum tested
before anyone quotes for injection components.

## What to carry

Spare primary and secondary elements, spare bowl seals, and the knowledge of how
to change and bleed them. On a boat this is the difference between a delay and a
tow.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should marine fuel filters be changed?**
At least annually, and more often if the bowl shows contamination. Interval by
hours misses the point — tank condition drives it.

**Can I fit a finer filter for better protection?**
No. A primary finer than specified restricts flow and starves the engine at high
revs. Use the rating the engine maker specifies.

**Why does my engine run rough but the filters look clean?**
Very often an air leak on the suction side — a perished bowl seal or a loose
fitting. It mimics an injection fault and costs a fraction to fix.

**Should I carry spares?**
Yes, and know how to fit and bleed them. It is the most likely repair you will
ever need to make under way.

**What is the vacuum gauge on my filter for?**
It shows how hard the engine is having to suck through the filter. A rising
reading means the element is loading up, giving you warning before it blocks.
