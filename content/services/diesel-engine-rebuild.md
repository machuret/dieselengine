# Diesel Engine Rebuild in Australia

A diesel engine is designed to be rebuilt. The bottom end is built for high
compression, which means heavy castings, replaceable bearings and — on many
engines — replaceable cylinder liners. This is why a 400,000km diesel is a
rebuild candidate where an equivalent petrol engine is scrap.

## When a rebuild is the right call

- Excessive blow-by, high oil consumption and low compression across cylinders
- Bearing knock, or low oil pressure from bearing wear
- A failure that damaged internals: dropped valve, broken ring, spun bearing
- Hydraulic lock or runaway damage
- Scoring from dust ingestion or fuel contamination
- The engine is sound in design and the vehicle is worth keeping

## What a full rebuild involves

**Strip and inspection.** The engine out and completely dismantled, every
component measured against specification. This is where the quote becomes real —
a pre-strip quote is an estimate, and any honest workshop will say so.

**Machining.** Boring and honing or re-linering, crankshaft grinding or
polishing, block deck and head surfacing, line boring where required.
→ [Engine block machining](/services/engine-block-machining/) ·
[Crankshaft grinding](/services/crankshaft-grinding/) ·
[Cylinder head repair](/services/cylinder-head-repair/)

**New components.** Pistons and rings, bearings, gaskets and seals, oil pump,
valve train components, timing chain or belt kit.

**Assembly to specification.** Measured clearances, correct torque sequences,
correct timing.

**Ancillaries.** This is where rebuilds are won and lost. The turbo, injectors,
cooling system and oil cooler all need attention. A freshly rebuilt engine fed
by worn injectors or starved by a blocked oil cooler does not stay fresh.

**Run-in.** A defined running-in procedure, and an oil change shortly after
start-up to clear assembly debris.

## The questions that separate a good rebuild from a bad one

- Is the machining done in-house or sent out, and to whom?
- Are the injectors being tested and the turbo assessed as part of the job?
- What is being done about the *cause* — dust ingestion, fuel contamination,
  overheating, oil starvation? An engine that failed for a reason will fail
  again if the reason stands.
- What does the warranty cover, and what voids it?
- Is the quote pre-strip or post-strip?

## Rebuild, recondition, exchange, or second-hand?

Four different things, frequently confused:

**Rebuild** — your engine, machined and reassembled. Longest downtime, known
history.

**Reconditioned exchange** — a remanufactured engine off the shelf, yours
returned as the core. Much shorter downtime.
→ [Diesel engine reconditioning](/services/diesel-engine-reconditioning/)

**Second-hand / import** — cheapest, and a gamble on unknown history.

**New crate engine** — most expensive, where available.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it worth rebuilding or should I replace the vehicle?**
Compare the rebuild against the vehicle's value with a good engine, and against
the cost of an equivalent replacement vehicle. On a sound 4WD or commercial
vehicle a rebuild is frequently the better economic decision.
→ [Rebuild or replace a diesel engine](/guides/rebuild-vs-replace-diesel-engine/)

**How long does a rebuild take?**
Typically two to six weeks, depending on machining queues and parts
availability. Exchange engines are much faster.

**Why did the price go up after they stripped it?**
Because a pre-strip quote cannot account for what is found inside. A workshop
that quotes firmly before stripping is guessing, and the guess is usually low.

**How long should a rebuilt engine last?**
Properly done, with the original cause corrected, comparable to a new engine.
The cause is the variable.

**What warranty should I expect?**
Ask for it in writing, including what voids it — most require documented
run-in servicing and correction of the original failure cause.
