# Winterisation and Seasonal Layup in Australia

Australian winterisation is not about frost. Outside the alpine lakes, nothing in
Australian boating freezes. It is about the damage that four or five idle months
do to an engine — and that damage is real, expensive, and almost entirely
preventable.

Melbourne, Hobart, Adelaide, Geelong, the Gippsland Lakes and southern NSW have
genuine layup seasons. Queensland does not, but any boat left standing for months
anywhere in Australia has the same problem.

## What actually happens over a layup

**Fuel degrades.** Diesel oxidises in storage, and water accumulates through
condensation in a part-full tank. That water lets microbial growth establish at
the fuel-water interface. Four months is ample time.
→ [Diesel bug treatment](/services/diesel-bug-treatment/)

**The impeller takes a set.** Blades left bent against the cam lose their spring
and pump poorly on the first start of the season — which is why so many boats
overheat on their first trip.
→ [Impeller replacement](/services/impeller-replacement/)

**Acidic combustion by-products sit in the oil**, attacking bearings for months
if the oil was not changed before layup. This is why the oil change belongs at
the start of the layup, not the end.

**Coolant inhibitors deplete** and the engine corrodes internally.

**Batteries sulphate** and are frequently dead in spring.

**Rubber perishes** — hoses, belts, seals and bellows.

**Growth accumulates** in the raw water circuit and on the hull.

**Rodents and insects** find engine bays attractive, and chew wiring.

## What a proper layup includes

- **Oil and filter change before layup**, not after — so the engine sits on clean
  oil rather than acidic oil
- **Fuel tank filled** to minimise condensation space, treated with a stabiliser
  and biocide
- **Fuel filters and water separator** serviced and drained
- **Coolant tested** and replaced if inhibitors are depleted
- **Raw water circuit flushed** with fresh water; on some installations drained
- **Impeller relieved or removed** so it does not take a set
- **Batteries** removed or on a maintainer
- **Engine ports and exhaust sealed** against rodents and insects, with a
  conspicuous reminder to remove the seals
- **Seacocks closed** and recorded
- **Anodes checked**, since they waste while the boat sits
- **Fogging or preservative oil** where the maker recommends it
- **Ventilation** — trapped humid salt air corrodes everything

## The other half

A layup is only half a job. What prevents spring failures is inspecting and
recommissioning before the first trip, not on it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why winterise in Australia when nothing freezes?**
Because the damage is from idle time, not frost — fuel degradation, microbial
growth, impeller set, acidic oil, depleted coolant and flat batteries.

**Should I change the oil before or after layup?**
Before. Used oil is acidic and attacks bearings the entire time the engine sits.

**Full or empty fuel tank over winter?**
Full. Air space condenses moisture, and water is what feeds diesel bug.

**Do I need to do this if the boat only sits for two months?**
The full routine, no. Fuel treatment, a battery maintainer and clean oil, yes —
they cost little and prevent most of it.

**What is the most commonly skipped item?**
Relieving the impeller and treating the fuel. Both cause first-trip failures in
spring.
