# Workboat and Ferry Engine Servicing in Australia

Workboats, ferries, pilot vessels, dive tenders, fishing boats and support craft
share one characteristic: the engine is a production asset. Its condition is
measured in availability, and its maintenance is a cost of operating rather than
an owner's discretion.

These vessels operate under the national system for domestic commercial vessels
administered by AMSA, with the survey and record-keeping obligations that brings.

→ [Commercial vessel engine servicing](/services/commercial-vessel-servicing/) ·
[Engine requirements for commercial vessel survey](/guides/commercial-vessel-survey-requirements/)

## The duty cycle

**High hours at sustained load.** Ferries in particular run to timetable at
near-constant load for long periods — arguably the hardest duty in marine
diesel, and one that rewards good cooling maintenance more than anything else.

**Frequent starts and stops** on short-run services, which is hard on batteries,
starters and the charging system.
→ [Marine engine electrical](/services/marine-engine-electrical/)

**Extended idling** on standby vessels, which glazes bores, builds carbon and
dilutes oil. An engine that idles for hours needs periodic loading.

**Heavy loading** on towing and working vessels, where sustained maximum output
makes cooling capacity and exhaust temperature the limiting factors.

## Maintenance that fits a commercial operation

**Planned maintenance windows** scheduled around the operating pattern, not
around failures.

**Condition monitoring** — oil analysis, exhaust temperature trending, fuel
consumption per hour. A rising consumption trend or a climbing exhaust
temperature identifies developing problems long before an alarm sounds.
→ [Marine engine oil analysis](/services/marine-engine-oil-analysis/)

**Component exchange rather than in-place repair.** Running an exchange engine,
gearbox or turbo takes the rebuild off the critical path and returns the vessel
to service sooner. On a revenue vessel this usually beats a cheaper in-place
repair.
→ [Marine engine reconditioning](/services/marine-engine-reconditioning/)

**Held spares** for the specific engines in the fleet.

**Documentation** that satisfies the vessel's safety management system.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is constant high load bad for a marine diesel?**
No — it is what they are designed for, provided cooling and oil condition are
maintained. Sustained load is easier on an engine than long idling.

**My vessel idles for hours on standby — is that a problem?**
Yes. Extended light-load running glazes bores, builds carbon and dilutes the oil.
Periodic loading is maintenance.

**Should I use an exchange engine rather than rebuild in place?**
On a revenue vessel, usually. It takes the rebuild off the critical path and
returns the vessel to service far sooner.

**What condition monitoring is worth doing?**
Oil analysis, exhaust temperature trending and fuel consumption per hour. All
three give warning well ahead of an alarm.

**What documentation should I get from my workshop?**
Enough to satisfy your safety management system — what was done, when, by whom
and to what specification, not just an invoice.
