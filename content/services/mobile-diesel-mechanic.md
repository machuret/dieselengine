# Mobile Diesel Mechanics in Australia

A mobile diesel mechanic comes to your home, workplace or yard in a
fully-equipped vehicle. For a large share of diesel work this is genuinely
better than a workshop — and for another large share it is not possible at all.
Knowing which is which saves a wasted call-out fee.

## What a mobile mechanic can do

- Servicing: oil, filters, fuel filters, water separator draining
- Diagnostics, with the same scan equipment a workshop uses
- Batteries, starters, alternators, glow plugs
- Sensors, actuators, wiring faults
- Belts, hoses, coolant, thermostats
- Fuel filter blockages and water contamination in the filter
- Forced DPF regenerations, where they carry the tooling
- Pre-purchase inspections
- Fleet servicing on site — often the strongest case for mobile work

## What needs a workshop

- Anything requiring a hoist: transmission, clutch, driveline, exhaust
- Internal engine work: heads, rebuilds, timing chains
- Turbo replacement on most engines
- Injector and pump work needing bench calibration
- Off-vehicle DPF cleaning
- Machining and pressing

A mobile operator who tells you a job needs a workshop is being straight with
you.

## When mobile is the better choice

**When travel time is the real cost.** In Sydney and Melbourne, getting a
vehicle to a workshop and back can cost more in lost time than the service.

**For fleets.** Servicing vans or trucks at your yard overnight or on a
weekend removes downtime entirely, which usually dominates the labour rate.

**When the vehicle can't be driven safely.** A mobile mechanic can diagnose in
place rather than you paying for a tow to find out it was a sensor.

**For pre-purchase inspections.** They come to the seller's vehicle, which is
where it is.
→ [Pre-purchase diesel inspection checklist](/guides/pre-purchase-diesel-inspection/)

## What to check before booking

1. **Do they have the diagnostic equipment for your vehicle?** Same question as
   for a workshop, and just as decisive.
2. **What is the call-out fee, and is it credited against the work?**
3. **Are they licensed and insured?** NSW and WA license repair businesses,
   including mobile operators.
4. **What happens if the job turns out to need a workshop?** Ask what you pay in
   that case before they come out.
5. **Where can they work?** Apartment car parks and some strata properties do not
   permit it, and environmental rules govern oil disposal.

## What it costs

A call-out fee plus labour. Often comparable in total to a workshop once travel
and downtime are counted.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a mobile diesel mechanic more expensive?**
The hourly rate is often similar; there is usually a call-out fee. Against that,
you lose no time delivering and collecting the vehicle. For fleets, mobile is
frequently cheaper overall.

**Can a mobile mechanic do my log book service?**
Yes, and it does not affect your warranty provided the work meets the
manufacturer's schedule and specification and is documented.
→ [Repair warranty rights](/guides/diesel-repair-warranty-rights-australia/)

**Can they do a DPF regeneration at my house?**
A forced regeneration, yes, if they carry the tooling. Off-vehicle cleaning
requires the filter to be removed and machine cleaned.

**What if they can't fix it on site?**
Ask this before booking. Most charge the call-out and diagnostic time and can
recommend or arrange a workshop.

**Can they work in an apartment car park?**
Often not — many strata and commercial properties prohibit it, and oil disposal
is regulated. Check before booking.
