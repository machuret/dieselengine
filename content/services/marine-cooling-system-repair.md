# Marine Cooling System Repair in Australia

Most marine diesels have two cooling circuits. The raw water circuit draws
seawater and dumps it overboard through the exhaust. The freshwater circuit is a
closed loop of coolant circulating through the engine, giving up its heat to the
raw water in the heat exchanger. This page covers the closed side; the seawater
side is [raw water system repair](/services/raw-water-system-repair/).

## The closed circuit, and what fails

**Thermostat.** Sticks closed and the engine overheats; sticks open and it never
reaches operating temperature. Running cold is a genuine fault, not a harmless
one — it glazes bores, builds carbon and dilutes oil with unburnt fuel.
→ [Engine running too cold](/marine-engine-problems/engine-running-cold/)

**Coolant.** Marine engines need the correct coolant with intact corrosion
inhibitors. Those inhibitors deplete on a schedule, and depleted coolant lets the
engine corrode internally — which is how an engine rots from the inside while
looking fine outside. Topping up with water is a slow way to destroy a block.

**Circulating pump.** Bearing and seal wear; a weeping pump leaves coolant traces
at the weep hole.

**Hoses, header tank and pressure cap.** A pressure cap that no longer holds
pressure lowers the coolant's boiling point and causes overheating that no other
component explains. It is cheap and routinely overlooked.

**Heat exchanger.** Where the two circuits meet.
→ [Heat exchanger service](/services/heat-exchanger-service/)

**Oil and gearbox coolers.** A perforated cooler mixes oil and coolant.

## Coolant loss with no visible leak

A closed system losing coolant with nothing on the bilge is a specific and
serious pattern. The possibilities, in rough order:

1. **Pressure cap** not holding, expelling coolant on heat cycles
2. **Perforated heat exchanger tube**, pushing coolant into the seawater and
   overboard — invisible because it goes out with the exhaust
3. **Perforated oil cooler**, coolant into the oil
4. **Head gasket or cracked head**, combustion gas into the coolant

The first two are common and comparatively cheap. The last is expensive. A
pressure test and a combustion gas test on the coolant distinguish them, and
both should happen before anyone quotes to remove a head.

→ [Losing coolant with no visible leak](/marine-engine-problems/coolant-loss-marine/) ·
[Milky oil on the dipstick](/marine-engine-problems/milky-oil-marine/)

## Maintenance that actually prevents failure

- Replace coolant on the manufacturer's schedule, with the correct
  specification — this is about corrosion protection, not freezing
- Test coolant condition rather than judging it by colour
- Replace the pressure cap periodically; it is a wear item
- Replace hoses on age
- Check the heat exchanger's pencil anode

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does coolant matter if it never freezes here?**
Because the important job of marine coolant in Australia is corrosion
protection, not antifreeze. Depleted inhibitors let the engine corrode
internally.

**Can I top up with water?**
In an emergency, briefly. As a habit it dilutes the inhibitors and shortens the
engine's life.

**My engine runs cold — is that a problem?**
Yes. A thermostat stuck open causes bore glazing, carbon build-up and fuel
dilution of the oil. It is worth fixing.

**I'm losing coolant but there's nothing in the bilge — where is it going?**
Most often out through the exhaust via a perforated heat exchanger tube, or
expelled past a failed pressure cap. Both are cheaper than the head gasket people
usually assume.

**How often should coolant be changed?**
On the manufacturer's schedule. Test it rather than judging by appearance —
coolant can look fine and have no inhibitor left.
