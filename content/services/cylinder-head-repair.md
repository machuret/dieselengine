# Cylinder Head Repair in Australia

The cylinder head carries the valves, the injectors, the glow plugs and, on most
modern diesels, the camshafts. It seals against combustion pressures far higher
than a petrol engine's, while carrying coolant and oil galleries through the same
casting. When it goes wrong, the symptoms are usually mistaken for something
cheaper.

## Symptoms

- Overheating, or coolant loss with no external leak
- White smoke from the exhaust that smells sweet
- Bubbles in the coolant, or pressurisation of the cooling system
- Oil and coolant mixing — "mayonnaise" under the oil cap
- Misfire on one cylinder with low compression
- A ticking or tapping noise from the top of the engine

## Head gasket, or head?

They present identically and cost very differently. The distinction is made by
inspection after removal:

**Head gasket failure alone** — the gasket has failed but the head is straight
and uncracked. Skim and reassemble with a new gasket.
→ [Head gasket replacement](/services/head-gasket-replacement/)

**Warped head** — heat distortion, common after an overheat. Machinable if
within limits.

**Cracked head** — usually between valve seats or into a coolant gallery.
Sometimes repairable by an experienced specialist; often not. Crack testing
after cleaning is what settles it.

**Damaged valve seats or guides** — from a dropped valve, or wear.

A head that is not crack-tested and pressure-tested before reassembly is a head
that may come straight back out.

## What the work involves

Removal, strip and clean, crack testing (dye penetrant or magnetic particle),
pressure testing, checking flatness against specification, machining the face,
valve and seat work, replacing stem seals and guides where worn, and
reassembling with correct valve clearances.

On many modern diesel engines, head bolts are torque-to-yield and must be
replaced, not reused. So must the head gasket, and on some engines the gasket
thickness is selected by measuring piston protrusion. A workshop reusing head
bolts on a torque-to-yield engine is not doing the job.

## Why it failed

Almost always overheating, and overheating almost always has a cause: a failed
thermostat, a tired radiator, a water pump, a blocked core, or a cooling system
that was marginal before it was asked to tow. On engines with an EGR cooler, a
cracked cooler leaks coolant into the exhaust and mimics head gasket failure
exactly — worth ruling out before pulling a head.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a cracked diesel cylinder head be repaired?**
Sometimes, depending on where the crack is and the head's material. An
experienced specialist will tell you honestly whether it is economic against a
replacement head.

**How do I know if it's the head or the head gasket?**
You do not, until it comes off and is tested. A combustion gas test on the
coolant confirms combustion gas is entering the cooling system, but not which of
the two is responsible.

**Do head bolts need replacing?**
On most modern diesels, yes — they are torque-to-yield and stretch on
tightening. Reusing them is a common cause of repeat failure.

**Why should the cooling system be repaired at the same time?**
Because it is almost certainly what caused the failure. A new head on the old
cooling system fails again.
