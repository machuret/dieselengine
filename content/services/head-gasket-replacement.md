# Head Gasket Replacement in Australia

The head gasket seals combustion pressure, coolant and oil at the joint between
head and block. On a diesel, combustion pressures are far higher than on a
petrol engine, so the gasket works harder and is less forgiving of a head that
is not perfectly flat.

## Symptoms

- Overheating, especially under load or when towing
- Coolant loss with no external leak
- White smoke from the exhaust, sweet-smelling
- Bubbles in the coolant reservoir; the cooling system pressurising quickly
- Oil and coolant mixing
- Rough running or a misfire on one cylinder

## Confirming it before committing

A combustion gas test on the coolant is the standard confirmation: it detects
combustion gases in the cooling system. Cheap, quick, and it prevents a major
job being started on a guess.

Also worth ruling out first, because each mimics head gasket failure and costs
far less:

- **A cracked EGR cooler**, leaking coolant into the exhaust
- **A failed oil cooler**, mixing oil and coolant
- **An external leak** that is only apparent under pressure
- **A cracked head or block**, which costs considerably more and needs a
  different plan

## What the job includes

Head removal, crack and pressure testing, checking flatness and machining as
required, new torque-to-yield head bolts, correct gasket selection (some engines
size the gasket by measured piston protrusion), reassembly to torque
specification, and a full cooling system service.

Skipping the machining check is the single most common reason a head gasket job
fails again within months. A warped head will not seal on a new gasket.

## Fix the cause, not just the gasket

A head gasket rarely fails on its own. It fails because the engine overheated,
and the engine overheated for a reason: thermostat, radiator, water pump, fan
clutch, blocked core, or a cooling system that was marginal and then asked to
tow up a range in February.

The cooling system repair belongs in the same quote. If it is not there, ask
why.

## The economics

On a high-kilometre engine that overheated severely, a head gasket may be the
first repair rather than the only one — a severe overheat can also damage
pistons, liners and bearings. Ask the workshop to assess the bottom end before
you commit, and price a rebuild alongside it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I drive with a blown head gasket?**
No. Coolant loss leads to overheating, and continued overheating turns a head
gasket job into an engine rebuild. Stop and get it recovered.

**Do head gasket sealants work?**
They may temporarily slow a very small leak. They also risk blocking radiator
and heater cores. They are not a repair, and they can make the proper repair
more expensive.

**How long does the job take?**
Commonly two to five days, longer if the head needs machining or a crack is
found.

**Why do some quotes include the cooling system and others don't?**
Because some workshops address the cause and some replace the part. The ones
that include it are quoting the job properly.

**Could it be something cheaper?**
Yes — a cracked EGR cooler or a failed oil cooler produce similar symptoms. Ask
for a combustion gas test and for those to be ruled out first.
