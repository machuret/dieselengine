# Diesel Engine Swap and Replacement in Australia

When an engine is beyond economic repair, replacement is the alternative to a
rebuild. It is faster, and on common Australian engines it is often cheaper — but
what you are actually buying varies enormously, and so does the risk.

## Your four options

**Reconditioned exchange engine.** A remanufactured unit with documented
machining and new components, usually warranted. The best balance of cost,
downtime and risk for most owners.
→ [Diesel engine reconditioning](/services/diesel-engine-reconditioning/)

**Rebuild your existing engine.** Longest downtime, known history, keeps the
original engine with the vehicle.
→ [Diesel engine rebuild](/services/diesel-engine-rebuild/)

**Second-hand or imported used engine.** Cheapest, and a genuine gamble.
Imported low-kilometre engines are common in Australia and many are fine, but
"low kilometres" is a claim, not a measurement. Compression and leak-down testing
before fitting is money well spent.

**New crate engine.** Highest cost, lowest risk, and only available for some
engines.

→ [Rebuild or replace a diesel engine](/guides/rebuild-vs-replace-diesel-engine/) ·
[Reconditioned vs second-hand engine](/guides/recon-vs-secondhand-engine/)

## Long motor, short motor, or complete?

The terms are used loosely and the difference is thousands of dollars:

- **Short motor** — block, crank, pistons, rods. No head.
- **Long motor** — the above plus the cylinder head assembly.
- **Complete / dressed** — with ancillaries fitted.

Get in writing exactly what is included and what you must supply from the old
engine. Every item you reuse is an item that can fail against a new engine's
warranty.

## What must be done at fitting

The ancillaries carried over from a failed engine are what kill replacement
engines early:

- **Oil cooler** — cleaned or replaced. Debris here goes straight into new
  bearings and is a common warranty exclusion.
- **Cooling system** — flushed; radiator replaced if the original overheated.
- **Injectors** — tested, not assumed.
  → [Diesel injector testing](/services/diesel-injector-testing/)
- **Turbo** — assessed, and the intercooler cleaned if it passed oil.
  → [Turbocharger repair](/services/turbo-repair/)
- **Intake tract** — inspected fully if the original failed from dust ingestion.
- **Fuel system** — checked for contamination if that caused the failure.

## Fitting a different engine

Swapping in a non-original engine — a different model or a larger unit — raises
issues beyond the mechanical work: ADR compliance, engineering certification,
state modification approval, insurance, and emissions compliance. It is done in
Australia, but it must be done through the proper approval process, and the
engineering cost is a real part of the budget.

→ [ADR compliance for engine modifications](/guides/adr-compliance-engine-modifications/)

## Frequently asked questions

**Is replacing cheaper than rebuilding?**
Often, on common engines with good exchange availability, and much faster. On
uncommon engines a rebuild may be the only option.

**Are imported used engines a good idea?**
They can be, but the history is unverifiable. Insist on compression and
leak-down testing before fitting, and factor the risk into the price.

**What warranty comes with a replacement engine?**
Varies widely. Ask the term, whether it covers labour, and what voids it —
typically failing to replace the oil cooler or to correct the original failure
cause.

**Can I fit a different engine to my vehicle?**
Only through the proper engineering and approval process, which affects
registration, insurance and emissions compliance. Budget for the certification.

**How long does an engine replacement take?**
Commonly under a week for an exchange unit, against weeks for a rebuild.
