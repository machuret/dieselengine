# Marine Turbocharger Repair and Replacement in Australia

A marine turbocharger works harder than most road turbos: sustained high load
rather than intermittent boost, in a salt atmosphere, often with a
seawater-cooled aftercooler immediately downstream.

## Symptoms

- Loss of power, particularly at the top end
- Black smoke under load
- A change in the turbo's whine, or a new siren-like note
- Blue smoke from oil passing the seals
- Oil in the intake or the aftercooler
- Oil consumption with no external leak

## Diagnose before replacing

Marine turbos are frequently condemned when the real fault is elsewhere and
cheaper:

**A blocked exhaust elbow.** High back pressure destroys turbo performance and
eventually the turbo itself. This is the most common marine cause and it is a
consumable part, not a turbo fault.
→ [Exhaust elbow replacement](/services/exhaust-elbow-replacement/)

**A fouled bottom or wrong propeller,** overloading the engine so it smokes and
underperforms.

**A restricted air filter or intake.**

**A blocked or leaking aftercooler.**
→ [Aftercooler service](/services/aftercooler-service/)

**Shaft play measured against specification**, which tells you whether the
bearings are actually worn.

## Why marine turbos fail

**Oil supply problems.** Blocked or carboned feed lines, low oil pressure,
extended oil intervals. The dominant cause.

**Exhaust back pressure** from a corroded or blocked elbow.

**Salt-laden air** corroding the compressor housing and wheel.

**Shutting down hot.** Stopping immediately after sustained hard running leaves
the bearing housing to heat-soak, coking the oil. On a boat this is easy to avoid
— idle while you approach the berth.

**Water ingestion** from a failed aftercooler or an exhaust problem.

## Repair or replace

Rebuilding — new core assembly, bearings, balancing — is legitimate and
considerably cheaper than new, where the housings are sound. Balancing is the
critical step.

Whichever you choose, the job is not finished without:

- Finding and fixing why it failed
- An oil and filter change, and flushing debris from the system
- A **new oil feed line**, not the old one cleaned
- Checking the oil drain for restriction
- Cleaning the aftercooler and intake of any oil the failed turbo passed
- Checking the exhaust elbow and back pressure

Fitting a turbo without that list is how owners buy two.

## Frequently asked questions

**My engine smokes and lacks power — is the turbo gone?**
Check the exhaust elbow, the bottom and the propeller first. A blocked elbow
produces these symptoms and is a far cheaper part.

**Can a marine turbo be rebuilt?**
Yes, where the housings are sound. Balancing is what makes a rebuild reliable —
ask whether it is done.

**Why did my turbo fail?**
Most often oil supply — a carboned feed line, low pressure or extended intervals
— or exhaust back pressure from a blocked elbow.

**Should I let the engine idle before shutting down?**
After sustained hard running, yes. It lets oil carry heat out of the bearing
housing instead of coking in it.

**Does salt air affect the turbo?**
Yes, particularly the compressor side. It is one reason marine turbos live harder
lives than road ones.
