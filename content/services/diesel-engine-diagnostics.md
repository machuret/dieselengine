# Diesel Engine Diagnostics in Australia

Diagnosis is the part of diesel repair people most want to skip and most
regret skipping. A fault code names a circuit or a deviation the ECU noticed. It
does not name the cause, and on a modern diesel the two are frequently in
different systems.

## The interaction problem

Here is the pattern that costs Australians the most money:

A DPF partially blocks. Exhaust back pressure rises. The turbo can no longer
achieve commanded boost. The ECU logs a boost deviation code and restricts power
to protect the engine. The scan tool reports a turbo fault.

Replace the turbo, and nothing improves — because the turbo was never faulty.

Substitute EGR, injectors, or a fuel supply restriction for the DPF in that
chain and the shape is the same. This is why diesel diagnosis takes hours and
why the diagnostic invoice is the cheapest line item on the job.

## What a real diagnosis includes

**Full code read across all modules**, including freeze-frame data — the
conditions at the moment the fault was logged, which is often more informative
than the code.

**Live data under load**, not at idle. Rail pressure against commanded, boost
against target, DPF differential pressure and soot load, EGR position against
commanded, exhaust gas temperatures, injector corrections.

**Physical testing**, which is what separates diagnosis from code reading:
boost leak pressure test, injector leak-off, compression or cylinder leak-down,
fuel supply pressure and filter restriction, fuel condition.

**A road test** with data logging, because many faults do not appear in a
workshop bay.

**A stated conclusion with evidence.** You should be told which measurement is
out of specification and by how much — not "it's probably the injectors".

## Equipment

Manufacturer-level diagnostic access, or equivalent aftermarket coverage for the
marque. A generic OBD scanner reads emissions codes only. It cannot read live
fuel system data, run actuator tests, command a regeneration, or read injector
correction values.

This is the question to ask when booking: *what diagnostic equipment do you have
for my vehicle?* On a modern diesel, the answer determines whether a diagnosis
is possible at all.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why should I pay for diagnosis when the code already says what's wrong?**
Because the code says what the ECU noticed, not what caused it. The most
expensive diesel repairs in Australia are parts fitted on the strength of a code.

**How much diagnostic time is reasonable?**
Two to four hours for a typical modern diesel fault. Intermittent faults take
longer. A workshop quoting fifteen minutes is reading codes, not diagnosing.

**Can I read the codes myself with a cheap scanner?**
You can read generic codes, and that is a useful starting point. It will not
give you live fuel system data or actuator control.

**Is diagnostic time credited if I proceed with the repair?**
Often, wholly or partly. Confirm at booking.

**The workshop says they can't find the fault — now what?**
Intermittent faults are genuinely hard. Ask what they measured and what they
ruled out; a good workshop will hand you that list, which is worth something to
the next one.
