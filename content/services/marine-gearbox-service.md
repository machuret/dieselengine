# Marine Gearbox and Transmission Service in Australia

The marine gearbox sits between engine and shaft, providing ahead, neutral and
astern, and usually a reduction ratio. Most Australian boats run a ZF, Twin Disc
or older Hurth unit. It is a separate component with its own oil, its own cooler
and its own service interval — and it is the single most frequently forgotten
item on a marine drivetrain.

→ [How a marine gearbox works](/guides/how-a-marine-gearbox-works/)

## Symptoms

- Slipping under load, particularly ahead at high revs
- Delayed or harsh engagement
- No drive in one direction
- Whining, grinding or a change in noise
- Overheating
- Oil that is milky (water ingress) or dark and burnt-smelling
- Vibration that changes with engagement

→ [Gearbox slipping or not engaging](/marine-engine-problems/gearbox-slipping/) ·
[Gearbox overheating](/marine-engine-problems/gearbox-overheating/)

## Check these before condemning the gearbox

**Oil level and condition.** Low oil causes slipping and overheating. It is the
first thing to check and it fixes a surprising proportion of cases.

**Cable or control adjustment.** A cable that does not move the selector through
its full travel gives partial engagement, which slips and burns the clutch
plates. This is a common and cheap fault that destroys gearboxes when left.
Adjusting the linkage properly is far cheaper than a rebuild.

**The oil cooler.** A gearbox oil cooler is raw-water cooled and blocks like
everything else in that circuit. A blocked cooler overheats the box and cooks the
oil.

**Damper plate.** The rubber-cushioned plate between engine and gearbox fails
with a characteristic rattle at idle that disappears in gear. It is frequently
mistaken for a gearbox fault and is a much smaller job — though it usually means
separating the box from the engine.

**Alignment.** Poor engine-to-shaft alignment loads the gearbox output bearing
and destroys it over time.
→ [Engine alignment and coupling](/services/engine-alignment/)

## Service

- Oil change at the specified interval with the correct fluid — some boxes take
  engine oil, some ATF, and using the wrong one causes exactly the slipping
  people then blame on wear
- Oil cooler inspection and cleaning
- Control cable and linkage adjustment through full travel
- Coupling bolts and damper plate check
- Alignment check
- Output shaft seal inspection

## Rebuild

Rebuilds are specialist work: clutch packs, bearings, seals, and setting
clearances to specification. General marine workshops remove and refit; a
transmission specialist does the internals. Ask who is doing the work.

On smaller boxes the cost of a rebuild can approach an exchange unit — get both
prices.

## Frequently asked questions

**My gearbox slips — does it need a rebuild?**
Check the oil level, the oil type, the cooler and the cable adjustment first.
Partial engagement from a badly adjusted cable is a common cause and a cheap fix,
though left long enough it burns the clutches and then it does need a rebuild.

**What oil does a marine gearbox take?**
It depends on the box — some take engine oil, some ATF. Using the wrong one
causes slipping. Check the plate or the manual rather than assuming.

**How often should gearbox oil be changed?**
On the manufacturer's interval, typically alongside an engine service. It is the
most commonly forgotten item on the drivetrain.

**There's a rattle at idle that stops in gear — is the gearbox failing?**
That is the classic failed damper plate symptom, not a gearbox fault — though
reaching it usually means separating the box from the engine.

**Why is my gearbox overheating?**
Most often a blocked raw-water oil cooler, low oil, or slipping clutches
generating heat. All three should be checked together.
