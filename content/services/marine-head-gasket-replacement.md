# Marine Head Gasket Replacement in Australia

The head gasket seals combustion pressure, coolant and — depending on the engine
— oil, at the joint between head and block. On a marine diesel it does that under
sustained high load, which is harder duty than a road engine's.

## Symptoms

- Overheating, particularly under sustained load
- Coolant loss with no visible leak
- White smoke, sweet-smelling
- Bubbles in the coolant, or rapid pressurisation
- Oil and coolant mixing
- Low compression, or a misfire on one cylinder

## Confirm it before committing

On a boat this matters more than on land, because the job is expensive and the
access is frequently poor. Before authorising head removal:

- **Combustion gas test on the coolant** — detects combustion gases in the
  cooling system
- **Cooling system pressure test**
- **Rule out a perforated heat exchanger tube**, which pushes seawater into the
  coolant or coolant overboard
  → [Heat exchanger service](/services/heat-exchanger-service/)
- **Rule out a failed oil cooler**, which mixes oil and coolant
- **Rule out a corroded aftercooler**
  → [Aftercooler service](/services/aftercooler-service/)
- **Rule out the exhaust elbow** putting seawater into a cylinder
  → [Exhaust elbow replacement](/services/exhaust-elbow-replacement/)
- **Check the pressure cap**

Several of those cost a small fraction of a head gasket job and produce
identical symptoms.

## The job

Head removal, crack and pressure testing, flatness measurement and machining as
required, new torque-to-yield head bolts, correct gasket selection, reassembly to
specification, and a full cooling system service.

Skipping the machining check is the most common reason the repair fails again
within months — a warped head will not seal on a new gasket.

## Fix the cause

A marine head gasket rarely fails on its own. It fails because the engine
overheated, and the engine overheated for a reason: impeller, strainer, heat
exchanger, thermostat, or an exhaust elbow restricting flow.

The cooling system repair belongs in the same quote. If it is not there, ask why.

→ [Raw water system repair](/services/raw-water-system-repair/) ·
[Marine cooling system repair](/services/marine-cooling-system-repair/)

## The bigger question

On a high-hour engine that overheated badly, the head gasket may be the first
repair rather than the only one. Severe overheating also damages bores, pistons
and bearings. Ask for the bottom end to be assessed before committing, and price
a rebuild alongside.

## Frequently asked questions

**Could it be something cheaper than a head gasket?**
Frequently, on a boat. A perforated heat exchanger tube, a failed oil cooler, a
corroded aftercooler or a failed pressure cap all produce similar symptoms for a
fraction of the cost. Test before pulling a head.

**Do head gasket sealants work?**
They may slow a very small leak briefly, and they risk blocking heat exchanger
tubes and coolant passages — which on a marine engine creates a worse problem
than the one you started with.

**Can I keep using the engine?**
No. Coolant loss leads to overheating, and continued overheating turns a head
gasket into a rebuild.

**Why do quotes vary so much?**
Access. On some boats the head comes off in a morning; on others the engine bay
makes it a multi-day job.
