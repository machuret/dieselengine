# Emergency and Roadside Diesel Repair in Australia

Breaking down in a diesel is different from breaking down in a petrol car,
mostly because the common roadside diesel faults are ones a general roadside
service cannot fix — fuel contamination, a blocked DPF, an AdBlue no-start, a
seized turbo.

## First: is it safe to keep going?

Some faults mean stop now, and continuing turns a repair into a replacement:

- **Overheating with the gauge in the red** — stop, do not restart hot
- **Low oil pressure warning** — stop immediately; every second is bearing damage
- **Engine runaway** (revving uncontrollably, unresponsive to the key) — this
  will not stop on its own and can destroy the engine
- **A sudden loud mechanical knock**
- **Any smoke or smell of burning**

→ [Diesel engine runaway](/diesel-problems/diesel-runaway/) ·
[Low oil pressure warning](/diesel-problems/diesel-low-oil-pressure/) ·

If you have just put petrol in a diesel tank: **do not start the engine**, not
even to move it. Have it towed.
→ [Put petrol in a diesel by mistake](/diesel-problems/put-petrol-in-diesel/)

## What roadside operators can usually fix on the spot

- Flat or failed batteries
- Blocked fuel filters and water in the filter
- Fuel supply problems, air in the system, bleeding
- Some sensor and electrical faults
- Belts, hoses and coolant losses
- Forced DPF regeneration, where they carry the tooling
- AdBlue top-ups and, sometimes, system resets

## What needs recovery

- Any of the stop-now faults above
- Misfuelling
- Internal engine faults, turbo failure, timing drive failure
- Anything requiring a hoist or bench work

Recovery is not defeat. Being towed 200km costs far less than an engine.

## Australian distances change the calculus

Outside the capitals, "roadside assistance" can mean hours. That makes
preparation worth real money on any long trip:

- A spare fuel filter and the tools to change it, plus knowledge of how to bleed
  your fuel system — the single most useful piece of remote diesel preparation
- Spare belts and coolant
- Enough AdBlue to clear a countdown, if your vehicle uses it
- A pre-trip inspection, particularly for vehicles that sit for months and then
  tour
- Your engine code and vehicle details written down, so a remote workshop can
  source parts before you arrive

→ [Pre-purchase diesel inspection checklist](/guides/pre-purchase-diesel-inspection/)

## Frequently asked questions

**My diesel won't start — is it worth calling someone out?**
Often yes. Fuel filter blockages, water in the fuel, battery and glow plug
faults are all fixable roadside, and diagnosing on the spot is cheaper than
towing to find out it was a sensor.

**Can a roadside mechanic clear my DPF warning?**
They can perform a forced regeneration if they carry the tooling and the filter
is not too far gone. A fully blocked filter needs removal and machine cleaning.

**What if I've run out of AdBlue and the vehicle won't start?**
Refilling may not be enough on its own — many systems need a diagnostic reset
once the countdown has expired.
→ [AdBlue warning and no-start countdown](/diesel-problems/adblue-warning-countdown/)

**Is it cheaper to be towed or repaired on site?**
On site, when the fault is genuinely roadside-fixable. Recovery is cheaper than
the engine damage caused by driving on a stop-now fault.
