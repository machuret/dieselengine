# Marine Engine Repair in Australia

Marine engine repair covers everything from a failed sender to a rebuild. What
distinguishes it from automotive repair is not the engine — it is the systems
around the engine, and the fact that the boat has to be brought to the work or
the work to the boat.

## Where marine repairs actually cluster

Australian marine engine repair is dominated by a small number of systems:

**Cooling and raw water.** The largest category by a wide margin — impellers,
strainers, heat exchangers, exhaust elbows, aftercoolers.
→ [Raw water system repair](/services/raw-water-system-repair/)

**Fuel.** Almost always originating in the tank rather than the engine.
→ [Fuel polishing and tank cleaning](/services/fuel-polishing/)

**Corrosion**, internal and external.
→ [Marine corrosion repair](/services/marine-corrosion-repair/)

**Electrical**, most of it connections rather than components.
→ [Marine engine electrical](/services/marine-engine-electrical/)

**Driveline** — gearbox, coupling, shaft, drive leg.
→ [Marine gearbox service](/services/marine-gearbox-service/)

Wear-out failures — the engine simply reaching the end of its life — are a
comparatively small share. Most marine engines are killed by something, not worn
out.

## Diagnosis on a boat

The propulsion system is the engine, the gearbox, the shaft, the propeller and
the hull. A complaint of "lost power" can be any of them, and only one is the
engine. A good marine mechanic establishes:

- When was it last antifouled? A fouled bottom is a very common answer.
- Does it reach rated wide open throttle RPM?
- Has anything changed — a new propeller, damage, added weight?
- Does the symptom appear at load, at idle, hot, or cold?
- What does the raw water flow look like at the exhaust?

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the most common marine engine repair in Australia?**
Raw water cooling work — impellers, strainers, heat exchangers and exhaust
elbows. It is the system doing the hardest job with the least attention.

**Why do marine repairs cost more than automotive?**
Access in a confined engine bay, marine-specification parts, and attendance at
the boat rather than the boat coming to a workshop.

**My boat has lost power — is it the engine?**
Check the bottom and the propeller first. A fouled hull or a damaged prop
produces the same complaint far more often than an engine fault does.

**Is it worth repairing an old marine diesel?**
More often than an old car engine, because the alternative — a repower — involves
beds, controls, shaft, exhaust and instruments, not just an engine.
