# Engine Block Machining in Australia

Block machining is specialist work done by engine reconditioners, not general
workshops. If your engine is being rebuilt, this is where a large part of the
outcome is determined — and it is worth knowing what is being done to your block
and by whom.

## The operations

**Boring and honing.** Cylinders bored oversize and honed to a specified
crosshatch pattern, with oversize pistons fitted. The honing pattern is what
lets rings bed in; done wrong, the engine uses oil from day one.

**Re-linering.** Many diesel engines use replaceable cylinder liners — wet or
dry. Worn liners are pressed out and new ones fitted to standard bore size,
which means standard pistons and, on a wet-liner engine, a rebuild that can be
repeated indefinitely. This is a major reason diesel engines outlive petrol ones.

**Deck surfacing.** The block face trued so the head gasket seals. Essential
after any overheat.

**Line boring.** The main bearing bore trued when it has distorted, typically
after a spun bearing or a severe overheat.

**Crack testing.** Dye penetrant or magnetic particle inspection. A cracked
block found after assembly is an expensive discovery.

**Pressure testing** of coolant galleries.

**Cleaning.** Hot tank or thermal cleaning, then thorough washing. Machining
debris left in oil galleries destroys new bearings quickly.

## Why the machine shop matters more than the workshop

Most general workshops send machining out. The shop they use determines the
quality of your rebuild, and you are entitled to ask which one and to deal with
them directly.

Questions worth asking:

- Is the block being crack-tested before machining, not after?
- What honing pattern and finish, and is it matched to the ring type being
  fitted?
- Is it being line-bored, or just checked?
- How is it cleaned after machining?
- Are the measurements documented?

A shop that provides measured results is a shop that measured.

## Regional depth

Melbourne, Sydney and Adelaide have the deepest engine machining capability in
Australia, with strong capacity in Newcastle, Wollongong and Perth serving heavy
industry. Turnaround time is usually set by the machine shop's queue rather than
the workshop's, which is why rebuild timelines stretch.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can any block be machined, or are some scrap?**
Cracks in critical areas, severe main bore damage, or a block already at maximum
oversize can end it. Crack testing early avoids paying to machine a block that
was never viable.

**What is re-linering and why does it matter?**
Replacing the cylinder liners rather than boring the block oversize. It returns
the engine to standard bore and allows the process to be repeated in future — a
key reason diesel engines can be rebuilt several times.

**How long does machining take?**
Usually the longest single item in a rebuild timeline, set by the machine shop's
queue. One to three weeks is common.

**Can I deal with the machine shop directly?**
Often yes, and it removes a markup. Ask your workshop who they use.
