# Diesel Fuel Contamination Repair in Australia

Modern diesel injection systems rely on the fuel itself for lubrication and
cooling, at pressures where microscopic tolerances matter. That makes them
extremely intolerant of anything in the fuel that shouldn't be there — and it is
why fuel contamination is one of the leading causes of expensive diesel failure
in Australia, particularly outside the capitals.

## The four kinds of contamination

**Water.** The most common. Condensation in part-full tanks, poor storage at
the point of sale, or a failed seal. Water strips the lubricating film in the
high-pressure pump and causes corrosion and cavitation damage.
→ [Water in fuel warning light](/diesel-problems/diesel-water-in-fuel-light/)

**Particulate.** Dirt, rust and debris, causing abrasive wear on pump and
injector internals. More likely with bulk storage tanks, drum refuelling and
remote outlets with low turnover.

**Microbial growth (diesel bug).** Bacteria and fungi living at the fuel-water
interface. They form sludge that blocks filters and produce acids that corrode
tanks. A serious problem in marine and stored-fuel applications, and in vehicles
left standing.

**Wrong fuel.** Petrol in a diesel tank. Petrol is a solvent, not a lubricant,
and it strips the lubricating film from the pump.
→ [Put petrol in a diesel by mistake](/diesel-problems/put-petrol-in-diesel/)

## If you have just misfuelled: do not start the engine

This is the single most important line on this page. Petrol sitting in a diesel
tank is a drain-and-flush job. Petrol that has been pumped through the
high-pressure system by starting the engine is potentially a pump, injectors,
rail and lines job.

Do not turn the key, not even to move the vehicle. Have it towed.

## What the repair involves

Scope depends entirely on how far the contamination travelled.

**Caught early, engine not run:** drain the tank, clean it, replace filters,
flush the lines, refill. Comparatively inexpensive.

**Engine run briefly:** the above plus inspection and testing of the
high-pressure pump and injectors.

**Engine run and damage done:** decontamination of the entire fuel system, with
pump and injector replacement. Every contaminated component has to be dealt
with — leaving one in place recontaminates the new parts.

## Prevention, especially in regional Australia

- Buy from outlets with high turnover; low-turnover tanks accumulate water
- Drain the water separator on schedule, and act on the warning light the day it
  appears
- Keep the tank fuller rather than emptier in storage — less air means less
  condensation
- Treat stored fuel, and inspect bulk tanks for water and growth
- Filter fuel from drums and jerry cans

## Frequently asked questions

**I put petrol in my diesel — what do I do right now?**
Don't start the engine. Don't turn the key. Have it towed. Starting it is what
turns a drain-and-flush into a fuel system replacement.

**How much water is too much?**
Any water reaching the high-pressure system is too much, which is why the water
separator and its warning light exist. Act on the light immediately.

**What is diesel bug and how do I know if I have it?**
Microbial growth at the fuel-water interface. Signs are dark sludge in filters,
repeated filter blockages, and a foul smell in the tank. Biocide treatment plus
tank cleaning is the fix; keeping water out is the prevention.

**Will my insurance cover misfuelling?**
It depends entirely on the policy — some cover it, many don't, and some cover it
only as an optional extra. Check before you need to know.
