# Diesel Generator Service in Australia

A standby generator's job is to start on the day everything else has failed.
That makes it different from every other engine on this site: it spends
almost all its life not running, and it is judged entirely on one start.

## Why standby generators fail

Nearly always because of what happens while they sit, not while they run:

**Fuel degradation.** Diesel degrades in storage. Stored fuel accumulates water
through condensation and supports microbial growth at the fuel-water interface.
Fuel that has sat for a year without treatment or turnover is a leading cause of
generators failing to start or run under load.

**Batteries.** The most common single failure. A battery that has sat on a
marginal charger for two years will not crank an engine in an emergency.

**Coolant and heater faults.** Jacket water heaters keep a standby set warm for
rapid starting. When one fails silently, the set is cold when it is needed.

**Seals and belts perishing** through age rather than use.

**Rodents and insects** in an enclosure that is rarely opened.

**Wet stacking.** Running a diesel generator at very light load for extended
periods means incomplete combustion, unburnt fuel accumulating in the exhaust,
and glazed bores. Generators sized far above their actual load do this to
themselves.

## What a service should include

- Load bank testing — the item most often skipped and most important. A set that
  starts and idles has not been proven; a set that carries rated load has.
- Fuel condition testing, tank inspection for water and growth, polishing or
  replacement where needed
- Battery load testing and replacement on age, not on failure
- Oil, filters and coolant to specification
- Belts, hoses, and the jacket water heater
- Control system and transfer switch operation
- Exhaust and enclosure inspection
- Documented results, with readings

## Critical installations

Sets backing hospitals, data centres, telecommunications and other critical
infrastructure sit under standards and obligations well beyond general
maintenance, including defined testing regimes and record-keeping. If your set is
in that category, the servicing regime is set by those requirements, not by
convenience.

## What it costs

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should a standby generator be serviced?**
Commonly annually for a full service, with more frequent exercise runs. Critical
installations follow the regime their standard requires.

**Is a monthly start-up enough?**
No. A no-load start proves it turns over. Only a load test proves it will carry
the load it exists for.

**How long does stored diesel last?**
Untreated fuel degrades measurably within a year, faster in heat and with water
present. Treatment plus turnover, or periodic replacement, is the answer.

**What is wet stacking?**
Unburnt fuel accumulating in the exhaust from prolonged light-load running. It
glazes bores and fouls the exhaust. Load testing both reveals and helps clear it.

**Why does my generator fail to start when I need it?**
The batteries first, then the fuel. Both are age-related, and both are cheap to
fix in advance.
