# Engine Alignment and Coupling in Australia

Engine alignment means setting the engine and gearbox output so that the coupling
faces meet the propeller shaft squarely, within a tolerance measured in
thousandths of an inch. It is invisible, easy to neglect, and quietly responsible
for a great deal of expensive wear.

## What misalignment destroys

- **The gearbox output bearing and seal**, loaded sideways every revolution
- **The cutless bearing**, wearing unevenly and quickly
- **The stern gland or shaft seal**, which cannot seal against a shaft that is
  moving off-axis
  → [Shaft seal and stern gland service](/services/shaft-seal-stern-gland/)
- **The shaft itself**, which can bend under sustained misalignment
- **Engine mounts**, which take the reaction loads
- **Comfort** — vibration that owners often attribute to the propeller

→ [Propeller shaft vibration](/marine-engine-problems/shaft-vibration/)

## When it needs checking

- After any engine removal, rebuild or repower
- After a grounding or striking something
- When the cutless bearing or shaft seal is replaced
- If engine mounts have been replaced or have sagged with age
- When new vibration appears
- On a new-to-you boat, as a baseline
- Periodically — mounts compress and settle over years

## The detail people miss: it must be done afloat

A hull is not rigid. It changes shape between being supported on a hard stand and
floating on its lines. An alignment set on the hard is not the alignment the boat
has in the water.

Correct practice is to align with the boat afloat, and after it has been back in
the water long enough to settle — commonly a day or more, and longer for timber
hulls, which take up. Doing it immediately on relaunch gives a result that drifts.

If a yard offers to align your engine on the hard as part of a haul-out, ask how
they handle this.

## Engine mounts

Flexible mounts compress, harden and sag with age, and sagging mounts pull the
engine out of alignment gradually. On any older boat, mount condition should be
assessed as part of an alignment — realigning on tired mounts is temporary.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should engine alignment be checked?**
After any engine work, grounding, or mount replacement, and periodically on older
boats as mounts settle. It is cheap relative to the wear it prevents.

**Can alignment be done on the hard?**
It should not be finalised there. Hull shape changes between the hard stand and
the water, so alignment is set afloat once the boat has settled.

**What are the symptoms of misalignment?**
Vibration, a shaft seal that will not stop leaking, a cutless bearing wearing
quickly, or repeated gearbox output seal failure.

**My boat vibrates — is it the propeller or the alignment?**
Either, and it needs checking rather than guessing. A damaged or unbalanced prop
and a misaligned shaft feel similar from the helm.

**Do engine mounts need replacing at the same time?**
If they are old, sagged or hardened, yes. Aligning on tired mounts gives a result
that does not last.
