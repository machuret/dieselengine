# Intercooler Service in Australia

The intercooler cools compressed air between the turbo and the engine. Cooler
air is denser, so the engine gets more oxygen per unit volume and runs cooler.
It is a simple component with no moving parts, and it is neglected accordingly —
usually until a turbo failure fills it with oil.

## Why it needs attention

**After any turbo failure.** A turbo passing oil fills the intercooler and its
piping. That oil does not stay there: after a new turbo is fitted, it is drawn
into the engine. At best it produces blue smoke and fouls the DPF. At worst the
engine ingests enough oil to run on it uncontrollably.
→ [Diesel engine runaway](/diesel-problems/diesel-runaway/)

Cleaning the intercooler is not optional after a turbo failure, and its absence
from a turbo quote is a warning sign.

**Boost leaks.** Intercooler cores crack, and the rubber hoses and clamps that
connect them split, particularly where heat cycling and engine movement work
them. A boost leak feels exactly like a failing turbo and costs a small fraction
to fix.
→ [Boost leak symptoms](/diesel-problems/diesel-boost-leak/)

**External blockage.** Front-mounted intercoolers collect mud, insects, grass
seed and stone damage. A blocked core stops cooling, which raises intake
temperature, which raises combustion temperature and exhaust gas temperature.
Relevant for anyone doing dirt roads, farm work or heavy towing.

**Internal contamination from EGR.** On some engines EGR gas passes through the
charge air system, and the soot-and-oil mix coats the core internally.

## The service

Inspection and pressure-testing of the whole charge air system, external
cleaning of the core, internal cleaning and flushing where contaminated, and
replacement of perished hoses and tired clamps.

Pressure-testing is the valuable part. It finds boost leaks definitively, and it
should be done before any turbo is condemned.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know if my intercooler is leaking?**
A pressure test is definitive. Symptoms — power loss, boost codes, a whistle
under load — overlap almost completely with turbo faults, which is why the test
matters.

**Does an intercooler need cleaning as routine maintenance?**
Not usually as a scheduled item, but it should be inspected if you do dirt
roads, tow heavily, or work in dusty or agricultural conditions.

**My turbo was replaced and now the car smokes — why?**
Very likely oil left in the intercooler from the old turbo being drawn through
the engine. It should have been cleaned during the turbo job.

**Is an upgraded intercooler worthwhile?**
For sustained heavy towing it can reduce intake temperatures meaningfully.
Confirm the effect on compliance and warranty before fitting one.
