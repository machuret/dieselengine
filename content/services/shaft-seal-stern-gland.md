# Shaft Seal and Stern Gland Service in Australia

The stern gland is where the propeller shaft passes through the hull. Its job is
to let the shaft turn while keeping the sea out — and it is the fitting most
likely to sink a boat at its berth.

## The two types

**Traditional packing gland (stuffing box).** Rings of packing material
compressed around the shaft by a threaded nut. It is designed to drip — the water
lubricates and cools the packing. A few drips per minute under way is correct; no
drips at all means it is over-tightened, which overheats the packing and scores
the shaft.

**Dripless / mechanical seal.** A carbon face running against a stainless rotor,
held in compression by a rubber bellows. It does not drip in normal operation.
The bellows is a rubber component with a finite life, and when it fails the water
comes in fast.

Both work well. Both need maintenance. The dripless type gives less warning.

## Symptoms and what they mean

- **A packing gland dripping heavily** — packing worn, needs adjustment or
  repacking
- **A packing gland running hot** — over-tightened; back it off before it scores
  the shaft
- **Any water from a dripless seal** — investigate now, not later
- **Salt crystals or rust staining** around the fitting
- **Squealing on start-up** — a dry seal face
- **A perished, cracked or swollen bellows** on a dripless unit

## The maintenance nobody does

**The bellows on a dripless seal has a service life** — commonly measured in
years, specified by the maker. It is rubber, underwater, under compression, in an
engine bay that gets hot. Owners routinely run them well past their interval
because nothing appears wrong.

When a dripless bellows fails, the ingress rate can exceed the bilge pump. This
is a genuine sinking risk, not a theoretical one.

**Packing has a life too,** and modern packing materials are considerably better
than traditional flax. Repacking is a straightforward job.

**Vent the seal.** Many dripless seals need burping after launch to expel trapped
air, or they run dry and overheat.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should my stern gland drip?**
A traditional packing gland should — a few drips per minute under way. A dripless
seal should not, and any water from one needs investigating.

**How often should a dripless seal bellows be replaced?**
On the manufacturer's interval, typically measured in years. It is rubber under
compression below the waterline, and a failure can sink a boat.

**My stuffing box is hot — is that normal?**
No. It means it is over-tightened and the packing is not being cooled. Back it
off; a scored shaft is an expensive consequence.

**Can I repack a stuffing box myself?**
Many owners do, at haul-out. Doing it afloat is possible on some installations
but is not a beginner's job.

**Why does my seal squeal after launching?**
Usually trapped air. Many dripless seals need venting after launch, or they run
dry.
