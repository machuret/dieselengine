# Marine Engine Rebuild in Australia

Marine diesels are built to be rebuilt. Heavy castings, replaceable bearings and
frequently replaceable liners mean an engine at the end of its service life is a
rebuild candidate rather than scrap — which matters more on a boat than in a car,
because the alternative is a repower involving beds, controls, shaft and
instruments.

→ [Repower or rebuild your marine diesel](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/)

## When a rebuild is the answer

- Low compression across cylinders, excessive blow-by, high oil consumption
- Bearing knock or low oil pressure from wear
- Seawater ingress damage
- Internal corrosion from long neglect or depleted coolant
- Overheating damage: scored bores, a cracked head, a warped block
- The engine is a good design, well supported, in a boat worth keeping

## In or out of the boat?

This is the first question, and it drives the cost.

**In situ** work is possible on some installations for a top-end overhaul, and
avoids the substantial cost of extraction.

**Removal** is required for a full rebuild, and on many boats the engine cannot
come out without removing a hatch, a section of deck, or occasionally a window.
Establish how your engine comes out before agreeing to anything — it is a genuine
and sometimes decisive cost.

## What a full rebuild involves

**Strip and measure.** Every component against specification. A pre-strip quote
is an estimate and any honest workshop will say so.

**Machining.** Boring and honing or re-linering, crank grinding, deck and head
surfacing, crack testing.

**New components.** Pistons, rings, bearings, gaskets, seals, oil pump, valve
train, and marine-specific items — heat exchanger, exhaust elbow, hoses.

**Assembly to measured clearances,** with correct torque sequences.

**Marine ancillaries, which decide whether it survives.** A freshly rebuilt
engine behind a blocked heat exchanger, a corroded exhaust elbow, worn injectors
or an unaddressed fuel tank will not stay fresh. These belong in the quote.

**Alignment and commissioning.** After a rebuild the engine must be realigned to
the shaft, and run in properly.
→ [Engine alignment and coupling](/services/engine-alignment/)

## Ask these before committing

- Does the engine have to come out, and how?
- Who does the machining?
- Are the heat exchanger, exhaust elbow, injectors and fuel tank in scope?
- What caused the failure, and how is that being addressed?
- Is the quote pre-strip or post-strip?
- What does the warranty cover and what voids it?

## Frequently asked questions

**Rebuild or repower?**
Rebuild keeps the beds, controls, shaft and instruments as they are, which is why
it frequently wins on a boat even when a car owner would replace. Repower makes
sense when the engine is obsolete, parts are unavailable, or you want modern
efficiency and support.

**Does the engine have to come out?**
For a full rebuild, usually. How it comes out varies enormously between boats and
is a real cost — establish it early.

**How long does a marine rebuild take?**
Commonly several weeks to a few months, driven by machining queues, parts and
access.

**Why did the price rise after they stripped it?**
Because a pre-strip quote cannot know what is inside. A firm quote before
stripping is a guess, and usually a low one.

**How long will a rebuilt marine engine last?**
Properly done with the original cause corrected, comparable to a new engine.
The cause is the variable — an engine that failed from cooling neglect will fail
again if the cooling is not fixed.
