# Marine Engine Repower and Replacement in Australia

A repower is the biggest decision most boat owners make, and the most commonly
underestimated. The engine is rarely more than half the cost. The rest is
everything the engine touches.

→ [Repower or rebuild your marine diesel](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/)

## What a repower actually involves

- **Removing the old engine,** which may require lifting a hatch, cutting an
  access panel, removing a window, or craning through the deck
- **Engine beds,** frequently needing modification or rebuilding for a different
  footprint
- **Mounts,** and realignment to the shaft
- **Gearbox,** which may or may not carry over — ratio and coupling must suit the
  propeller
- **Propeller,** commonly needing repitching or replacement to match the new
  engine's power and RPM
- **Shaft and coupling** alignment
- **Exhaust system,** sized and configured for the new engine, with correct riser
  height and loop geometry
- **Fuel system,** tank condition, lines, filtration — a new engine fed from a
  contaminated tank is a fast way to destroy an injection system
- **Cooling,** seacock, strainer and hose sizing to suit the new engine's demand
- **Electrical,** wiring loom, alarms, charging
- **Instruments and controls,** often entirely new on a modern electronic engine
- **Sea trial and commissioning**

Owners who budget for an engine and get a quote for a project are usually seeing
this list for the first time.

## Choosing the replacement

**Match the boat, not the brochure.** More power than the hull can use costs fuel
and achieves nothing. The correct engine is the one that lets the boat reach its
designed speed at the engine's rated RPM with a suitable propeller.

**Consider physical fit** — height, length, beds, and whether it will pass
through the access route.

**Weight matters** on a small boat, affecting trim and handling.

**Support and parts in Australia** should weigh heavily. An engine with a thin
local dealer network is a problem you own for twenty years.

**Electronic or mechanical.** Modern electronic engines are cleaner and more
efficient, and they need brand-specific diagnostics that not every regional
operator holds. For remote cruising that is a real consideration.

**Repowering with the same brand and family** is usually cheapest, because beds,
controls, exhaust and instruments are more likely to carry over.

## Timing

Combine a repower with a haul-out and other major work. The boat is already out,
access is already open, and the shaft and stern gear should be inspected while
they are reachable.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much of a repower is the engine?**
Frequently around half or less. Beds, exhaust, propeller, controls, instruments,
alignment and commissioning make up the rest.

**Should I fit a more powerful engine?**
Usually not. A displacement hull has a speed limit set by its waterline, and
excess power burns fuel without adding speed. Match the engine to the hull.

**Will my existing gearbox and propeller carry over?**
Sometimes, but the ratio and pitch must suit the new engine's power and RPM. A
carried-over propeller is a common reason a repowered boat underperforms.

**How long does a repower take?**
Weeks to months depending on access, parts and yard availability.

**Is repowering with the same brand cheaper?**
Usually, because beds, controls, exhaust and instruments are more likely to fit.

**Should I repower or rebuild?**
Rebuild if the engine is well supported and the installation is sound. Repower if
the engine is obsolete, parts are scarce, or you want modern efficiency.
