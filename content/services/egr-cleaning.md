# EGR Cleaning in Australia

The EGR valve recirculates a measured amount of exhaust gas back into the
intake. Exhaust gas is inert, so it lowers peak combustion temperature, and
lower combustion temperature means less NOx. That is the entire purpose.

The cost of it is soot. You are deliberately routing sooty exhaust into the
intake tract, and over time that soot combines with oil vapour from the crankcase
breather to form a hard carbon deposit that coats the valve, the cooler and the
intake manifold.

→ [How a common rail diesel system works](/guides/how-a-common-rail-system-works/)

## Symptoms of a clogged EGR system

- Loss of power, often with limp mode
- Rough idle, particularly when cold
- Black smoke under acceleration
- A fault code for EGR position, flow, or insufficient/excessive flow
- Poor fuel economy
- On EGR cooler failure: coolant loss with no external leak, white smoke

## What the job involves

**Cleaning the valve.** Removal, chemical and mechanical cleaning, checking the
actuator or solenoid, and resetting adaptations. Straightforward on some
engines, a substantial strip-down on others — engine access is the single
biggest variable in the price.

**Cleaning the intake manifold.** Usually the more important half, and often
skipped. A clean valve feeding a manifold with a 10mm carbon lining has not
solved the problem. On some engines the manifold has to come off and be cleaned
or replaced.

**Checking the EGR cooler.** A cracked cooler leaks coolant into the exhaust
stream — a different and more serious fault, and one that gets misdiagnosed as a
head gasket.
→ [Losing coolant with no visible leak](/diesel-problems/diesel-coolant-loss-no-leak/)

**Checking the crankcase breather.** Oil vapour from the breather is half of
what makes the deposit stick. Excessive breather oil means excessive blow-by,
which is an engine condition problem, not an EGR problem.
→ [Excessive blow-by](/diesel-problems/diesel-excessive-blowby/)

## Clean, or replace?

Cleaning is worth doing when the valve is mechanically sound and the actuator
works. Replace when the valve is seized, the actuator or position sensor has
failed, or the housing is worn. On a number of engines the cost of thorough
cleaning approaches the cost of a new valve, and replacement is the better buy —
ask the workshop to price both.

## Why it comes back

EGR fouling is a consequence of the design and the duty cycle, not a defect. It
recurs faster on vehicles doing short trips and heavy idling, because low
exhaust temperature means wetter, stickier deposits. A vehicle that gets regular
sustained runs fouls far more slowly.

Deleting the EGR system is not a legal answer on a road-registered vehicle.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often does an EGR valve need cleaning?**
It varies enormously with duty cycle. Short-trip vehicles may need it every
60,000–80,000km; vehicles doing sustained highway running may never need it.

**Do EGR cleaning additives work?**
Intake-system cleaners can slow deposit formation on a system that is only
lightly fouled. They will not clear a manifold that is already choked.

**Is a blocked EGR valve related to my DPF problem?**
Frequently, yes. A malfunctioning EGR system changes combustion and soot
production, which loads the DPF faster. They should be diagnosed together.
→ [DPF cleaning](/services/dpf-cleaning/)

**Should the intake manifold be cleaned at the same time?**
Almost always. Cleaning the valve alone, on a heavily fouled engine, is a
short-lived repair.
