# Aftercooler and Charge Air Cooler Service in Australia

On a turbocharged marine diesel, the turbo compresses intake air and heats it in
doing so. The aftercooler cools that air before it enters the engine, making it
denser. On most marine installations it does that using raw seawater — which
means a seawater-cooled heat exchanger sitting directly in your engine's intake
tract.

That arrangement is why aftercoolers matter far more on a boat than an
intercooler does on a truck. If it corrodes through, seawater goes straight into
the cylinders.

## Why they fail

**Seawater-side corrosion.** The primary failure. Salt water plus dissimilar
metals plus heat, on a component with thin tube walls.

**Marine growth and scale** restricting the seawater side, which reduces cooling
and raises intake temperature.

**Neglected anodes.** Many aftercoolers have their own anode. When it is gone,
the aftercooler corrodes instead.
→ [Anode inspection and replacement](/services/anode-replacement/)

**Oil fouling on the air side** from turbo seal leakage or crankcase breather
carryover, which insulates the tubes.

## Symptoms

- Loss of power at the top end, and higher exhaust temperature
- Black smoke under load
- Salt water in the intake manifold or in a cylinder — the serious one
- White smoke and rough running, if water is entering
- Hard starting or a hydrolocked engine after sitting

The progression matters. An aftercooler that has begun to perforate may only
weep under pressure at first, then increasingly, then enough to hydrolock the
engine while it sits.

→ [Saltwater in the engine oil](/marine-engine-problems/saltwater-in-engine-oil/) ·
[Marine engine hydrolock](/marine-engine-problems/marine-engine-hydrolock/)

## The service

- Removal and dismantling; end caps off
- Cleaning of both the seawater side (descaling, growth removal) and the air side
  (oil and carbon)
- **Pressure testing** — the essential step, confirming no path between seawater
  and air sides
- New O-rings, seals and gaskets
- Anode replacement
- Inspection of the turbo, since oil on the air side means a turbo seal issue

Many engine makers specify an aftercooler service interval. It is one of the
most commonly skipped items on marine engines, and one of the most consequential.

## If yours has already leaked

Water in the intake is not a "clean it and carry on" situation. The engine needs
inspection for water in the cylinders, corrosion of valves and bores, and
contamination of the oil. Depending on how long it ran, the range runs from an
oil change and inspection to a rebuild.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should an aftercooler be serviced?**
Most manufacturers specify an interval — commonly measured in hundreds of hours
or a small number of years. It is frequently skipped, and it is one of the few
maintenance items that can put seawater directly into an engine.

**How do I know if my aftercooler is leaking?**
Pressure testing is the only reliable way. Symptoms — power loss, white smoke,
water in the intake — usually appear once damage is already occurring.

**Does my aftercooler have an anode?**
Many do. If yours does and it has never been changed, that is a strong reason to
have the unit serviced now.

**Why does an aftercooler matter more on a boat than a truck?**
Because it is cooled by seawater rather than air. A corroded automotive
intercooler leaks air; a corroded marine aftercooler leaks seawater into your
engine.
