# Diesel Injector Testing in Australia

Testing before replacing is the difference between a diagnosed repair and an
expensive guess. Diesel fuel injection specialists do this work on calibrated
benches; general workshops send it out.

## The tests, from cheapest to most definitive

**Leak-off test (on vehicle).** Return lines are disconnected and flow into
measuring containers while the engine runs. An injector returning substantially
more than its neighbours is passing internally. Quick, inexpensive, and often
enough to identify a clearly failed injector.

**Live data correction values (on vehicle).** The ECU's per-cylinder fuel trims,
read with diagnostic equipment. Useful, but not conclusive on its own — low
compression produces similar readings.

**Back-leakage and response testing (bench).** The injector removed and tested
under controlled pressure.

**Full bench calibration test.** Delivery volume measured at multiple test
points across the operating range, plus spray pattern and opening pressure,
compared against the manufacturer's specification. This is the definitive
answer, and it produces a document.

## Why the test certificate matters

A bench test produces measured numbers against specification. That document is
what tells you whether an injector is genuinely faulty, and whether a
"reconditioned" injector you are being sold is actually within specification.

If someone offers you tested or reconditioned injectors without test data, they
are asking you to take it on faith. Don't.

## What testing rules in and out

A good test distinguishes between:

- An injector with a worn nozzle over-delivering, causing smoke and heat
- An injector passing internally, diluting the oil with fuel
- An injector that is fine, on a cylinder with low compression — a mechanical
  problem
- An injector that is fine, on an engine with a fuel supply or pump problem
  affecting all cylinders

Those four look similar from the driver's seat and cost wildly different amounts
to fix.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can injectors be tested without removing them?**
A leak-off test and live correction data can be done on the vehicle and will
often identify a clearly failed injector. Definitive testing requires removal.

**Is testing worth it if I'm probably replacing them anyway?**
Yes — because "probably" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Testing
regularly finds that the fault is compression or fuel supply, not injectors.

**How long does bench testing take?**
Typically a day or two once the specialist has the injectors, depending on their
workload.

**What if the test says the injectors are fine?**
Then you have saved a large sum and the diagnosis moves to compression, fuel
supply, the high-pressure pump, or air intake.
