# DPF Cleaning in Australia

The diesel particulate filter is the most common source of modern diesel
complaints in Australia, and the great majority of those complaints come down to
one thing: the vehicle is not being driven in a way that lets the filter clean
itself.

## How a DPF is supposed to work

The filter traps soot from the exhaust. Periodically the engine raises exhaust
temperature — usually to somewhere in the range of 550–650°C — and burns the
trapped soot to ash. That is regeneration, and it happens automatically when
conditions allow: sustained load, sustained speed, adequate fuel level, no
active fault.

Ash, unlike soot, does not burn off. It accumulates permanently over the
filter's life, and it is what eventually ends a DPF regardless of how well the
vehicle is driven. The wrong engine oil accelerates this considerably.

→ [How a DPF works](/guides/how-a-dpf-works/)

## Why they block

**Short trips.** The dominant cause in Australian cities. The filter never
reaches regeneration temperature, so soot accumulates faster than it clears.
→ [Why short trips damage diesel engines](/guides/short-trips-kill-diesel-engines/)

**Interrupted regenerations.** Switching the engine off during an active
regeneration leaves partially burnt soot behind, and dumps fuel into the sump.

**An underlying fault.** This is the one that matters most. A leaking injector,
a stuck EGR valve, a failing turbo or a worn engine producing excess soot will
block a DPF, and cleaning it without fixing that fault just resets the clock.
Any workshop that cleans a DPF without asking why it blocked is selling you the
same job again in six months.

**Wrong oil.** Non-low-SAPS oil deposits ash the filter cannot burn.

## What DPF cleaning actually means

Three different things get sold under this name, and they are not equivalent:

**Forced regeneration.** The workshop commands a regeneration with diagnostic
equipment. Appropriate for a filter loaded with soot but not yet critical.
Requires manufacturer-level tooling, not a generic scanner. Fastest and
cheapest, but does nothing for ash.

**On-vehicle chemical cleaning.** Cleaning solution introduced through the
exhaust or a sensor port, then a regeneration. Middle ground. Effectiveness
varies with how badly loaded the filter is.

**Off-vehicle cleaning.** The filter is removed and cleaned in a dedicated
machine — typically thermal or aqueous, with before-and-after flow testing.
This is the only method that genuinely addresses ash, and the only one that
comes with a measurement proving it worked. Ask for the flow test results.

If a workshop cannot tell you which of the three they are doing, go elsewhere.

## When cleaning won't work

A DPF that is cracked, melted, or has reached the end of its ash capacity cannot
be cleaned. Melting usually follows a runaway regeneration caused by an
unaddressed fault. At that point it is replacement, and replacement is
expensive.

## Removal is not an option

Removing or defeating a DPF on a road-registered vehicle is not legal in
Australia. State EPA legislation carries penalties, it affects your insurance
position, and it makes the vehicle non-compliant. Any workshop offering it is
offering to create a problem for you.

## Preventing the next one

Once the filter is clean, the fault that blocked it needs fixing, and the
driving pattern needs to allow regeneration — a sustained run at highway speed
every week or two is usually enough. If your usage genuinely cannot provide
that, a diesel may be the wrong vehicle for you, and that is worth knowing.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often does a DPF need cleaning?**
A well-driven, fault-free vehicle may never need an off-vehicle clean before the
filter reaches end of ash life. A short-trip vehicle may need attention every
year or two. The driving pattern determines it, not the odometer.

**Can I clean a DPF myself with an additive?**
Additives sold as DPF cleaners can help a lightly loaded filter regenerate more
easily. They do not clear a blocked filter and they do nothing about ash. They
are not a substitute for finding out why it blocked.

**How long does DPF cleaning take?**
A forced regeneration is under an hour. On-vehicle chemical cleaning is a few
hours. Off-vehicle cleaning is same-day if the workshop has the machine on site,
and several days if the filter is sent away — ask which before you book.

**Will cleaning fix my problem permanently?**
Only if the underlying cause is also fixed. A DPF that blocked because of a
leaking injector will block again.

**My DPF light came on — can I keep driving?**
A steady warning generally means the filter needs a regeneration: a sustained
highway run may clear it. A flashing light, a limp-mode restriction, or
additional warnings means stop and get it diagnosed.
