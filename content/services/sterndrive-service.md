# Sterndrive and Outdrive Service in Australia

A sterndrive puts the engine inside the hull and the drive unit outside the
transom. It is compact and efficient, and it introduces a set of wear items that
shaft-drive boats simply do not have — most of which sit below the waterline and
fail expensively when neglected.

→ [Shaft drive vs sterndrive vs IPS](/guides/shaft-vs-sterndrive-vs-ips/)

## The wear items

**Bellows.** Rubber boots sealing the drive shaft, exhaust and shift cable
openings through the transom. They perish with age, UV and ozone, and when the
drive bellows splits, water enters the boat. This is the highest-consequence item
on a sterndrive, and it is age-based rather than use-based.

Most manufacturers specify a replacement interval — commonly a small number of
years. Boats routinely exceed it, because nothing appears wrong right up until
water is coming in.
→ [Sterndrive bellows failure](/marine-engine-problems/sterndrive-bellows-failure/)

**Gimbal bearing.** Supports the drive shaft where it passes through the transom.
Fails from water ingress past a failed bellows, and rumbles or squeals.

**U-joints.** Wear and corrode; a failed U-joint can leave you without drive.

**Drive oil and seals.** Milky drive oil means water is entering past a seal —
act on it, because seawater in a gear case destroys bearings quickly.

**Anodes.** Sterndrives carry several, and they waste faster than hull anodes.
→ [Anode inspection and replacement](/services/anode-replacement/)

**Shift cable and mechanism,** which fail progressively and cause hard shifting.

**Trim system.** Rams, hoses and the pump; leaks and corrosion are common.

## The service

- Drive oil change, with the old oil inspected for water and metal
- Bellows inspection and replacement at interval
- Gimbal bearing check and greasing
- U-joint inspection
- Anode replacement
- Trim system inspection
- Shift cable adjustment
- Propeller and prop shaft seal check

Most of this requires the boat out of the water and the drive removed —
so sterndrive servicing is normally a haul-out job.
→ [Slipway and haul-out engine work](/services/slipway-haul-out/)

## The pattern that costs owners the most

A perished bellows lets water into the gimbal housing. The gimbal bearing
corrodes and fails. Its failure damages the drive shaft and U-joints. What began
as an inexpensive rubber part becomes a drive rebuild — and in the worst case a
boat taking on water at the berth.

Replace bellows on time. It is the clearest example on this site of cheap
prevention against expensive failure.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should sterndrive bellows be replaced?**
On the manufacturer's interval — commonly a few years — regardless of hours. They
fail from age and UV, not use, and a split drive bellows lets water into the boat.

**My drive oil is milky — what does that mean?**
Water has entered past a seal. Act promptly; seawater in a gear case destroys
bearings and gears quickly.

**Can sterndrive work be done in the water?**
Some inspection, but proper servicing needs the boat hauled and the drive
removed.

**Are sterndrives higher maintenance than shaft drives?**
Yes, meaningfully. More wear items, more anodes, and more of them below the
waterline. The trade-off is efficiency and packaging.

**What does a rumbling noise from the transom mean?**
Commonly a failing gimbal bearing, usually the consequence of a bellows that let
water in. Have it checked before it takes the U-joints and shaft with it.
