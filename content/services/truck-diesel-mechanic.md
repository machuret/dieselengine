# Truck and Heavy Vehicle Diesel Mechanics in Australia

Heavy vehicle diesel work is a different trade from light vehicle work — not a
bigger version of it. Different engines, different diagnostic systems, different
licensing, different premises, and a completely different economic driver:
downtime, not labour rate.

## Why downtime dominates

For a working truck, a day off the road usually costs far more than the
difference between two workshops' hourly rates. That reverses the normal
decision:

- A more expensive workshop that turns the vehicle around overnight is usually
  cheaper overall
- Preventive maintenance scheduled around your operation beats reactive repair
- After-hours and weekend capability has genuine value
- Parts availability for your engine matters more than the labour rate

Ask about turnaround and after-hours access before you ask about the rate.

## Emissions systems on trucks

Modern trucks carry DPF and SCR systems, and the consequences of a fault are
sharper than on a car: derates that cut power mid-trip, and no-start conditions
when an AdBlue countdown expires. On a vehicle earning money, that is the
difference between a service and a stranded load.

Urban distribution work — constant stop-start, heavy idling — is hard on both
systems, which is why Australian metro fleets see disproportionate DPF and SCR
work.

## Compliance

Vehicles operating under the Heavy Vehicle National Law carry maintenance
obligations well beyond a light vehicle roadworthy, including maintenance
management and record-keeping expectations. Chain of Responsibility means those
obligations extend beyond the driver. Your workshop should understand this and
keep records accordingly — if they do not raise it, they may not work on
commercial vehicles often.

## Choosing a heavy vehicle workshop

1. Do they work on your engine brand daily, with the manufacturer diagnostic
   software?
2. What is realistic turnaround, and do they offer after-hours drop-off?
3. Can they do mobile or on-site work at your yard?
4. Do they stock or quickly source parts for your fleet's engines?
5. Do their records satisfy your maintenance management obligations?

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a car diesel mechanic work on my truck?**
Generally no. Different diagnostic systems, different licensing, and most
light-vehicle premises physically cannot lift a heavy vehicle.

**How do I minimise truck downtime?**
Preventive servicing scheduled around your operation, a workshop with your
engine's parts and software, and after-hours or on-site capability.

**Why does my truck keep having DPF problems?**
Urban stop-start work and heavy idling prevent regeneration. It is the duty
cycle, and it is why metro distribution fleets see it most.

**What happens if AdBlue runs out in a truck?**
The system counts down and then prevents restart. Act on the first warning.
→ [AdBlue and SCR repair](/services/adblue-scr-repair/)

**Does my workshop need to keep maintenance records for compliance?**
Under the Heavy Vehicle National Law, maintenance record-keeping matters to your
compliance position. Use a workshop that understands it.
