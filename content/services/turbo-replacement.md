# Turbocharger Replacement in Australia

When a turbo cannot be rebuilt — damaged housings, shaft contact, or an
integrated electronic actuator that cannot be separately calibrated —
replacement is the job. Doing it properly is mostly about everything other than
the turbo.

→ [Turbocharger repair](/services/turbo-repair/) for whether yours can be
rebuilt instead.

## What a correct replacement includes

A turbo fitted on its own, into an unaddressed system, is the most common reason
people buy two turbos in a year. A complete job includes:

**Finding and fixing the cause.** Oil starvation, oil contamination, a
restricted drain, or foreign object damage. Fitting a new turbo without this is
not a repair.

**Oil and filter change, and an oil system flush.** Debris from the failed turbo
is in the oil. It will find the new bearings.

**New oil feed line — not the old one cleaned.** Feed lines carbon up
internally, and a partially restricted feed is exactly what killed the last
turbo. This is a cheap part and a non-negotiable one.

**Checking the oil drain.** A restricted drain pressurises the centre housing
and forces oil past the seals.

**Cleaning the intercooler and intake piping.** A failed turbo fills the
intercooler with oil. Left there, it is drawn into the engine after the new
turbo is fitted — which at worst causes a runaway.
→ [Intercooler service](/services/intercooler-service/) ·
[Diesel engine runaway](/diesel-problems/diesel-runaway/)

**Checking the air filter and intake tract** for the debris source, if there was
foreign object damage.

**Actuator calibration.** Electronic actuators on modern VNT turbos require
calibration to the vehicle. An uncalibrated actuator produces boost faults on a
brand new turbo.

**Priming before first start.** Cranking oil pressure up before the turbo spins.

Ask which of these are included in the quote. The difference between a cheap
quote and a complete one is usually this list.

## New, reconditioned, or aftermarket?

**Genuine new** — highest cost, correct specification, full warranty.

**Reconditioned exchange** — a legitimate middle ground from a reputable turbo
specialist, balanced and tested, usually with a warranty. Ask about balancing
and what the warranty covers.

**Budget aftermarket** — highly variable. Some are competent; some fail quickly
and take the engine's oil supply with them. On a component spinning at 100,000+
rpm, the cheapest option is a genuine risk.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is one quote so much cheaper than another?**
Usually because it covers the turbo and the labour to swap it, and not the oil
flush, feed line, intercooler cleaning and actuator calibration. Compare the
inclusions, not the totals.

**Do I really need the intercooler cleaned?**
Yes, if the old turbo passed oil. Oil left in the intercooler is drawn into the
engine afterwards, and in the worst case causes a runaway.

**Can I fit an upgraded turbo instead?**
Possible, but it affects emissions compliance, warranty and insurance, and needs
supporting fuelling and cooling changes.
→ [ADR compliance for engine modifications](/guides/adr-compliance-engine-modifications/)

**How long does the job take?**
Commonly a day, longer on engines where access is poor or where the exhaust
manifold studs shear — which is common on high-kilometre vehicles.

**What warranty should I expect?**
Ask specifically whether the warranty is void if the cause of the original
failure is not documented as corrected. Reputable suppliers require it, and that
is a sign of a good supplier, not a bad one.
