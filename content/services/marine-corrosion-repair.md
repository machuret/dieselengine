# Marine Engine Corrosion Repair in Australia

Corrosion is the background process on every marine engine in Australia. It
never stops, it works from the inside as well as the outside, and it is the
reason marine engines are retired far more often than they are worn out.

## The kinds, and what each attacks

**Galvanic corrosion.** Dissimilar metals in an electrolyte. Controlled by
sacrificial anodes, and uncontrolled when those anodes are neglected.
→ [Anode inspection and replacement](/services/anode-replacement/)

**Stray current corrosion.** An electrical fault putting current through the
water and into the metal. This is fast and destructive — it can consume a
propeller or drive leg in a season. Causes include wiring faults, poor bonding,
and shore power problems, sometimes originating from a neighbouring boat.

**Crevice corrosion.** Under fittings, in joints, beneath fasteners, where
oxygen is excluded. Common on stainless in salt water.

**Internal corrosion of the coolant circuit.** Depleted coolant inhibitors let
the block, head and cooling passages corrode from inside. This is invisible until
it isn't.
→ [Marine cooling system repair](/services/marine-cooling-system-repair/)

**Atmospheric salt attack.** Every engine bay in Australia lives in salt air.
Alternator cases, starter motors, electrical connections, terminals and earth
points corrode, and a very high proportion of "engine" faults are actually a
corroded connection.
→ [Marine engine electrical](/services/marine-engine-electrical/)

## Where it shows up first

- Alternator and starter cases, and every electrical terminal
- Exhaust elbow and manifold
- Heat exchanger and aftercooler end caps
- Engine mounts and their fasteners
- Gearbox housing and cooler
- Fuel and coolant fittings

## Repair, and when it is not worth repairing

Some corrosion is repairable — cleaning, treating, replacing fasteners, remaking
electrical connections, replacing individual components. Some is not. A block or
head with corrosion into a water jacket, or a manifold corroded through, is a
replacement.

The honest assessment is worth paying for. It is common for a corrosion
inspection to conclude that an engine is economically finished, and knowing that
before spending on a series of individual repairs saves real money.

→ [Repower or rebuild your marine diesel](/guides/repower-vs-rebuild/)

## Prevention, which is the whole point

- Anodes correct for your water, inspected and replaced on time
- Coolant with live inhibitors, changed on schedule
- Engine bay ventilation — trapped humid salt air is the enemy
- Electrical connections protected and inspected; a corroded earth causes faults
  that look mechanical
- Freshwater rinsing where practical
- Investigate anodes wasting abnormally fast rather than just replacing them

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is my engine corroding when it never touches seawater directly?**
Salt-laden air in the engine bay is enough. Electrical connections and alloy
housings corrode from the atmosphere alone.

**What is stray current corrosion?**
An electrical fault putting current through the water, dramatically accelerating
metal loss. It can destroy a propeller or drive leg in months and needs
electrical diagnosis, not more anodes.

**Can a corroded engine block be repaired?**
Sometimes locally, but corrosion into a water jacket usually ends the block. Get
an honest assessment before spending on piecemeal repairs.

**Does tropical water corrode faster?**
Yes. Warm, saline water accelerates galvanic activity considerably, which is why
northern Australian boats need shorter anode intervals.
