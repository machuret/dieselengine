# Mobile Marine Mechanics in Australia

On a boat, mobile is the norm rather than the exception. Most marine diesel work
happens where the boat lives — at a marina berth, on a swing mooring reached by
tender, on a private pontoon, or in a dry stack.

## What can be done at the boat

- Servicing: oil, filters, fuel filters, water separator, impeller, belts
- Diagnosis, with the same equipment a workshop uses
- Cooling system work: strainers, hoses, thermostats, heat exchanger service
- Anodes above the waterline, including engine internal anodes
- Fuel system: filters, bleeding, lift pumps, water separator work
- Electrical: alternators, starters, batteries, alarms, instrumentation
- Exhaust elbow inspection and replacement
- Gearbox oil and cable adjustment
- Pre-purchase inspections and sea trials
- Emergency response

That covers the large majority of marine engine work.

## What needs a workshop or a haul-out

- **Bench work**: injectors, pumps, turbochargers, gearbox rebuilds
- **Machining and engine reconditioning**
- **Anything below the waterline**: shaft, cutless bearing, propeller, skin
  fittings, saildrive seals, sterndrive removal, hull anodes
  → [Slipway and haul-out engine work](/services/slipway-haul-out/)
- **Engine removal** for a rebuild or repower

## Access is the thing to settle first

This determines whether the job is possible and what it costs.

**Marina berth** — straightforward, and the cheapest attendance.

**Swing mooring** — the mechanic needs a tender, everything has to be carried,
and the weather has a vote. It adds real time and cost, and some operators do not
service moorings at all.

**Private pontoon** — depends on access and whether they can get a vehicle
reasonably close.

**Dry stack** — the boat usually has to be launched, which is a coordination
problem more than a technical one.

**On the water, under way** — for diagnosing faults that only appear under load.

Tell the operator which of these applies when you first call. It is the single
most useful thing you can say.

## What to check before booking

1. Do they have the diagnostic equipment for your engine?
2. Do they cover your marina or waterway? Coverage is genuinely patchy,
   particularly in Sydney.
3. Will they attend a mooring, if that is where your boat is?
4. What is the call-out or attendance charge, and is it credited?
5. What happens if the job turns out to need a workshop?
6. Are they insured to work on your vessel, and does your marina require
   accreditation from contractors?

That last point catches people out — many marinas require contractors to be
registered and insured with them before they can work on site.

## What it costs

Attendance plus labour, with attendance varying substantially by access.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a mobile marine mechanic more expensive?**
Attendance is charged, but for most marine work there is no alternative — the
boat cannot come to them. Berth attendance is cheaper than mooring attendance.

**Will they come out to a swing mooring?**
Many will, with a tender, and it costs more. Some will not. Ask when booking, and
say up front that the boat is on a mooring.

**Can they do a full service at the berth?**
Yes. Servicing, cooling work, fuel system, electrical and most repairs happen
dockside as standard.

**Does my marina need to approve them?**
Frequently yes. Many marinas require contractors to be registered and insured
with them before working on site.

**What if they can't fix it at the boat?**
Ask before booking. Most charge attendance and diagnostic time and can arrange or
recommend the workshop or haul-out required.
