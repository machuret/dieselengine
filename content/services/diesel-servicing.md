# Diesel Servicing and Log Book Service in Australia

Servicing a diesel is not servicing a petrol engine with different oil. Three
items on a diesel service have no petrol equivalent and each of them, done
badly, causes an expensive failure later.

## What a proper diesel service covers

**Engine oil to the correct specification.** Not just the right viscosity — the
right specification. Diesel particulate filters require low-SAPS oil (low
sulphated ash, phosphorus and sulphur). Standard oil in a DPF-equipped engine
deposits ash the filter cannot burn off, and ash accumulation is permanent. A
few services with the wrong oil measurably shortens DPF life.

**Fuel filter and water separator.** The fuel filter is the only thing standing
between contaminated fuel and a high-pressure injection system. Draining the
water separator is a routine item that people skip until it costs them a set of
injectors.
→ [Water in fuel warning light](/diesel-problems/diesel-water-in-fuel-light/)

**Air filtration, checked properly.** Not just replaced — the intake tract
inspected for dust tracking past the filter. Dust past the filter is an abrasive
going straight into the engine, and it is a leading cause of premature wear on
Australian vehicles that run unsealed roads.

Plus the usual: coolant condition and concentration, belts, hoses, brakes,
driveline, and a scan for stored fault codes.

## Service intervals, and why the book is often wrong for you

Manufacturer intervals assume normal operating conditions. Most Australian
diesel use is not normal by the manufacturer's definition. Severe-duty
conditions — which shorten the interval, usually by half — typically include
towing, sustained heavy loads, dusty conditions, extensive idling, and
predominantly short trips.

That describes a very large share of Australian diesel ownership. If you tow, or
run unsealed roads, or only do short suburban trips, the severe-duty schedule is
your schedule, not an optional upgrade.

## Log book servicing and your warranty

An independent workshop can perform log book servicing without affecting your
new-vehicle warranty, provided the work meets the manufacturer's schedule, uses
specified fluids and parts, and is properly documented and stamped. This is
protected under Australian Consumer Law. A dealer telling you otherwise is
wrong.

Keep every invoice. The documentation is what protects you, not the stamp alone.

## The low-kilometre trap

Oil degrades with time and contamination, not just distance. A vehicle doing
5,000km a year still needs annual servicing — arguably more careful servicing,
because short trips mean fuel dilution in the oil, water accumulation and a DPF
that never fully regenerates.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should I service a diesel?**
Follow the manufacturer's schedule, then check whether your usage meets their
severe-duty definition — towing, dust, short trips, heavy loads. If it does, use
the shortened interval.

**Does the oil specification really matter?**
On a DPF-equipped engine, yes, considerably. Non-low-SAPS oil deposits ash the
filter cannot clear, and that damage does not reverse.

**Can an independent do my log book service?**
Yes, without affecting your warranty, provided the work meets specification and
is documented.

**My car does barely any kilometres — can I skip a year?**
No. Time-based degradation, fuel dilution and moisture happen regardless of
distance.
