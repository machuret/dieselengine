# Diesel Bug Treatment and Removal in Australia

"Diesel bug" is microbial contamination — bacteria, yeasts and fungi living in
the fuel tank. They live at the boundary between the diesel and any water that
has accumulated beneath it, feeding on the fuel and using the water to survive.

It is a marine problem far more than a road problem, for one reason: boats have
large tanks that sit part-full for months in humid conditions, which is precisely
the environment the organisms need.

## What it actually does

**Blocks filters.** The organisms form a mat and produce sludge that blocks
primary and secondary filters, often suddenly and usually under load.

**Corrodes the tank.** The by-products are acidic. Established growth attacks
aluminium and steel tanks from inside.

**Damages injection equipment.** What gets past the filters is abrasive and
acidic, going into a system with microscopic clearances.

**Comes back.** This is the part owners underestimate. If you kill the organisms
but leave the dead biomass and the water in the tank, you have left both the food
and the habitat for the next colony.

## Symptoms

- Filters blocking repeatedly, particularly in rough conditions
- Dark slime, jelly or stringy material in the filter bowl or on the element
- Loss of power under load, clearing at idle
- Water in the separator
- A sour or sulphurous smell from the tank
- Black specks or sludge in a tank bottom sample

## Treatment done properly

**1. Confirm it.** Sample the tank bottom. Test kits exist and are worth using —
several other faults produce similar filter blocking.

**2. Remove the water.** Without water the organisms cannot live. This is the
step that determines whether the problem returns.

**3. Kill the growth with a biocide.** Use a marine-specific product at the
correct shock dose, and follow the contact time. Underdosing selects for
resistant organisms rather than killing them.

**4. Remove the dead biomass.** Killing the colony converts it into a large
quantity of sludge that then blocks your filters. This is why boats frequently
have worse filter problems immediately after treatment. Polishing or tank
cleaning after treatment is part of the job, not an optional extra.
→ [Fuel polishing and tank cleaning](/services/fuel-polishing/)

**5. Replace all filters,** primary and secondary.

**6. Address why water got in.** A poorly sealing deck fill, a failed tank vent,
condensation from long part-full storage, or contaminated fuel at the supplier.
Without this the cycle restarts.

## Prevention

- Keep tanks as full as practical during layup
- Drain the water separator routinely
- Use a maintenance dose of biocide where the boat sits for long periods
- Check the deck fill seal and the tank vent
- Buy fuel from high-turnover suppliers

## What it costs

Treatment alone is inexpensive. Treatment plus polishing or tank cleaning — which
is what an established case needs — is not.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will a biocide alone fix it?**
It kills the organisms but leaves the dead biomass and the water. Both need
removing or the problem returns — and the dead material itself blocks filters.

**Why did my filters get worse right after treatment?**
Because the treatment killed a large colony and released it into the fuel as
sludge. This is expected, and it is why polishing and filter changes follow
treatment.

**Can I prevent it entirely?**
Keep water out of the tank and it cannot establish. Full tanks over layup, a
sound deck fill and vent, and routine separator draining do most of the work.

**Is diesel bug worse in Australia?**
Warm humid conditions favour it, so northern Australia is a higher-risk
environment than the south.

**How do I know it's the bug and not just dirty fuel?**
Sample the tank bottom and use a test kit. Slime and jelly indicate biological
growth; dry particulate suggests sediment or rust instead.
