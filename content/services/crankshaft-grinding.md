# Crankshaft Grinding in Australia

The crankshaft runs on plain bearings lubricated by pressurised oil. When that
oil film fails — low pressure, contamination, or extended intervals — the
bearings and the journals they run on wear or seize. Grinding restores the
journals to a true, correctly sized surface so undersize bearings can be fitted.

## When it is needed

- A spun bearing
- Scored or ovalled journals after oil starvation or contamination
- Journals worn beyond specification on a high-kilometre engine
- Crankshaft flex or distortion after a severe failure

## The work

The crank is crack-tested, measured for straightness and journal size, ground to
a defined undersize, and the fillet radii are maintained — that last point
matters more than it sounds. The fillet radius where the journal meets the web
is where fatigue cracks start; a grinder that squares it off has weakened the
crank regardless of how good the journal finish looks.

Journals are then polished to the required surface finish, and the crank is
balanced where required. Matching undersize bearings are fitted.

Also checked: oil gallery cleanliness (grinding debris in a gallery destroys the
new bearings), and thrust surfaces.

## When to replace instead

- Cracks in the journals, webs or fillets
- Already ground to maximum undersize
- Bent beyond straightening limits
- An engine where reconditioned exchange cranks are cheap and available

For many common Australian diesel engines an exchange crankshaft costs little
more than grinding, with much shorter downtime. Ask for both prices.

## Diagnose the cause first

A crankshaft does not wear for no reason. Oil starvation, contaminated oil,
extended intervals, a failing oil pump, or a blocked oil cooler will do the same
thing to the new bearings. Fixing the crank without fixing the oil system is a
short-lived repair.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many times can a crankshaft be ground?**
Until it reaches the maximum undersize the manufacturer specifies bearings for —
commonly a few regrinds, depending on the engine.

**Is a reground crankshaft as strong as new?**
Correctly ground with the fillet radii maintained, yes for normal service.
Incorrect fillet grinding is what weakens a crank.

**Should I grind or replace?**
Compare against an exchange crank. On common engines the price gap is often
small and replacement is faster.

**What caused my bearing failure?**
Nearly always an oil supply problem: low pressure, contamination, extended
intervals, or a blocked cooler. It must be found, or the new bearings go the
same way.
