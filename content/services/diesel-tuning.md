# Diesel Tuning and ECU Remapping in Australia

Diesel tuning modifies the engine control unit's fuelling, boost and timing
calibration. Done well, on a healthy engine, with the supporting hardware and
within legal limits, it can improve driveability and towing performance. Done
badly it damages engines, and done without regard for compliance it creates
legal, warranty and insurance problems.

Read the compliance section before the performance section. It is the part that
costs people money.

## Compliance, warranty and insurance

**ADR compliance.** Vehicles must comply with the Australian Design Rules,
including emissions standards. A tune that alters emissions performance can put
the vehicle outside its compliance, and any tune that defeats emissions control
equipment is not legal on a road-registered vehicle.
→ [ADR compliance for engine modifications](/guides/adr-compliance-engine-modifications/) ·
[Diesel emissions rules in Australia](/guides/diesel-emissions-rules-australia/)

**Warranty.** Manufacturers can decline claims for damage attributable to a
modification. Australian Consumer Law protects you against a blanket "any
modification voids everything" position — the manufacturer must show the
modification caused the failure — but a tuned engine with a failed turbo or
melted pistons is a difficult argument to win.
→ [Repair warranty rights](/guides/diesel-repair-warranty-rights-australia/)

**Insurance.** Undisclosed modifications can give an insurer grounds to reduce
or decline a claim. Disclose it. If your insurer will not accept it, that is
information you need before the tune, not after an accident.

**State modification rules.** Each state has its own framework for what requires
approval. Check yours.

## What tuning actually changes

**Fuelling.** More fuel per injection event. The limit is air: fuel beyond what
the available oxygen can burn produces smoke, heat and soot rather than power —
which is why a "more power" tune that produces black smoke is a badly done tune
loading your DPF.

**Boost.** More air. Constrained by the turbo's efficiency range and by the
engine's cylinder pressure limits.

**Timing.** Injection timing, which affects both efficiency and peak cylinder
pressure.

**Torque limiters.** Factory limits that often exist to protect the
transmission and driveline, not the engine. Raising them moves the failure
somewhere else.

## What a responsible tuner does

- Checks engine health first — compression, boost leaks, injector condition, DPF
  and EGR state. Tuning a worn engine accelerates its failure.
- Monitors exhaust gas temperature and sets limits accordingly. EGT is the
  number that determines whether a tune is safe, and it is the one budget tunes
  ignore.
- Considers the transmission and driveline, not just the engine.
- Tunes on a dynamometer under load, not by loading a generic file.
  → [Dyno tuning](/services/dyno-tuning/)
- Discusses compliance honestly, including what they will not do.
- Provides a stock file so the vehicle can be returned to standard.

A tuner who does not ask what you use the vehicle for, does not measure EGT, and
supplies a generic file for your engine model is selling you risk.

## Towing tunes are a different job

A towing calibration prioritises mid-range torque, EGT headroom and drivability
under sustained load — not peak power. If you tow, say so. A peak-power tune is
close to the worst thing you can do to a vehicle that spends its life pulling a
caravan up ranges.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is diesel tuning legal in Australia?**
Tuning within compliance limits is done legally, but any tune that alters
emissions performance can put a vehicle outside its ADR compliance, and defeating
emissions equipment is not legal on a road-registered vehicle. Check your state's
requirements and get it in writing from the tuner.

**Will a tune void my warranty?**
It does not automatically void everything, but a manufacturer can decline a claim
for damage the modification caused — and that is a hard argument to win on an
engine or driveline failure.

**Do I need to tell my insurer?**
Yes. An undisclosed modification can give an insurer grounds to reduce or decline
a claim.

**Will tuning improve fuel economy?**
Sometimes modestly, at a given driving style. Most of the benefit is usually spent
on the extra performance rather than saved.

**Is a plug-in tuning box the same as a remap?**
No. Boxes intercept and alter signals rather than recalibrating the ECU, and they
generally have less ability to respect safety limits like EGT.

**Can a tune damage my engine?**
Yes — through excessive exhaust gas temperature, cylinder pressure beyond design
limits, or driveline loads above what the transmission was built for. This is
what a competent tuner is managing.
