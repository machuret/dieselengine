# Marine Engine Servicing in Australia

Marine engines are serviced by hours and by calendar, whichever comes first —
and for most Australian boats the calendar arrives long before the hours. A
recreational boat doing 40 hours a year still needs annual servicing, and for
reasons that have nothing to do with wear.

→ [Marine engine service schedule by hours](/guides/marine-engine-service-schedule/)

## What a proper marine service covers

**Engine oil and filter,** to marine specification. Marine engines run at
sustained load and their oil works harder than a car's.

**Fuel filters and water separator.** Primary and secondary, plus draining and
inspecting the bowl — which tells you the state of your tank.
→ [Fuel filter and water separator service](/services/marine-fuel-filter-service/)

**Raw water impeller,** inspected or replaced, with the strainer cleaned and the
seacock exercised.
→ [Impeller replacement](/services/impeller-replacement/)

**Anodes.** Engine internal anodes as well as visible ones — the heat exchanger
pencil anode especially.
→ [Anode inspection and replacement](/services/anode-replacement/)

**Coolant condition,** tested rather than eyeballed. Marine coolant's job here is
corrosion protection, and inhibitors deplete on a schedule.

**Gearbox oil,** which is a separate oil on a separate interval and is routinely
forgotten.
→ [Marine gearbox service](/services/marine-gearbox-service/)

**Belts, hoses and mounts,** replaced on age rather than appearance.

**Exhaust elbow inspection** at its due interval — the highest-value check on the
engine.
→ [Exhaust elbow replacement](/services/exhaust-elbow-replacement/)

**Batteries and charging,** tested under load.

**A run-up,** confirming temperature, pressure, exhaust water flow and that the
engine reaches rated RPM.

## Why low hours does not mean low maintenance

The things that damage a lightly-used marine engine are time-based, not
hour-based:

- Fuel degrades and grows microbial contamination in the tank
- Water accumulates through condensation in part-full tanks
- The impeller takes a set against the cam and loses its pumping ability
- Coolant inhibitors deplete regardless of running
- Anodes waste continuously while the boat is in the water
- Rubber hoses and belts perish with age
- Batteries sulphate
- Internal corrosion continues in an engine that never gets warm

A 200-hour engine that has sat for five years is frequently in worse condition
than a 2,000-hour engine used weekly.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should a marine diesel be serviced?**
By hours or annually, whichever comes first. For most recreational boats the
annual interval arrives well before the hours.

**My boat did 20 hours this year — can I skip a service?**
No. The damage in a lightly used marine engine is time-based: fuel degradation,
impeller set, coolant inhibitor depletion, anode consumption and perishing
rubber.

**Is marine oil different from automotive oil?**
Marine engines run at sustained high load, and the correct specification matters.
Use what the engine maker specifies rather than the nearest automotive
equivalent.

**Does the gearbox get serviced at the same time?**
It should, but it has its own oil and its own interval and is frequently
overlooked. Ask explicitly.

**What is the most commonly skipped service item?**
The engine's internal anodes and the exhaust elbow inspection. Both are cheap and
both prevent expensive failures.
