# Diesel Engine Reconditioning in Australia

"Reconditioned" usually means an exchange engine: a remanufactured unit supplied
off the shelf, with your old engine returned as the core. It is the main
alternative to rebuilding your own engine, and its key advantage is time.

## Reconditioning versus rebuilding

| | Rebuild | Reconditioned exchange |
|---|---|---|
| Whose engine | Yours | A remanufactured unit |
| Downtime | Weeks | Days |
| History | Known | Documented by the remanufacturer |
| Availability | Any engine | Only common engines |
| Numbers matching | Yes | No |

For a common Australian engine — a 1KD-FTV, a 4JJ1, a P5AT — exchange units are
readily available and downtime is short. For an uncommon engine there may be no
exchange option at all, and a rebuild is the only path.

→ [Diesel engine rebuild](/services/diesel-engine-rebuild/)

## What "reconditioned" should actually mean

The word is not regulated, and the range of what gets sold under it is enormous.
A properly remanufactured engine has been:

- Completely dismantled and cleaned
- Crack-tested (block and head)
- Machined as required — bored, honed or re-linered, crank ground, decks and
  head surfaces trued
- Fitted with new pistons, rings, bearings, gaskets, seals, oil pump and valve
  train components
- Assembled to measured specification
- Tested, ideally on a dynamometer, with documented results

Ask for that specification in writing. An engine described as reconditioned but
supplied with no machining schedule, no crack test and no test results may be a
cleaned and resealed used engine.

## Questions to ask the supplier

- What was machined, and to what specification?
- What components are new versus reused?
- Was it crack-tested? Was it run-tested?
- What is the warranty term, and does it cover labour as well as parts?
- What voids the warranty? (Usually: not fitting a new oil cooler, not
  addressing the original failure cause, not following run-in.)
- What is the core charge and what condition must the core be in?

## The ancillaries decide whether it survives

An exchange engine typically arrives as a long or short motor. Everything you
bolt to it comes from the failed engine, and that is where reconditioned engines
die early.

Non-negotiable at fitting: a cleaned or replaced oil cooler, a flushed cooling
system, a new radiator if the original overheated, tested injectors, an assessed
turbo, and a clean intercooler. If the original engine failed from dust, the
entire intake tract needs inspection.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a reconditioned engine as good as a rebuild?**
A properly remanufactured engine is comparable, and it comes with a warranty and
far less downtime. The variable is the remanufacturer's standard — which is why
the written specification matters.

**How long does fitting an exchange engine take?**
Commonly a few days to a week, against weeks for a rebuild.

**What is a core charge?**
A deposit against the return of your old engine, refunded when it is returned in
acceptable condition. Ask what "acceptable" means — a block cracked by the
failure may not qualify.

**Is a second-hand import engine a cheaper option?**
Cheaper up front, with unknown history and usually little warranty. It can work
out, and it can be an expensive mistake.
→ [Reconditioned vs second-hand engine](/guides/recon-vs-secondhand-engine/)

**Do I need a new turbo and injectors as well?**
They need assessing, not automatically replacing. Fitting a reconditioned engine
behind worn injectors or a failing turbo is how a warranty claim gets declined.
