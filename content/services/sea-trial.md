# Sea Trial and Engine Performance Testing in Australia

A sea trial puts the engine under the load it was built for. Faults that hide
completely at the dock — overheating, fuel starvation, gearbox slip, an
overloaded engine — appear within minutes under sustained load.

## The single most important measurement

**Does the engine reach its rated wide open throttle RPM?**

Every marine engine has a rated maximum RPM. Reaching it at full throttle, with a
normal load, means the engine and propeller are correctly matched. This one
number tells you more than any other single measurement on a boat.

**Under rated RPM** means the engine is overloaded. The propeller may be
over-pitched, the bottom may be fouled, or the engine may not be making its
power. A chronically overloaded diesel runs hot, smokes and wears out early —
this is one of the most common and most damaging conditions in the Australian
recreational fleet, and most owners have no idea their boat has it.

**Over rated RPM** means under-propped: the engine cannot deliver its designed
power and the boat is slower than it should be.

→ [Won't reach wide open throttle RPM](/marine-engine-problems/wont-reach-wot-rpm/)

## What else to check under way

- **Temperature** at sustained cruise, not just at idle. Cooling faults reveal
  themselves under load.
- **Exhaust smoke** at idle, on acceleration and at cruise
- **Oil pressure** hot at idle and at cruise
- **Gearbox** engagement ahead and astern, and gearbox temperature after a
  sustained run
- **Vibration** through the rev range, which points at shaft, coupling,
  alignment or propeller
- **Fuel delivery** under sustained load — filter and tank problems show up here
  and nowhere else
- **Charging voltage** under load
- **Steering, controls and alarms**
- **Exhaust water flow** throughout

Run at sustained cruise for long enough to reach true thermal equilibrium. Ten
minutes is not enough; problems commonly appear after twenty or thirty.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the most important thing to measure on a sea trial?**
Whether the engine reaches its rated wide open throttle RPM. It tells you whether
engine, propeller and hull are correctly matched.

**My boat won't reach rated RPM — what does that mean?**
The engine is overloaded. Check the bottom first, then the propeller pitch, then
the engine. Running a diesel chronically overloaded shortens its life
substantially.

**How long should a sea trial run?**
Long enough to reach thermal equilibrium under sustained cruise — commonly
twenty to thirty minutes, not ten.

**Do I need a sea trial after antifouling?**
It is worth checking RPM afterwards. A clean bottom can change the picture
significantly, and it is the correct baseline for propeller decisions.
